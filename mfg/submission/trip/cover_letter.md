*Transportation Research Interdisciplinary Perspectives*

**Submission: "Many small carriers: the mean-field limit of competitive urban delivery under zone-time charges"**

Dear Editor,

I submit the above manuscript as a research article for Transportation Research Interdisciplinary Perspectives. It asks a question a city faces when it charges parcel-delivery traffic by zone and hour: which charge is safe when the delivery market consists of many small carriers rather than a few large ones, and what changes when carriers are few or can enter freely.

The main findings, computed on nine cities, are these:

- **Many small carriers.** A simple charge set from observed zone-hour totals approaches the marginal-cost charge, and its welfare loss shrinks with the largest carrier's share for every instance; the gap to the competitive limit falls like N^-0.88 between 24 and 1536 carriers.
- **Few carriers (a negative result).** The same charge lowers welfare, and the efficient charge is a subsidy that lump-sum fees can fund only with carrier-specific terms.
- **Free entry.** The subsidy attracts entrants and lowers welfare net of fixed costs; entry control is the largest welfare effect we computed.
- **Through traffic.** The delay that delivery vehicles impose on through traffic is about eight times the in-zone congestion that the base model prices; this rests on a weak value-of-time input, which the manuscript says plainly.
- **Who gains and who loses**, with exposure per resident, and a section of implications for planners and operators.
- **Theory.** The worst-case convergence rate of the finite market is the square root of the largest carrier share and is attained when plans couple zones, so no instance-free 1/N bound exists for planners to rely on.

I believe the paper fits the scope of the journal because it is policy- and practice-oriented, it reports negative and uncomfortable results (the plain charge lowers welfare with few carriers; the efficient charge cannot be funded by lump-sum fees; entry can erase the gain), and it applies a method from mean-field games and aggregative games that is new to urban freight. The paper is also frank about its limits: the hourly profile is stylised, travel times are deterministic in the experiments, the congestion calibration is weak and several parameters are of grade C.

An earlier version of this work was assessed at initial review by another Transportation Research journal and was not advanced to full review because the editors judged its fit and significance for that journal insufficiently established; it has not been reviewed in full elsewhere. The present version has been reframed around the transport and policy questions. The manuscript builds on a companion paper by the author, cited as Dang (2026a) and under review. The manuscript is 38 pages (A4, single column, 11 pt) with 53 references. Code, instances and results are in the repository named in the data availability statement and in a Zenodo deposit (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24357664, reserved; the record will be published with the final archive). The work has not been published elsewhere and is not under consideration by another journal.
