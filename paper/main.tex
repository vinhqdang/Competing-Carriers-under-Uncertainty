\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Several delivery firms serve the same neighbourhoods, sell to overlapping customers and slow one another down on the same streets. City authorities respond with instruments that act on all of them: congestion and low-emission zones, kerbside rules, and charges or subsidies per delivery \citep{Quak2009,HolguinVeras2016,Dey2019,XuSun2024}. Choosing such an instrument means predicting how the carriers react, and the reaction is itself the outcome of routing decisions that are hard to compute.

We treat the city as the leader of a bilevel problem. Three difficulties are stacked.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*),leftmargin=*]
\item \emph{Each follower solves an NP-hard routing problem under imprecise probabilities.} A carrier has a few weeks of travel-time observations. These support bounds on the distribution function of each arc time (a probability box) and say nothing about the dependence between arcs. The robust cost of a route is its worst-case expected loss over all joint laws compatible with the boxes, and the carrier's best response is a vehicle routing problem with this cost.
\item \emph{The followers play a congestion game.} The price a carrier obtains falls with the total deliveries in a zone (Cournot competition) and travel times rise with vehicle density. If congestion depends on vehicle-hours (exposure), the game has an exact potential and pure equilibria exist. If it depends on vehicle counts, pure equilibria may fail to exist and deciding whether one exists is strongly NP-complete (Section~\ref{sec:theory}).
\item \emph{The leader's problem is a bilevel program whose lower level is the game above.} The usual approach evaluates a grid of charges and computes one equilibrium per grid point. With 63 grid points this took \GridCallsFullMin--\GridCallsFullMax{} routing-oracle calls per city and seed in our experiments, each call an NP-hard problem.
\end{enumerate}
This paper gives algorithms for each layer and tests them on nine metropolitan areas. We state at once what is new and what is not.

\paragraph{Contributions, in order, with their novelty level}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=*]
\item \emph{The problem and the model} (Section~\ref{sec:model}): a bilevel model with a public leader above carriers who choose routing plans, interact through zone aggregates of deliveries and vehicle-hours, and plan with probability-box travel times. The ingredients are standard; the combination, and the two congestion regimes (exposure and vehicle count) with their different existence properties, are what we set up.
\item \emph{Budget-resource labelling (BRL)} (Section~\ref{sec:brl}): exact pricing of routes under probability boxes with unknown dependence, and, with branch-and-price, exact integer plans for the carriers. The reduction to a comonotone worst case and the stop-loss decomposition are classical \citep{Meilijson1979}, and a comonotone reduction for ambiguous demand is known in routing \citep{GhosalHoWiesemann2024}. The new element is to use the per-arc budget of the decomposition as a \emph{decision resource} of the pricing problem, one per kink of the loss function, together with a dominance rule restricted to a computable completion window and its exactness proof. Branch-and-price itself is standard machinery.
\item \emph{Leader pricing without equilibrium search} (Sections~\ref{sec:dip} and~\ref{sec:arp}). \emph{Dual implementation pricing (DIP)} computes a socially good target profile and then finds charges under which it is an equilibrium, by cutting planes whose separation step is one best response per carrier. \emph{Aggregate-response pricing (ARP)} lets the leader see only the zone aggregates of the market after each posted charge, and a marginal tariff variant needs no iteration. Both rest on classical ideas: the Pigou--Cournot tax, toll polyhedra, inverse optimisation, trial-and-error tolls and marginal-contribution tariffs \citep{Buchanan1969,Bergendorff1997,Ahuja2001,Yang2004,Wolpert2001,Sandholm2002,Maheshwari2022}. What is new is limited to the following: the potential--welfare identity of the lumpy Cournot--congestion game with loss bounds in terms of lot asymmetry; the use of the carriers' own NP-hard best responses as the separation step; a contraction rate $\varrho=\max_z\kappa_z/(b_z+\kappa_z)$ for the observation-only iteration that does not depend on the number of zones, carriers, lots, routing costs or probability boxes; and the shift of the implementing charges by a $\Gamma$-maximin surcharge.
\item \emph{Theory} (Section~\ref{sec:theory}): a credal absorption theorem, which gives a belief width bounded independently of the number of carriers above which a mixed equilibrium rounds to an exact pure imprecise equilibrium, and shows that the bound is attained (closely related to \citealp{Liu2023}, who obtain an approximate equilibrium of the precise game); the split between exposure-based congestion (pure equilibria always exist) and vehicle-count congestion (strongly NP-complete existence), which is incremental to \citet{Apt2022}; and $\Gamma$-maximin beliefs as a surcharge game. The discrete core of these results is checked in Lean~4 without unproved steps; Section~\ref{sec:lean} says what that does and does not cover.
\item \emph{Evidence} (Sections~\ref{sec:data} and~\ref{sec:results}): nine metropolitan areas built from OpenStreetMap, gridded population (GHSL, epoch 2025) and web-sourced operating parameters with quality grades; three random draws (seeds) of each city slice; an exact integer oracle; and results on algorithm performance, equilibria, charges, the price of anarchy and imprecision, reported with their spread over seeds. The instance pipeline, with its parameters, is released as a benchmark generator for multi-carrier routing games.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{What is not new, and what we do not claim} The comonotone upper prevision of a route, the stop-loss decomposition, label-setting for elementary shortest paths, branch-and-price and ng-routes are classical. The approximate pure equilibrium of nonconvex aggregative games with an error that does not grow with the number of players is due to \citet{Liu2023}. The hardness of pure-equilibrium existence by a kernel reduction is of the kind in \citet{Apt2022}. The tangent charge, the marginal-contribution tariff and trial-and-error pricing are classical; the closest neighbour of ARP in the adaptive-pricing literature is \citet{Maheshwari2022,Maheshwari2026}, which treats atomic Cournot games with continuous strategies. We do not claim a forecast for any city: the instances are a stylised slice of each city with proxy demand and several weak parameters, and the price-of-anarchy values are empirical, computed from the equilibria that our methods found. We do not claim optimal charges (the leader's optimum is defined with optimistic equilibrium selection), a guarantee about which equilibrium the carriers select, or any guarantee for the pure game under ARP unless its fixed point is reached. We do not claim results for vehicle-count congestion at city scale. Zone-market clearing (ZMC), an earlier method of ours for equilibrium computation, did not outperform plain best response on the city games; Remark~\ref{rem:zmc} and \ref{app:zmc} report this.

\paragraph{Organisation} Section~\ref{sec:related} places the work in the literature, Section~\ref{sec:model} defines the model, Section~\ref{sec:algo} gives BRL, the equilibrium engine, DIP and ARP, Section~\ref{sec:theory} the equilibrium theory, Section~\ref{sec:data} the data, and Section~\ref{sec:results} the computational study. Sections~\ref{sec:implications} and~\ref{sec:limits} give implications and limits. Proofs are in the appendices.

\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Competing carriers and leaders above carriers} Carriers competing in sequential auctions for requests were studied by simulation by \citet{Figliozzi2004,Figliozzi2007}. Stylised games treat shared collection points and parcel lockers \citep{Li2024,Mercurio2026}, and ride-hailing and crowdsourcing models give equilibrium evidence on competition for workers and customers, with queues instead of routes \citep{Taylor2018,Bernstein2021,Benjaafar2022,Alnaggar2021,Mancini2022}. \citet{Cerulli2024} place a platform above carriers who solve profitable tour problems, but the carriers do not interact. \citet{Catalano2014} cast terminal design as a Stackelberg game. Curb management supplies pricing leaders \citep{XuSun2024,LiuMaQian2023}, and policy work documents the restrictions cities use \citep{Quak2009,HolguinVeras2016,Dey2019}. Collaborative routing \citep{GanstererHartl2018,Cleophas2019,Los2025} gives savings that bound what cooperation could recover, not a price of anarchy.

\paragraph{Routing under uncertainty} Robust and distributionally robust routing keeps the combinatorial structure when the ambiguity set is suitable \citep{Gounaris2013,GhosalWiesemann2020,Zhang2021,Jaillet2016,Adulyasak2016}. Closest to BRL, \citet{GhosalHoWiesemann2024} show that a capacitated routing problem with ambiguous demand marginals and unknown dependence reduces, through a comonotone worst case, to a scenario problem solved by branch-and-cut. The comonotone worst case and the stop-loss decomposition of comonotone sums are due to \citet{Meilijson1979} \citep[see also][]{Kaas2000,Dhaene2002}, and worst-case marginals of a probability box under monotone losses are standard \citep{Williamson1990}. Branch-price-and-cut under mean-covariance travel times \citep{Rostami2021} and path dominance by second-order stochastic dominance for independent arc times \citep{Nie2012} address other dependence models. None of these adds competition among carriers.

\paragraph{Games, aggregation and ambiguity} Potential and congestion games guarantee pure equilibria \citep{Rosenthal1973,Monderer1996}, and selfish-routing theory bounds their inefficiency \citep{Roughgarden2002,Acemoglu2007,Cominetti2009}. A carrier chooses tours, so its strategy set is finite and nonconvex. \citet{Liu2023} prove, with a Shapley--Folkman argument in the tradition of \citet{Starr1969}, that nonconvex aggregative games have approximate pure equilibria whose error does not grow with the number of players. Approximate pure equilibria in large games go back to \citet{Kalai2004} and \citet{Azrieli2013}, and finding them can be PPAD-complete \citep{Goldberg2023}. Equilibrium under ambiguous beliefs has been studied as equilibrium in beliefs \citep{Lo1996,Marinacci2000}, as pure equilibrium in beliefs with ambiguous payoffs \citep{Beauchene2016}, and as robust-optimisation equilibrium \citep{Aghassi2006,Crespi2025}; \citet{Crespi2025} show that robust equilibria are approximate equilibria of the nominal game, the converse direction of our absorption result. \citet{Feik2026} study convex aggregative games with an optimal-transport ambiguity ball. Deciding pure-equilibrium existence is NP-complete in polymatrix and integer programming games \citep{Apt2022,Carvalho2022}.

\paragraph{Tolls, implementation and adaptive pricing} Corrective taxes for market power and externalities go back to \citet{Buchanan1969} and \citet{Barnett1980}. Toll polyhedra that implement a target flow are classical for nonatomic routing \citep{Bergendorff1997,Hearn1998}, cost-balancing tolls exist for atomic games \citep{Fotakis2008}, and \citet{Bhaskar2019} compute implementing tolls from an equilibrium oracle. The cutting-plane loop of DIP is an inverse-optimisation cutting plane \citep{Ahuja2001,Wang2009,Bodur2022}, and best-response cuts are used for equilibria of integer programming games \citep{Dragotto2023,Duguet2025}. Marginal-contribution tariffs make welfare the potential of the game \citep{Wolpert2001,Marden2013}. Trial-and-error implementation of marginal-cost pricing from observed flows is due to \citet{Yang2004}, evolutionary implementation of pricing to \citet{Sandholm2002}, and adaptive incentive design for atomic Cournot games with continuous strategies to \citet{Maheshwari2022,Maheshwari2026}. The closest leader-side method for combinatorial games updates tolls by zeroth-order steps around an equilibrium solver, including freight carriers with hard tour problems \citep{Masiha2026}; like a grid, it needs an equilibrium solve per outer step. Our novelty relative to these is stated in Sections~\ref{sec:dip} and~\ref{sec:arp}. Our searches were web and index searches, with snippet-level reading for several neighbours; a Scopus and Web of Science search has not been done.

\section{Model}\label{sec:model}

Notation: $[n]=\{1,\dots,n\}$; for $v\in\R^d$ and $w\in\R^d_{>0}$, $\|v\|_{w,\infty}=\max_k|v_k|/w_k$ and $\|v\|_M^2=\ip{v}{Mv}$.

\subsection{Carriers, plans and congestion}

The city is divided into zones $z\in[Z]$. There are $N$ carriers. Carrier $i$ has a finite plan set $\cP_i$; a plan is a set of depot-to-depot routes over the carrier's customer zones. A plan $p$ determines deliveries $q_{iz}(p)$, vehicles $n_{iz}(p)$ and vehicle-hours $\tau_{iz}(p)$ in each zone, and a routing cost $C_i(p)$ (Section~\ref{sec:routing}). Write $Q_z=\sum_iq_{iz}$ and $T_z=\sum_i\tau_{iz}$. Inverse demand in zone $z$ is $\alpha_z-b_zQ_z$ with $b_z>0$, and a vehicle-hour costs $\gamma(\xi_z+c_zX_z)$, where $\gamma>0$ is the hourly cost, $\xi_z$ the free-flow travel-time factor with mean $\mu_z$, $c_z>0$ the congestion slope and $X_z$ the congestion variable. The city charges $s_z$ per delivery and $\theta_z$ per unit of the congestion variable. Carrier $i$'s profit is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:profit}
u_i=\sum_z\big[q_{iz}(\alpha_z-b_zQ_z)-\gamma\tau_{iz}(\xi_z+c_zX_z)-s_zq_{iz}-\theta_zx_{iz}\big]-C_i(p).
\end{equation}
Two congestion regimes are distinguished.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{D1 (exposure-based):} $x_{iz}=\tau_{iz}$ and $X_z=\sum_i\tau_{iz}$.
\item \textbf{D2 (vehicle-count):} $x_{iz}=n_{iz}$ and $X_z=\sum_in_{iz}$.
\end{itemize}
Under D1 the carriers' interaction depends on the same quantity that determines the carrier's own congestion cost. Under D2 the externality depends on a different quantity (counts) than the carrier's own exposure (hours), which breaks the potential structure (Section~\ref{sec:theory}).

For the theory it is convenient to write the interaction abstractly. Let $d=2Z$ with coordinates $(z,Q)$ and $(z,X)$.

\begin{assumption}[Finite plans]\label{ass:plans}
Plan $p\in\cP_i$ has a contribution $a_i(p)\in\R^d_{\ge0}$ to the aggregate, an exposure $\varphi_i(p)\in\R^d_{\ge0}$ to the aggregate, a traffic exposure $t_i(p)\in\R^Z_{\ge0}$ and a private profit $\pi_i(p)$. At a pure profile $\sigma$, $A(\sigma)=\sum_ia_i(\sigma_i)$ and $A_{-i}=A-a_i(\sigma_i)$.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Affine coupling]\label{ass:affine}
There are a diagonal $M\ge0$ and $\gamma>0$ such that, for a rival aggregate $m\in\R^d_{\ge0}$ and traffic factor $\xi\in\R^Z_{\ge0}$, carrier $i$ obtains $u_i(p;m,\xi)=\pi_i(p)-\ip{\varphi_i(p)}{Mm}-\gamma\ip{t_i(p)}{\xi}$.
\end{assumption}

Equation~\eqref{eq:profit} has this form with $a_{i,(z,Q)}=\varphi_{i,(z,Q)}=q_{iz}$, $M_{(z,Q)}=b_z$, $a_{i,(z,X)}=x_{iz}$, $\varphi_{i,(z,X)}=\tau_{iz}$, $M_{(z,X)}=\gamma c_z$ and $t_{iz}=\tau_{iz}$. The game is \emph{of type D1} if $\varphi_i=a_i$ for all carriers and plans, and \emph{of type D2} otherwise. Social welfare, with charges treated as transfers, is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:welfare}
W(\sigma)=\sum_z\big[\alpha_zQ_z-\tfrac{b_z}{2}Q_z^2-\gamma\mu_zT_z-\gamma c_zT_zX_z\big]-\sum_iC_i(\sigma_i),
\end{equation}
that is, consumer surplus minus the congestion cost that carriers impose on each other, minus routing costs; in the experiments the traffic term is part of the routing cost and the social cost is $\mathrm{SC}=-W$. The affine coupling excludes power-law congestion such as BPR curves, which we linearise (Section~\ref{sec:data}).

\subsection{The routing layer}\label{sec:routing}

For every arc $a$ the carrier knows a probability box: distribution functions $\Fl_a\le\Fu_a$ on a bounded support $[0,B_a]$, for example a Dvoretzky--Kiefer--Wolfowitz band around the empirical distribution of $n$ observed days, $\Fl_a(x)=\max\{F_n(x)-\varepsilon,0\}$ with $\varepsilon=\sqrt{\ln(2/\alpha)/(2n)}$ \citep{DKW1956,Massart1990}. The dependence between arcs is unknown, so the joint credal set is the Fr\'echet class $\cM$ of all laws whose $a$-th marginal lies between $\Fl_a$ and $\Fu_a$. A route of duration $T_r=\sum_{a\in r}X_a$ incurs a convex piecewise-linear loss
\begin{equation}\label{eq:loss}
\phi(T)=c_0+\gamma T+\textstyle\sum_{j=1}^{H}\rho_j\pos{T-\Theta_j},\qquad \gamma\ge0,\ \rho_j>0,\ 0\le\Theta_1<\dots<\Theta_H.
\end{equation}
In the experiments $H=1$: a fixed cost, an hourly cost and an overtime penalty after the shift length. The robust route cost is the upper prevision $\Eu[\phi(T_r)]=\sup_{P\in\cM}\E_P\phi(T_r)$, and $C_i(p)=\sum_{r\in p}\Eu[\phi(T_r)]$. A carrier's best response to prices on its deliveries and hours is therefore a prize-collecting routing problem in which every route is priced by $\Eu[\phi]$. We call this problem the \emph{carrier oracle}:
\begin{assumption}[Oracle]\label{ass:oracle}
For each $i$ there is an oracle that, for price vectors $\lambda^a,\lambda^\varphi\in\R^d$, $\nu\in\R^Z$ and a diagonal $Q\succeq0$, returns a plan in $\arg\max_{p\in\cP_i}\{\pi_i(p)+\tfrac12\ip{a_i(p)}{Qa_i(p)}-\ip{\lambda^a}{a_i(p)}-\ip{\lambda^\varphi}{\varphi_i(p)}-\ip{\nu}{t_i(p)}\}$.
\end{assumption}
A best response at the belief $(m,\xi)$ is the call $(0,Mm,\gamma\xi,0)$. Section~\ref{sec:brl} gives an exact implementation of the oracle. We count \emph{oracle calls} as the cost unit of every method below.

\subsection{The leader's problem}

The city chooses charges $u$ from an admissible set $\mathcal U$. For a charge $u$ let $\mathrm{NE}(u)$ be the set of pure Nash equilibria of the carriers' game. With optimistic selection the leader's problem is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bilevel}
\min_{u\in\mathcal U,\ \sigma}\ \mathrm{SC}(\sigma)\qquad\text{s.t.}\qquad\sigma\in\mathrm{NE}(u).
\end{equation}
In the D1 instance used for computation (Section~\ref{sec:dip}), each carrier serves its customer zones in all-or-nothing lots, and the charges reach the game through one number per zone. The three methods compared in Section~\ref{sec:results} differ in what the leader may use: the \emph{grid} and DIP use the carriers' routing oracle, ARP uses only the zone aggregates the market produces after each posted charge.

\section{Algorithms}\label{sec:algo}

The algorithms form a stack. BRL (Section~\ref{sec:brl}) and branch-and-price (Section~\ref{sec:bp}) implement the carrier oracle exactly. An equilibrium engine (Section~\ref{sec:engine}) calls the oracle to compute a pure equilibrium for a given charge. DIP (Section~\ref{sec:dip}) and ARP (Section~\ref{sec:arp}) choose the charges; the grid baseline calls the engine once per grid point.

\subsection{Budget-resource labelling (BRL)}\label{sec:brl}

Let $\Qb_a(u)=\inf\{x:\Fl_a(x)\ge u\}$ be the upper quantile of arc $a$, $\xb_a=\int_0^1\Qb_a(u)\,du$ and $\Pib_a(d)=\int_0^1\pos{\Qb_a(u)-d}du$, the stop-loss transform of the lower distribution function. $\Pib_a$ is convex and nonincreasing with slopes in $[-1,0]$.

Let $\Qb_a(u)=\inf\{x:\Fl_a(x)\ge u\}$ be the upper quantile of arc $a$, $\xb_a=\int_0^1\Qb_a(u)\,du$ and $\Pib_a(d)=\int_0^1\pos{\Qb_a(u)-d}du$, the stop-loss transform of the lower CDF; $\Pib_a$ is convex and nonincreasing with slopes in $[-1,0]$.

\begin{theorem}[Upper prevision and budget decomposition]\label{thm:brl-main}
For every route $r$,
\[
\Eu[\phi(T_r)]=\int_0^1\phi\Big(\sum_{a\in r}\Qb_a(u)\Big)du
=c_0+\sum_{a\in r}\gamma\xb_a+\sum_{j=1}^{H}\rho_j\min\Big\{\sum_{a\in r}\Pib_a(d^j_a):\ \sum_{a\in r}d^j_a\le\Theta_j\Big\}.
\]
The supremum is attained by the comonotone coupling of the lower CDFs; each minimum is attained, at an integer vector when quantiles and thresholds are integers.
\end{theorem}

The first equality and the stop-loss identity for comonotone sums with fixed marginals are due to \citet{Meilijson1979} (see also \citealp{Kaas2000,Dhaene2002}), and the choice of the lower CDF as worst marginal is standard \citep{Williamson1990}. We re-prove the statement in \ref{app:brl} because the algorithm relies on the inequality-budget form and on integrality. With a common quantile grid of $L$ levels (weights $\omega_l$), the route profile $S_{r,l}=\sum_{a\in r}q_{a,l}$ gives $\Eu[\phi(T_r)]=c_0+f_r(T_{\max})$ for $H=1$, where $f_r(D)=\gamma\sum_l\omega_lS_{r,l}+\rho\sum_l\omega_l\pos{S_{r,l}-D}$.

\begin{theorem}[Budget-resource reformulation]\label{thm:resource}
Let $y$ be dual prices and define the arc costs \[c_a(d^1,\dots,d^H)=\gamma\xb_a+\sum_j\rho_j\Pib_a(d^j).\] The dependence-robust pricing problem \[\min_{r\in\cR}\Big\{\Eu[\phi(T_r)]-\sum_{i\in r}y_i\Big\}\] equals an elementary shortest path problem in which every arc chooses a consumption $d^j_a$ of each of $H$ budget resources, paid through the separable convex cost $c_a$ and subject to the budget constraints $\sum_{a\in r}d^j_a\le\Theta_j$, $j\le H$. The number of resources is $H$; it does not depend on $L$, the sample size or the number of arcs.
\end{theorem}

A scenario view of the same problem, which is what the comonotone reduction of \citet{GhosalHoWiesemann2024} gives, needs the $L$ level sums $(S_{p,l})_l$ as resources and compares labels pointwise. BRL instead compares labels through their cost functions, but only where a completion can still reach. For a partial path (label) $p$ ending at $v$ with level profile $S_p$, collected duals $y(p)$, visited set $\mathcal V(p)$ and load $\ell(p)$, concatenation with a completion $q$ is an inf-convolution, $f_{p\oplus q}(T_{\max})=\min_D\{f_p(D)+f_q(T_{\max}-D)\}$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:infconv}). Let $C_{\min}(v)$ be a lower bound on the duration of any completion from $v$ and define the completion window $\mathcal W(p)=[\Dl(p),\Dh(p)]$ with $\Dh(p)=\min\{S_{p,L},T_{\max}-C_{\min}(v)\}$ and $\Dl(p)=\min\{S_{p,1},\Dh(p)\}$.

\begin{definition}[Functional dominance]\label{def:fdom}
Label $p_1$ functionally dominates $p_2$, both ending at $v$, if $\mathcal V(p_1)\subseteq\mathcal V(p_2)$, $\ell(p_1)\le\ell(p_2)$ and $(f_{p_1}(D)-y(p_1))-(f_{p_2}(D)-y(p_2))\le0$ for all $D\in\mathcal W(p_2)$.
\end{definition}

The difference is piecewise linear with breakpoints at the level sums, so the test needs at most $2L+2$ evaluations.

\begin{theorem}[Validity and exactness]\label{thm:fdom}
Functional dominance implies that every completion of $p_2$ is a completion of $p_1$ with no larger reduced cost. The labelling algorithm that discards dominated labels (Algorithm~\ref{alg:brl}) returns the minimum reduced cost exactly, with a route attaining it.
\end{theorem}

The window is what makes the rule exact and strong at the same time. The slope of $f_p$ lies in $[-\rho,0]$ and that of $D\mapsto f_q(T_{\max}-D)$ in $[0,\rho]$, so an optimal split never lies above $\Dh(p)$, and below $S_{p,1}$ the difference of two label functions is nondecreasing (Lemma~\ref{lem:window}). Quantile-vector dominance and sector dominance are special cases of functional dominance, and neither converse holds (Proposition~\ref{prop:compare}). Under independent arc times concatenation is a convolution of laws rather than an inf-convolution and the method does not apply. The natural shortcut of fixing a single risk level for all routes gives a lower bound but is not exact (Proposition~\ref{prop:fixedu}).

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{BRL pricing for the dependence-robust routing problem ($H=1$)}\label{alg:brl}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require graph, demands, capacity; level weights $\omega$; arc level profiles $q_{a,\cdot}$; $\gamma,\rho,T_{\max},c_0$; duals $y$
\State $C_{\min}(v)\gets$ shortest path from $v$ to the depot with arc lengths $q_{a,1}$
\State buckets $\mathcal B_v\gets\emptyset$; queue $\gets\{$depot label$\}$
\While{queue nonempty}
  \State pop $p$; record the closed route $p\oplus(v,0)$ with reduced cost $c_0+f_{p\oplus(v,0)}(T_{\max})-y(p)$
  \For{each customer $j\notin\mathcal V(p)$ that fits the capacity}
    \State $p'\gets$ extension of $p$ to $j$ ($S_{p'}=S_p+q_{(v,j),\cdot}$, $y(p')=y(p)+y_j$)
    \If{some $o\in\mathcal B_j$ functionally dominates $p'$} discard $p'$
    \Else\ delete the labels of $\mathcal B_j$ dominated by $p'$; insert and push $p'$
    \EndIf
  \EndFor
\EndWhile
\State \Return recorded routes sorted by reduced cost
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

On the city instances the pricing runs over ng-routes \citep{Baldacci2011}: the visited set is replaced by the ng-memory, and Theorem~\ref{thm:fdom} holds verbatim, with the weaker ng-relaxation bound. Column generation \citep{Feillet2004,Irnich2005} with BRL pricing returns the exact LP bound of the dependence-robust routing problem when no negative reduced cost remains; an integer program over the generated columns (price-and-branch) gives a feasible plan but is not an exact integer method. Section~\ref{sec:bp} makes the integer step exact.

\paragraph{Prior art and the claim} The upper-prevision formula and the stop-loss decomposition are classical (Meilijson--N\'adas; Theorem~\ref{thm:brl-main} is re-proved in \ref{app:brl} because the algorithm uses its inequality-budget form and its integrality). Label-setting with resources is standard, and the comonotone reduction for ambiguous marginals is in \citet{GhosalHoWiesemann2024}. We did not find in the literature (i) the per-arc budget used as a \emph{decision resource} of the pricing problem (Theorem~\ref{thm:resource}) or (ii) the completion-window functional dominance with its exactness proof (Theorem~\ref{thm:fdom}). A referee may reasonably describe BRL as Meilijson--N\'adas plus resource-constrained labelling. Our answer is empirical and structural: the dominance rule is what keeps the label sets small (Section~\ref{sec:res-brl}), and with independent arc times the method does not apply.

\subsection{Branch-and-price with BRL pricing}\label{sec:bp}

Price-and-branch, an integer program over the columns of the root relaxation, can miss the integer optimum. We therefore embed the BRL pricer in branch-and-price. The master is a set-partitioning model for the routing benchmark, or a prize-collecting set-packing model for the carrier oracle, with one column pool shared by all nodes. Each node solves its LP by column generation with the exact BRL pricer, restricted to the node's allowed arcs with $C_{\min}$ recomputed on them; functional dominance remains valid because the restrictions are local.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item \emph{Branching} is on the aggregated arc flow $f_{ij}$: $f_{ij}=0$ deletes the arc; $f_{ij}=1$ deletes the other arcs leaving $i$ and entering $j$. Integral arc flows imply an integral plan.
\item \emph{Fleet size.} When every customer must be served, a row for the minimum number of routes is added at the root and a fractional route count is branched on first.
\item \emph{ng-routes} \citep{Baldacci2011}. The visited set is replaced by the ng-memory, and Theorem~\ref{thm:fdom} holds verbatim with the weaker relaxation. If a node solution contains a non-elementary column, the ng-sets along its cycle are enlarged; reported plans are elementary.
\item \emph{Bounds.} The node LP value and the Lagrangian bound during column generation are valid lower bounds, marked invalid if a pricing call hits the label limit. Upper bounds come from integral nodes and from an integer program over the generated elementary columns. Nodes are chosen by best bound; the method stops at a relative gap of $10^{-6}$.
\end{itemize}
Since ng-routes relax elementary routes, a zero gap proves optimality for the elementary problem. This is standard branch-and-price machinery; its purpose here is to remove the inexact integer step, not to add a branching rule. Column generation \citep{Feillet2004,Irnich2005} with exact pricing returns the exact LP bound, which also gives every integer plan a valid optimality gap.

\subsection{The equilibrium engine}\label{sec:engine}

For a given charge, a pure equilibrium of the D1 game is computed in two phases. In the first phase, zone-market clearing (ZMC) queries every carrier's oracle at a common zone-price vector for eight rounds, using the sign of the clearing residual to update the prices (\ref{app:zmc}). A restricted master over the collected plans and derandomised rounding give a start profile. In the second phase, sequential best responses (one oracle call each) run until no carrier improves; since D1 has an exact potential (Proposition~\ref{prop:D1}) this terminates at a pure Nash equilibrium. Each carrier's column pool is kept across calls, because route costs do not depend on prices. In the city experiments a carrier chooses node-disjoint routes from its depot over 22 customer zones; each served zone adds its whole lot (all-or-nothing) together with prize, own Cournot and congestion terms, charges and prices as node costs, and each route costs its BRL upper expectation.

\begin{remark}[ZMC and its negative result]\label{rem:zmc}
ZMC has a clearing identity that bounds the potential gap and each carrier's regret by the clearing error, and a price-bracketing rate that does not depend on $d$ (\ref{app:zmc}). On synthetic games with non-separable plan sets, it gave the smallest potential gap in \ZMCsynthWins{} of \ZMCsynthN{} instances at an equal call budget (Table~\ref{tab:zmc-synth}). On the nine city games it did not beat plain best response: it used \EfiveCallsZMCmin--\EfiveCallsZMCmax{} calls, against \EfiveCallsBRmin--\EfiveCallsBRmax{} for best response, and was strictly better than all other methods in \EfiveZMCstrictBestN{} cities (Section~\ref{sec:res-engine}). We use it as the starting phase of the engine and make no claim for it.
\end{remark}


\subsection{Dual implementation pricing (DIP)}\label{sec:dip}

A direct approach searches a grid of charges and solves one equilibrium per grid point; with 63 points this costs between \GridCallsFullMin{} and \GridCallsFullMax{} oracle calls per city and seed in the three cities where the exact grid is complete (Section~\ref{sec:res-dip}). DIP replaces the search by an implementation step. It computes the profile the city wants and then computes charges under which that profile is an equilibrium, using the carriers' own oracle to separate candidate charges. No equilibrium is solved for the leader's decision.

\paragraph{Setting} We use the D1 instance of Section~\ref{sec:model} in cost form. Carrier $i$ serves a subset of its customer zones with all-or-nothing lots $d_{iz}>0$, so $q_{iz}\in\{0,d_{iz}\}$ and $q_{iz}^2=d_{iz}q_{iz}$; vehicle-hours are $\tau_{iz}=r\,q_{iz}$ for one ratio $r>0$ (assumption (S3)); $R_i(p)\ge0$ is the robust routing cost of plan $p$; $g_z=\gamma c_z$. The city posts $s_z$ per delivery and $t_z$ per vehicle-hour. Carrier $i$'s cost and the social cost (charges are transfers) are
\[
J_i(p;\sigma_{-i})=R_i(p)+\sum_z\big[q_{iz}(-\alpha_z+b_z(q_{iz}+Q_{-i,z})+s_z)+\tau_{iz}(g_z(\tau_{iz}+X_{-i,z})+t_z)\big],
\]
\[
\mathrm{SC}(\sigma)=\sum_iR_i(\sigma_i)+\sum_z\big[-\alpha_zQ_z+\tfrac{b_z}{2}Q_z^2+g_zX_z^2\big].
\]
Write $\kappa_z=g_zr^2$ (congestion slope in delivery units), $m_z=b_z+\kappa_z$ (the slope the carriers face) and $\bar d_z$ for the mean lot in zone $z$.

\begin{proposition}[One instrument per zone]\label{prop:instr}
(a) For every plan, $\sum_z(s_zq_{iz}+t_z\tau_{iz})=\ip{u}{q_i}$ with $u_z=s_z+rt_z$, so the game depends on the charges only through $u\in\R^Z$. (b) Every $u$ is realised by a uniform per-delivery charge $s=\min_zu_z$ over zones with lots and non-negative zone congestion charges $t_z=(u_z-s)/r$. (c) The grid of Section~\ref{sec:res-leader} is the two-parameter family $u=s\mathbf 1+\vartheta\,r\,g\odot X^{\rm NE}$.
\end{proposition}

Under $u$ the game has the exact potential $\Phi_u(\sigma)=\sum_iR_i(\sigma_i)+\sum_z[-\alpha_zQ_z+\tfrac{m_z}{2}(Q_z^2+\sum_iq_{iz}^2)+u_zQ_z]$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:D1}).

\begin{lemma}[Potential--welfare identity]\label{lem:identity}
Fix $Q^\circ\in\R^Z$, let $u^{\tan}_z=\kappa_zQ^\circ_z-\tfrac{m_z}{2}\bar d_z$ and $v=u-u^{\tan}$. For every pure profile $\sigma$,
\[
\Phi_u(\sigma)=\mathrm{SC}(\sigma)-\sum_z\tfrac{\kappa_z}{2}(Q_z-Q^\circ_z)^2+L(\sigma)+\ip{v}{Q}+\sum_z\tfrac{\kappa_z}{2}(Q^\circ_z)^2,\qquad L(\sigma)=\sum_{i,z}\tfrac{m_z}{2}(d_{iz}-\bar d_z)q_{iz}.
\]
\end{lemma}

The \emph{tangent charge} $u^{\tan}$ is the classical corrective tax of a Cournot market with an externality: the marginal external damage at the target aggregate minus a market-power subsidy \citep{Buchanan1969,Barnett1980}. Two details are specific to all-or-nothing lots: the subsidy is half a lot, $\tfrac{m_z}{2}\bar d_z$ per delivery, and it includes the carriers' own congestion. With carrier-specific charges $U_{iz}=\kappa_zQ^*_{-i,z}-\tfrac{b_z}{2}d_{iz}$ every carrier's cost differences equal the social-cost differences, so every social optimum is an equilibrium; this is the classical marginal-contribution charge \citep{Wolpert2001,Marden2013} (Proposition~\ref{prop:pers} in \ref{app:dip}). A city cannot charge per carrier, and the next results measure what flat zone charges lose. In the convexified (mixed) game let $x^*$ minimise the convexified social cost $\overline{\mathrm{SC}}$, with aggregate $\bar Q^*$.

\begin{theorem}[Loss under asymmetric lots]\label{thm:asym}
Let $u=\kappa\odot\bar Q^*-\tfrac m2\odot\bar d$. Every convexified equilibrium $x^u$ satisfies $0\le\overline{\mathrm{SC}}(x^u)-\overline{\mathrm{SC}}(x^*)\le(1+\varrho)E$, where $\varrho=\max_z\kappa_z/(b_z+\kappa_z)<1$ and $E=\sum_{i,z}\tfrac{m_z}{2}|d_{iz}-\bar d_z|\,d_{iz}$ measures how unequal the lots in a zone are.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Exact implementation with symmetric lots]\label{thm:convex}
If all lots in a zone are equal, the tangent charge makes $x^*$ a convexified equilibrium, and every convexified equilibrium under it has aggregate $\bar Q^*$ and social cost $\overline{\mathrm{SC}}(x^*)$.
\end{theorem}

\emph{Sketch.} The identity holds for the convexified potential and social cost with the same algebra. Because $\overline{\mathrm{SC}}$ is linear plus a quadratic in $\bar Q$, its expansion around $x^*$ is exact; comparing it with the identity at a minimiser of the potential bounds the aggregate deviation by the lot-asymmetry term $L$, and $|L|\le E$. With equal lots $L\equiv0$.

\paragraph{The pure game} For a pure target $\sigma^*$ and an admissible box $\mathcal U$ of charges let
\[
P(\sigma^*)=\big\{u\in\mathcal U:\ \ip{q_i(p)-q_i(\sigma^*_i)}{u}\ge J^0_i(\sigma^*_i;\sigma^*_{-i})-J^0_i(p;\sigma^*_{-i})\ \ \forall i,\ \forall p\in\mathcal P_i\big\},
\]
with $J^0_i$ the cost at zero charges. Since $J^u_i=J^0_i+\ip{u}{q_i}$, $P(\sigma^*)$ is exactly the set of charges under which $\sigma^*$ is a pure Nash equilibrium: a polyhedron, possibly empty. Toll polyhedra of this kind are classical for nonatomic routing \citep{Bergendorff1997,Hearn1998} and inverse optimisation \citep{Ahuja2001}.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{DIP: dual implementation pricing}\label{alg:dip}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State \textbf{Target.} Column generation on the convexified social cost (the carriers' oracle at social prices), rounding and coordinate polish $\Rightarrow$ pure profile $\sigma^*$ with aggregate $Q^*$
\State \textbf{Reference.} $u^{\rm ref}\gets\kappa\odot Q^*-\tfrac m2\odot\bar d$ (tangent charge); cuts $\mathcal C\gets\emptyset$; $u\gets u^{\rm ref}$
\Repeat
  \State for each $i$: $b_i\gets$ best response to $\sigma^*_{-i}$ under $u$ \Comment{$N$ routing calls, no equilibrium solve}
  \State for each $i$ with $J^u_i(b_i)<J^u_i(\sigma^*_i)$: add $\ip{q_i(b_i)-q_i(\sigma^*_i)}{u}\ge J^0_i(\sigma^*_i)-J^0_i(b_i)+\varepsilon$ to $\mathcal C$
  \State \textbf{if} no cut was added: \Return $u$ \Comment{$\sigma^*$ is an exact pure equilibrium under $u$}
  \State $u\gets\arg\min\{\|u-u^{\rm ref}\|_1:u\in\mathcal U\text{ satisfies }\mathcal C\}$ (LP); \textbf{if} infeasible: \textbf{break}
\Until{}
\State \textbf{Fallback} (only if $P(\sigma^*)=\emptyset$): best-response dynamics from $\sigma^*$ under the least-violating charge and under $u^{\rm ref}$; keep the better equilibrium
\State \textbf{Instruments.} $s=\min_zu_z$, $t_z=(u_z-s)/r$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:instr})
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{theorem}[Separation and finite termination]\label{thm:cut}
(a) $u\in P(\sigma^*)$ if and only if no carrier has a strictly improving best response to $\sigma^*_{-i}$ under $u$; an improving best response gives that carrier's most violated constraint. (b) With $\varepsilon=0$, Algorithm~\ref{alg:dip} stops after at most $1+\sum_i(|\mathcal P_i|-1)$ rounds, either with $u\in P(\sigma^*)$ or with an infeasible LP, which happens only if $P(\sigma^*)=\emptyset$. Each round costs $N$ oracle calls and no equilibrium solve.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proposition}[Single-level form]\label{prop:opt}
With optimistic selection, the leader's value is $V^\star=\min\{\mathrm{SC}(\sigma):\sigma\text{ pure},\ P(\sigma)\ne\emptyset\}\ge\min_\sigma\mathrm{SC}(\sigma)$. If Algorithm~\ref{alg:dip} ends in its main loop, the leader's loss is at most the suboptimality of the target, $\mathrm{SC}(\sigma^*)-\min_\sigma\mathrm{SC}(\sigma)$; in particular DIP is at least as good as any grid over any subfamily of charges when its target is a social optimum.
\end{proposition}

\begin{theorem}[Loss of the fallback]\label{thm:fallback}
For any charge $u$ with $v=u-u^{\tan}$ and $Q^\circ=Q^*$, the equilibrium $\sigma^u$ reached from $\sigma^*$ by improving moves satisfies $\mathrm{SC}(\sigma^u)-\mathrm{SC}(\sigma^*)\le\sum_z\tfrac{\kappa_z}{2}(Q^u_z-Q^*_z)^2+L(\sigma^*)-L(\sigma^u)+\ip{v}{Q^*-Q^u}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[Oracle calls]\label{cor:calls}
DIP uses the calls of the social target, $N$ calls per cutting-plane round, and, only if $P(\sigma^*)$ is empty, the calls of the fallback dynamics. A grid of $G$ points with an equilibrium solve of $C_{\rm eq}$ calls per point uses $GC_{\rm eq}$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{theorem}[Imprecision shifts the charge]\label{thm:credal}
Under $\Gamma$-maximin credal boxes of width $\delta$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:gamma}), the charges that implement $\sigma^*$ are $P(\sigma^*)-\hat s(\delta)$ with $\hat s_z(\delta)=\delta(b_zw_{(z,Q)}+r\,g_zw_{(z,X)})$. One run of DIP therefore gives $u(\delta)=u(0)-\hat s(\delta)$ for every width at no further oracle cost, and the leader attains the same social cost at every width as long as $u(\delta)$ is admissible. For E-admissibility, maximality and interval dominance, $u(0)$ works at every width.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proposition}[When one instrument suffices]\label{prop:uniform}
A uniform per-delivery charge implements the convexified optimum under equal lots if the tangent charge is the same in every active zone. With the calibration of Section~\ref{sec:data} ($\alpha_z=1.5\,$fee, $b_z=\alpha_z/(2Q^{\rm full}_z)$) and $n_z$ carriers with equal lots in zone $z$, the congestion-free tangent charge is $u_z=-0.375\,\mathrm{fee}/n_z$: a subsidy of 37.5\% of the fee in single-carrier zones and 19\% in two-carrier zones. The congestion charge contributes only spatial differentiation.
\end{proposition}

Proofs are in \ref{app:dip}. Theorem~\ref{thm:cut}(a) is what makes the method cheap: the separation oracle is one best response per carrier at the fixed target, not an equilibrium solve. Selection remains optimistic: the target is an exact equilibrium under the returned charges, but other equilibria may coexist.
\paragraph{Prior art and the claim} The tangent charge is the Pigou--Cournot corrective tax \citep{Buchanan1969,Barnett1980}; carrier-specific charges are marginal-contribution utilities \citep{Wolpert2001,Marden2013}; toll polyhedra and implementing a target flow are classical \citep{Bergendorff1997,Hearn1998,Fotakis2008,Bhaskar2019}; the loop is an inverse-optimisation cutting plane \citep{Ahuja2001,Wang2009,Bodur2022} with best-response cuts as used for integer programming games \citep{Dragotto2023}. The closest leader-side method \citep{Masiha2026} needs an equilibrium solve per outer step. What remains new is modest: the exact potential--welfare identity of the lumpy Cournot--congestion game and the loss bounds it gives with the lot-asymmetry number $E$; the use of the carriers' own NP-hard best responses at the fixed target as the separation step; the translation of the implementing charges by the $\Gamma$-maximin surcharge; and the competitor-count rule.


\subsection{Aggregate-response pricing (ARP)}\label{sec:arp}

DIP and the grid need the carriers' routing oracle, which a real city does not have. ARP asks how much of the leader's gain is reachable from what the city can observe. The city knows its own demand and congestion slopes $(b,g,r)$ and hence $\kappa_z=g_zr^2$ and $m_z=b_z+\kappa_z$. It posts a per-delivery-equivalent charge $u\in\R^Z$ (realised, as in Proposition~\ref{prop:instr}, by a uniform per-delivery charge plus non-negative zone congestion charges), the carriers settle in a pure equilibrium by their own best responses, and the city observes the deliveries $Q_z$ and the number $n_z$ of active carriers in each zone, nothing else.

\paragraph{The flat rule} With an estimate $\bar d_z$ of the mean lot, the city updates
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rule}
u^{t+1}_z=(1-\theta_z)u^t_z+\theta_z\big[\kappa_zQ^t_z-\tfrac{m_z}{2}\bar d_z\big],\qquad\theta_z\in(0,1].
\end{equation}
With $\theta=1$ this is DIP's tangent charge evaluated at the observed aggregate instead of a computed target. The lot estimate is $Q_z/n_z$; when $n_z$ changes by one between observations, the jump of $Q_z$ is the lot of the carrier that entered or left (Lemma~\ref{lem:lots}); for a zone never served nothing is learned and the city-wide mean observed lot is used.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{ARP flat rule}\label{alg:arp}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require slopes $b,g,r$; damping $\theta$
\State $u\gets0$; observe $(Q,n)$ of the unpriced equilibrium
\Repeat
  \State update the lot estimates $\bar d_z$ from $(Q,n)$ and the previous observations
  \State $u_z\gets(1-\theta)u_z+\theta[\kappa_zQ_z-\tfrac{m_z}{2}\bar d_z]$ for all $z$
  \State post $u$; the carriers best-respond from their current plans until nobody moves; observe $(Q,n)$
\Until{$u=\kappa Q-\tfrac m2\bar d$ (fixed point) or the posted charge and the market state repeat (cycle)}
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph{The marginal tariff (ARP-M)} The flat rule charges each delivery the externality at the observed aggregate. The exact externality of carrier $i$'s lot on the others is $\kappa_zd_{iz}Q_{-i,z}$, and its market-power wedge is $\tfrac{b_z}{2}d_{iz}^2$; both are functions of the carrier's own deliveries and of the zone total, so an anonymous nonlinear tariff can bill them exactly, in the limit of re-tangenting at every transaction.

\begin{theorem}[Marginal tariff; the mechanism is classical]\label{thm:mct}
Let every carrier pay, in every zone, $T_z(q_{iz},Q_z)=\kappa_zq_{iz}(Q_z-q_{iz})-\tfrac{b_z}{2}q_{iz}^2$. Then (a) $J^T_i(p;\sigma_{-i})=\mathrm{SC}(p,\sigma_{-i})-\mathrm{SC}(\emptyset_i,\sigma_{-i})$ for all $i$, $p$ and $\sigma_{-i}$, so $\mathrm{SC}$ is an exact potential of the tariff game; (b) the pure equilibria are exactly the profiles that no single carrier can improve socially, every social optimum is a pure equilibrium, and best-response dynamics from any profile never increase $\mathrm{SC}$ and stop at such an equilibrium; (c) given the rivals, the tariff is linear in the carrier's own service indicators, so a best response is one oracle call with the zone prices shifted by $\kappa_zQ_{-i,z}-\tfrac{b_z}{2}d_{iz}$.
\end{theorem}
The mechanism is the marginal-contribution tariff of \citet{Wolpert2001}, \citet{Sandholm2002} and \citet{Marden2013}; what is stated here is its lumpy form in the city game and the consequence that DIP's implementation step is unnecessary under it. The tariff needs billing on own deliveries and the zone total. It leaves equilibrium selection open: the carriers' dynamics stop at a unilateral social optimum, which may be a local optimum of $\mathrm{SC}$. In the experiments we run it with the carriers' own best responses from the status quo (tariff+BR) and with the potential-minimising solver of the engine on the social-cost potential (tariff+SO).

\paragraph{Theory of the flat rule} Convexify the plan sets: let $\cX=\prod_i\Delta(\cP_i)$, $\bar q_i(x_i)=\E_{x_i}q_i$, $\bar Q(x)=\sum_i\bar q_i$ and $\cQ=\bar Q(\cX)$. With a reference lot vector $\bar d$ define the carriers' aggregate value function $G(Q)=\min\{\sum_i\E_{x_i}[R_i+\sum_z\tfrac{m_z}{2}(d_{iz}-\bar d_z)q_{iz}]:\bar Q(x)=Q\}$ and $F(Q)=G(Q)+\sum_z[-\alpha_zQ_z+\tfrac{m_z}{2}Q_z^2+\tfrac{m_z}{2}\bar d_zQ_z]+\iota_{\cQ}(Q)$, where $R_i$ is the routing cost. Let $L_{\bar d}(\sigma)=\sum_{i,z}\tfrac{m_z}{2}(d_{iz}-\bar d_z)q_{iz}$ be the lot-asymmetry term of Section~\ref{sec:dip} with reference lots $\bar d$, $E_{\bar d}=\sum_{i,z}\tfrac{m_z}{2}|d_{iz}-\bar d_z|d_{iz}$, and $g(Q)=\sum_z[\tfrac{\kappa_z}{2}Q_z^2-\tfrac{m_z}{2}\bar d_zQ_z]$. All statements below are proved in \ref{app:arp}; $\|v\|_w^2=\sum_zw_zv_z^2$.

\begin{theorem}[The response is a proximal map]\label{thm:resolvent}
$F$ is $1$-strongly convex in $\|\cdot\|_m$. For every charge $u$, all convexified equilibria share one aggregate $Q(u)=\arg\min_Q\{F(Q)+\ip uQ\}=\nabla F^*(-u)$, and $\|Q(u_1)-Q(u_2)\|_m\le\|u_1-u_2\|_{m^{-1}}$. The leader's problem $\min\overline{\mathrm{SC}}+L_{\bar d}$ is the Fenchel dual $\min_uD(u)$, $D(u)=F^*(-u)+\sum_z(u_z+\tfrac{m_z}{2}\bar d_z)^2/(2\kappa_z)$, whose gradient $\kappa^{-1}\odot(u+\tfrac m2\bar d)-Q(u)$ is observable from the aggregate alone. Its unique minimiser $u^*$ is the unique fixed point of $u\mapsto\kappa\odot Q(u)-\tfrac m2\odot\bar d$. With symmetric lots within zones the fixed point implements a convexified social optimum; in general it is within $E_{\bar d}$ of one.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Instance-free contraction]\label{thm:rate}
Let $\varrho=\max_z\kappa_z/m_z<1$ over zones with a lot. In the convexified game, (a) the plain rule ($\theta=1$) satisfies $\|u^{t+1}-u^*\|_{\kappa^{-1}}\le\varrho\|u^t-u^*\|_{\kappa^{-1}}$; (b) the damped rule with $\theta=2/(2+\varrho)$ contracts at ratio $\varrho/(2+\varrho)<1/3$. The ratios depend on the instance only through $\varrho$, not on $Z$, $N$, the number of customers, the lots, the routing costs or the probability boxes.
\end{theorem}
The rate comes from the Cournot slope $b_z$: without the demand side ($b=0$), $\varrho=1$ and there is no contraction, which is why the nonatomic literature uses averaging \citep{Yang2004}. The flat rule is gradient descent on $D$ in the $\kappa^{-1}$ geometry; this identification, and the resulting rate in a lumpy NP-hard routing game, is the new part.

\begin{theorem}[Pure game and identification]\label{thm:pure}
(a) If $\sigma$ is a pure equilibrium under $u=\kappa\odot Q(\sigma)-\tfrac m2\odot\bar d+v$, then for every carrier $i$ and plan $p$, with $\sigma'=(p,\sigma_{-i})$ and $\Delta Q=Q(\sigma')-Q(\sigma)$, $\mathrm{SC}(\sigma')\ge\mathrm{SC}(\sigma)+[L_{\bar d}(\sigma)-L_{\bar d}(\sigma')]+\sum_z\tfrac{\kappa_z}{2}\Delta Q_z^2-\ip v{\Delta Q}$: at an exact fixed point with symmetric lots, no carrier can lower the social cost unilaterally. (b) If the observed aggregate $Q^t$ differs from the convexified response by $e^t$, then $\|u^{t+1}-u^*\|_{\kappa^{-1}}\le\varrho\|u^t-u^*\|_{\kappa^{-1}}+\|e^t\|_\kappa$, hence $\limsup\|u^t-u^*\|_{\kappa^{-1}}\le\sup\|e^t\|_\kappa/(1-\varrho)$; if the carriers settle in potential minimisers, $\|e^t\|_\kappa\le\sqrt{2\varrho\eta_t}$ with $\eta_t$ the rounding gap of the potential. (c) The convexified response map determines $F$ up to a constant, so the convexified optimum is all that an observation-only scheme can target; the pure social optimum is not identified (Example~\ref{ex:ident}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proposition}[Imprecise carriers]\label{prop:arpcredal}
Under $\Gamma$-maximin beliefs of width $\delta$ the response to $u$ is the precise response to $u+\hat s(\delta)$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:gamma}), so the flat rule needs no knowledge of $\delta$ and contracts at the same rates; it only moves the fixed point, and the loss in $\overline{\mathrm{SC}}+L_{\bar d}$ against the precise fixed point is at most $\tfrac12\|\hat s(\delta)\|_{m^{-1}}^2$. Under E-admissibility of width $\delta$, a pure target is implemented exactly once the posted charge is within the surcharge band $|u_z-u^*_z|\le\hat s_z(\delta)$ of an implementing charge (with a side condition on rival volumes, \ref{app:arp}), after at most $\lceil\log(\cdot)/\log(1/\varrho)\rceil$ rounds.
\end{proposition}

\paragraph{Prior art and the claim} The central step, charge the externality at the observed aggregate and iterate, is classical: trial-and-error marginal-cost pricing \citep{Yang2004}, evolutionary implementation of pricing \citep{Sandholm2002}, and, for atomic Cournot games with continuous strategies, adaptive incentive design \citep{Maheshwari2022,Maheshwari2026}, whose fixed points are socially optimal. Marginal-contribution tariffs are classical. What is new, as far as we found, is the proximal-map identification and the instance-free rates in the lumpy routing game (Theorems~\ref{thm:resolvent} and~\ref{thm:rate}), the fixed-point and identification results for the pure game (Theorem~\ref{thm:pure}), and the absorption of the leader's error by the carriers' credal width (Proposition~\ref{prop:arpcredal}). We do not claim a guarantee on which pure equilibrium the carriers select; Theorem~\ref{thm:pure}(b) is conditional on potential minimisation.

\section{Equilibrium theory}\label{sec:theory}

This section states the results on pure and imprecise equilibria; complete proofs are in \ref{app:proofs}. Results labelled \emph{classical} are known and cited.

\subsection{Imprecise beliefs}

\begin{assumption}[Traffic box]\label{ass:traffic}
$\Xi=\prod_z[\underline\mu_z,\overline\mu_z]\subset\R^Z_{\ge0}$ with widths $\varpi_z=\overline\mu_z-\underline\mu_z$; a nominal mean $\hat\mu\in\Xi$ is fixed and the true mean $\mu$ lies in $\Xi$.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Credal sets, clipped and consistent]\label{ass:credal}
Fix weights $w\in\R^d_{>0}$ and a width $\delta\ge0$. For $c\in\R^d_{\ge0}$ let $K^\delta(c)=\{m\in\R^d_{\ge0}:|m_k-c_k|\le\delta w_k\ \forall k\}$. At a pure profile $\sigma$, carrier $i$'s credal set $\cM_i^\delta(\sigma)$ is the set of Borel probability laws $P$ on $\R^d_{\ge0}\times\R^Z_{\ge0}$ with finite first moments such that $\E_P[m]\in K^\delta(A_{-i}(\sigma))$ and $\E_P[\xi]\in\Xi$.
\end{assumption}

The box is centred at the realised rival aggregate, which is the consistency requirement of equilibrium in beliefs \citep{Lo1996}, and it is clipped at zero, so every belief is a feasible aggregate. The width $\delta$ measures how much a carrier is unsure about its rivals' activity, in units $w_k$.

\begin{definition}[Decision criteria; \citealp{Troffaes2007}]\label{def:criteria}
For a finite option set $\cP$ and a set $\cM$ of laws, with $\underline\E=\inf_{P\in\cM}\E_P$ and $\overline\E=\sup_{P\in\cM}\E_P$, an option $p$ is \emph{E-admissible} if it maximises $\E_P[u(\cdot)]$ for some $P\in\cM$; \emph{maximal} if no $p'$ has $\underline\E[u(p')-u(p)]>0$; \emph{interval-undominated} (ID) if $\overline\E[u(p)]\ge\max_{p'}\underline\E[u(p')]$; and \emph{$\Gamma$-maximin} if $\underline\E[u(p)]=\max_{p'}\underline\E[u(p')]$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Imprecise credal Nash equilibrium]\label{def:icne}
For $C\in\{E,M,ID,\Gamma\}$ and $\delta\ge0$, $\ICNE_C(\delta)$ is the set of pure profiles $\sigma$ in which every $\sigma_i$ is $C$-admissible for $(\cP_i,\cM_i^\delta(\sigma))$ with payoff $u_i$. The existence threshold is $\delta_0^C=\inf\{\delta\ge0:\ICNE_C(\delta)\neq\emptyset\}$.
\end{definition}

\subsection{Reduction to mean boxes and the criteria}

Write $\ub_i(p;m,\xi)$ for the payoff of Assumption~\ref{ass:affine} evaluated at the means $(m,\xi)$ and $K_i^\delta(\sigma)=K^\delta(A_{-i}(\sigma))\times\Xi$.

\begin{lemma}[Reduction of the credal set to its mean box]\label{lem:reduction}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:plans}--\ref{ass:credal}: (a) for every $P\in\cM_i^\delta(\sigma)$ and every $p$, $\E_P[u_i(p)]=\ub_i(p;\E_Pm,\E_P\xi)$; (b) $P\mapsto(\E_Pm,\E_P\xi)$ maps $\cM_i^\delta(\sigma)$ onto $K_i^\delta(\sigma)$; (c) hence each criterion of Definition~\ref{def:criteria} coincides with the same criterion evaluated over the compact box $K_i^\delta(\sigma)$, and infima and suprema are attained.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[Nesting and monotonicity]\label{lem:nesting}
For every $\delta\ge0$, \[\ICNE_E(\delta)\subseteq\ICNE_M(\delta)\subseteq\ICNE_{ID}(\delta)\quad\text{and}\quad\ICNE_\Gamma(\delta)\subseteq\ICNE_M(\delta).\] For $C\in\{E,M,ID\}$ and $\delta\le\delta'$, $\ICNE_C(\delta)\subseteq\ICNE_C(\delta')$. For $C=\Gamma$ the monotonicity fails (Example~\ref{ex:gamma} in \ref{app:proofs}).
\end{lemma}

The inclusions between criteria are due to \citet{Troffaes2007}. Monotonicity in $\delta$ says that more imprecision can only enlarge the set of E-admissible, maximal or interval-undominated equilibria; $\Gamma$-maximin behaves differently because the worst case moves with $\delta$.

\begin{lemma}[$\Gamma$-maximin surcharge]\label{lem:gamma}
If $\varphi_i(p),t_i(p)\ge0$, the lower expectation over $K^\delta(c)\times\Xi$ is attained, for every plan simultaneously, at the upper corner $(c+\delta w,\overline\mu)$ and is linear in mixtures. Consequently $\ICNE_\Gamma(\delta)$ is the set of pure Nash equilibria of the precise game $G^\Gamma_\delta$ with private profits
\[
\pi_i^\Gamma(p)=\pi_i(p)-\delta\ip{\varphi_i(p)}{Mw}-\gamma\ip{t_i(p)}{\overline\mu},
\]
the same coupling, and traffic set to zero.
\end{lemma}

The surcharge $\delta\ip{\varphi_i(p)}{Mw}$ is what a cautious carrier adds to the cost of activity in zone $k$ because it fears up to $\delta w_k$ more rival activity than it observes. Section~\ref{sec:results} uses it to compute $\Gamma$-maximin equilibria of the city games.

\subsection{Existence under exposure-based congestion}

\begin{proposition}[Existence under D1 at every width]\label{prop:D1}
Under D1, for every $\hat\xi\in\Xi$,
\[
\Psi_{\hat\xi}(\sigma)=\textstyle\sum_i\big[\pi_i(\sigma_i)+\tfrac12\|a_i(\sigma_i)\|_M^2-\gamma\ip{t_i(\sigma_i)}{\hat\xi}\big]-\tfrac12\|A(\sigma)\|_M^2
\]
is an exact potential of the precise game at traffic $\hat\xi$. Every maximiser lies in $\ICNE_E(\delta)$ for all $\delta\ge0$, so $\delta_0^E=\delta_0^M=\delta_0^{ID}=0$, and $\ICNE_\Gamma(\delta)\neq\emptyset$ for all $\delta\ge0$.
\end{proposition}

\emph{Sketch.} For a unilateral change of carrier $i$ from contribution $a$ to $a'$ with rival aggregate $R$, the quadratic terms give $\tfrac12\|a'\|_M^2-\tfrac12\|a\|_M^2-\tfrac12\|R+a'\|_M^2+\tfrac12\|R+a\|_M^2=-\ip{a'-a}{MR}$, which equals the change of the coupling term when $\varphi_i=a_i$. A maximiser of an exact potential is a pure equilibrium \citep{Monderer1996}, and the centre of every credal box is the realised aggregate. For $\Gamma$, $G^\Gamma_\delta$ is again of type D1.

\subsection{Credal absorption}

Let $x^*$ be a mixed equilibrium of the precise game at traffic $\hat\xi\in\Xi$ (it exists by \citealp{Nash1951}; under affine coupling, mixed equilibria are exactly the profiles in which every plan in the support is a best response to the mean rival aggregate, Proposition~\ref{prop:relaxed}). Write $B_j$ for carrier $j$'s best-response set at $x^*$, $\abar_j=\sum_px^*_j(p)a_j(p)$, $\Abar^*_{-i}=\sum_{j\ne i}\abar_j$, and define the spreads $s_{jk}=\max_{p,p'\in B_j}|a_{jk}(p)-a_{jk}(p')|$ and $r_{jk}=\max_{p\in B_j}|a_{jk}(p)-\abar_{jk}|$, with $\bar\sigma=\max_{j,k}s_{jk}/w_k$, $\bar\rho=\max_{j,k}r_{jk}/w_k$, $d_L=\dim\spn\bigcup_j(a_j(B_j)-a_j(B_j))$ and $k^\sharp=\min\{N,d_L\}$. Let $\sigma_{\max}=\max_{i,k}\operatorname{range}_{p\in\cP_i}a_{ik}(p)/w_k$, so that $\bar\rho\le\bar\sigma\le\sigma_{\max}$. The \emph{anchored radius} of a pure profile $p\in\prod_jB_j$ is $\hat\delta(p)=\max_i\|A_{-i}(p)-\Abar^*_{-i}\|_{w,\infty}$.

\begin{theorem}[Credal absorption]\label{thm:main}
Assume \ref{ass:plans}--\ref{ass:credal} and let $x^*$ be a mixed equilibrium at $\hat\xi\in\Xi$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=*]
\item \emph{Absorption.} For every $p\in\prod_jB_j$ and every $\delta\ge\hat\delta(p)$, $p\in\ICNE_E(\delta)\subseteq\ICNE_M(\delta)\subseteq\ICNE_{ID}(\delta)$.
\item \emph{Radius.} $\min_{p\in\prod_jB_j}\hat\delta(p)\le\min\big\{\max_i\max_k\sum_{j\ne i}r_{jk}/w_k,\ k^\sharp\bar\sigma+\bar\rho,\ \bar\sigma\sqrt{k^\sharp\ln(2d)/2}+\bar\rho\big\}$.
\item \emph{Uniform existence.} $\delta_0^{ID}\le\delta_0^M\le\delta_0^E\le\sigma_{\max}\min\{N-1,\ d+1,\ 1+\sqrt{d\ln(2d)/2}\}$.
\item \emph{Construction.} A profile achieving the second bound is obtained from $x^*$ by one linear program (a vertex of the polytope of Shapley--Folkman decompositions) followed by an arbitrary choice for at most $k^\sharp$ carriers; one achieving the third bound by independent sampling of these carriers, with success probability at least $1-2d\exp(-2t^2/(k^\sharp\bar\sigma^2))$ per draw for target $t+\bar\rho$; one achieving the first bound is any $p\in\prod_jB_j$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\emph{Sketch.} Every carrier in $p\in\prod_jB_j$ plays a best response to the \emph{anchor} $\Abar^*_{-i}$, which is nonnegative. If $\delta\ge\hat\delta(p)$, the anchor lies in the clipped box around the realised aggregate $A_{-i}(p)$, so the Dirac law at the anchor belongs to the credal set and witnesses E-admissibility. The radius bounds follow from the decomposition $A_{-i}(p)-\Abar^*_{-i}=\sum_{j\in J}(a_j(p_j)-y_j)-(a_i(p_i)-\abar_i)$, where $J$ is the set of at most $k^\sharp$ carriers left mixed at a vertex of the Shapley--Folkman polytope (Theorem~\ref{thm:sf}); the sum is bounded deterministically by $k^\sharp\bar\sigma$ or, after independent sampling, by Hoeffding's inequality \citep{Hoeffding1963} and a union bound. The own term $a_i(p_i)-\abar_i$ cannot be removed (it is the ``$+1$'' in the bound of \citealp{Liu2023}).

\begin{corollary}[$\varepsilon$-Nash in the precise game; cf.\ \citealp{Liu2023}]\label{cor:eps}
A profile $p$ as in Theorem~\ref{thm:main}(a) is an $\varepsilon$-Nash equilibrium of the precise game at any $\mu\in\Xi$ with $\varepsilon_i\le\hat\delta(p)L_i(p)+\gamma\sum_z\varpi_zT_{iz}(p)$, where $L_i(p)=\max_{p'}\sum_kw_kM_k|\varphi_{ik}(p')-\varphi_{ik}(p_i)|$ and $T_{iz}(p)=\max_{p'}|t_{iz}(p')-t_{iz}(p_i)|$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}[Novelty and regime]\label{rem:regime}
Corollary~\ref{cor:eps} restates, in our notation, the $N$-independent approximate equilibrium of \citet{Liu2023}, who also use a Shapley--Folkman argument on the generators of a convexified equilibrium; we claim no novelty for it. What Theorem~\ref{thm:main} adds is that under E-admissibility the rounding error is \emph{absorbed} by the belief width: the rounded profile is an \emph{exact} imprecise equilibrium, not an approximate one. The proof of part (a) is short; the content of the result lies in the bound, its attainment (Proposition~\ref{prop:tight}), its failure for $\Gamma$-maximin (Remark~\ref{rem:gamma-abs}), and the contrast with the complexity result below. In the city instances $N\le6$ and $d=2Z\ge84$, so $k^\sharp=N$, the Shapley--Folkman step selects nothing, and the operative bound is the support-selection bound $(N-1)\bar\rho$. Shapley--Folkman matters only when the number of players exceeds the dimension, for example with per-vehicle or crowdsourced players.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[$\Gamma$-maximin]\label{rem:gamma-abs}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:gamma}, $\ICNE_\Gamma(\delta)$ is the pure-equilibrium set of a \emph{precise} game, so absorption does not apply to it, and under D2 that set can be empty (Proposition~\ref{prop:tight} at $\delta=0$).
\end{remark}

\subsection{Tightness}

\begin{proposition}[The support-selection bound is attained]\label{prop:tight}
Let $N=2$, one zone, D2, $M=\gamma c=1$ and weight $w>0$. Carrier 1 has plans $A_1:(n,\tau,\pi)=(1,4,6)$ and $B_1:(3,1,0)$; carrier 2 has $X:(1,1,0)$ and $Y:(3,4,6)$; payoffs are $\pi-\tau\cdot n_{\rm rival}$. Then the game has no pure Nash equilibrium, $\delta_0^E=\delta_0^M=1/w$, and the bound $(N-1)\bar\rho$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}(b) equals $1/w$.
\end{proposition}

In each of the four pure profiles exactly one carrier has a strict incentive to deviate at the realised count, and it becomes indifferent only at a belief that differs from the realised count by one vehicle.

\begin{proposition}[Linear dependence on the number of zones is necessary]\label{prop:cycles}
For every $Z\ge1$ and $N\ge2$ there is a D2 game with $Z$ zones in which every pure profile has $\max_i\varepsilon_i\ge3\lceil Z/2\rceil$, while every profile of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}(a) satisfies $\hat\delta(p)L_i(p)\le3Z$ ($w=1$).
\end{proposition}

\subsection{Complexity under vehicle-count congestion}

\begin{theorem}[NP-completeness under D2]\label{thm:np}
Consider the problem of deciding $\ICNE_C(\delta)\neq\emptyset$ for a game given by explicit plan lists with rational data, $M$, $w$, a degenerate traffic box and a rational $\delta\ge0$. For each $C\in\{E,M,ID,\Gamma\}$ the problem is in NP. For every digraph $D$ with maximum out-degree $\Delta^+\ge1$ one constructs in polynomial time a D2 instance with two plans per carrier and one zone per arc such that, for all $\delta\in[0,1/(2\Delta^++1))$, $\ICNE_E(\delta)=\ICNE_M(\delta)=\ICNE_{ID}(\delta)=\ICNE_\Gamma(\delta)$ is the set of profiles that play ``in'' exactly on a kernel of $D$. Hence the problem is strongly NP-complete for each criterion, already at $\delta=0$, and for every fixed $\delta\in[0,1/5)$.
\end{theorem}

\emph{Sketch.} Deciding whether a digraph has a kernel (an independent set that absorbs every other vertex) is NP-complete \citep{Chvatal1973}, also for planar digraphs with in- and out-degree at most two \citep{Fraenkel1981}. Each vertex becomes a carrier with an ``out'' plan of zero data and an ``in'' plan that puts one vehicle on each out-arc zone and two vehicles on each in-arc zone, with exposure one on out-arcs and a small exposure on in-arcs. The gain of ``in'' is positive if no out-neighbour plays ``in'' and at most $-1$ otherwise, and the sign is constant on every credal box of width below $1/(2\Delta^++1)$, so all four criteria select the realised strict best response.

\begin{corollary}[Inapproximability of the existence threshold]\label{cor:inapprox}
Unless $\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}$, no polynomial algorithm approximates $\delta_0^E$ of a D2 instance within any fixed factor, or within an additive error below $1/5$.
\end{corollary}

Pure-equilibrium existence is known to be NP-complete in polymatrix games and integer programming games \citep{Apt2022,Carvalho2022}; Theorem~\ref{thm:np} is a direct kernel reduction specialised to the rank-one D2 structure and extended to four credal criteria and a range of widths. Together with Proposition~\ref{prop:D1} it gives a complexity split between the two congestion regimes: charging or modelling congestion by exposure (vehicle-hours in a zone) yields a potential game with guaranteed pure imprecise equilibria, while counting vehicles does not. Under D2 the mixed equilibrium that Theorem~\ref{thm:main} rounds is itself hard to compute: every bimatrix game embeds as a D2 instance with $N=2$, so the step is PPAD-hard \citep{Chen2009,Daskalakis2009} (Proposition~\ref{prop:ppad}).

\subsection{Efficiency}

\begin{lemma}[Imprecise equilibria are approximate equilibria]\label{lem:transfer}
If $\sigma\in\ICNE_C(\delta)$ and $\mu\in\Xi$, then $\sigma$ is an $\varepsilon$-Nash equilibrium of the precise game with $\varepsilon_i\le\delta L_i(\sigma)+\gamma\sum_z\varpi_zT_{iz}(\sigma)$ for $C\in\{E,M\}$ and $\varepsilon_i\le2\delta H_i+2\gamma\Theta_i$ for $C=ID$, with $H_i=\max_p\sum_kw_kM_k\varphi_{ik}(p)$ and $\Theta_i=\max_p\sum_z\varpi_zt_{iz}(p)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proposition}[Monotone efficiency curves]\label{prop:cs}
For $C\in\{E,M,ID\}$ and $W>0$, $\mathrm{PoA}_C(\delta)=W^*/\min_{\ICNE_C(\delta)}W$ is nondecreasing and $\mathrm{PoS}_C(\delta)=W^*/\max_{\ICNE_C(\delta)}W$ nonincreasing in $\delta$, and both are step functions with at most $\prod_i|\cP_i|$ steps. For $C=\Gamma$ neither monotonicity holds in general.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Price of stability via the potential, D1]\label{prop:pos}
Under D1 with zero charges and $\mu=\hat\mu$, let $\sigma^*$ maximise $W$. Every profile $\sigma$ with $\Psi_{\hat\mu}(\sigma)\ge\max\Psi_{\hat\mu}-\eta$ satisfies
$W(\sigma)\ge W^*-\sum_z\big[\tfrac{b_z}{2}\sum_iq_{iz}^{*2}+\tfrac{\gamma c_z}{2}(1-\tfrac1N)T_z(\sigma)^2\big]-\eta$. In particular a potential maximiser, which lies in every $\ICNE_E(\delta)$, bounds $\mathrm{PoS}_C(\delta)$ for all $\delta$ and $C\in\{E,M,ID\}$.
\end{proposition}

A smoothness-based price-of-anarchy bound also transfers to imprecise equilibria through Lemma~\ref{lem:transfer} (Proposition~\ref{prop:smooth}), but it is conditional: we do not have smoothness constants for the Cournot--congestion game with arbitrary private routing costs. The price-of-anarchy values in Section~\ref{sec:results} are therefore empirical.

\subsection{Exact thresholds and the rounding algorithm}\label{sec:cert}

For any pure profile $p$, the exact E-threshold of carrier $i$,\[A^E_i(p)=\min\{\delta: p_i\text{ is E-admissible at width }\delta\},\]is the value of a linear program over the box, so $p\in\ICNE_E(\delta)$ if and only if $\delta\ge\max_iA^E_i(p)$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:cert}). Theorem~\ref{thm:main} and this threshold computation define an algorithm with four phases: relax (a mixed equilibrium by Frank--Wolfe on the concave relaxed potential under D1, or by a double oracle under D2; \citealp{McMahan2003}), sparsify (a vertex of the Shapley--Folkman polytope), round, and compute the exact width. Under D2 its output satisfies the bounds of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}(b) (Theorem~\ref{thm:D2}), but its relaxation phase is PPAD-hard in general. Under D1 the relaxation-and-rounding technique is classical \citep{Aubin1976,Udell2016,Bonnans2023}. The full rounding algorithm was not run on the city games, which use the D1 engine of Section~\ref{sec:algo}; the exact thresholds of Theorem~\ref{thm:cert} were used in the tests of Section~\ref{sec:res-theory}.

\begin{remark}[Nonaffine congestion]\label{rem:nonaffine}
With a power congestion term the expected payoff under mixed rival strategies is no longer a function of the mean aggregate; Lemma~\ref{lem:reduction} and the exact credal statements fail, although relaxed equilibria still exist. We do not treat this case.
\end{remark}

\subsection{What is machine-checked}\label{sec:lean}

A Lean~4 file with Mathlib (\texttt{lean/CCU/LeanTargets.lean} in the repository) formalises the finite aggregative game with clipped credal boxes and E-admissibility. It compiles with no unproved steps (no \texttt{sorry}), and every theorem in it depends only on the standard axioms \texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice} and \texttt{Quot.sound}. The following discrete core statements are machine-checked: E-admissibility implies maximality, and $\ICNE_E(\delta)$ is monotone in $\delta$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:nesting}, case E); the core of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}(a) (a profile whose plans are best responses to nonnegative anchors within the box is in $\ICNE_E(\delta)$); the Shapley--Folkman lemma for finite sets in a finite-dimensional real space, with the dimension of the space in place of $d_L$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:sf}; the formal proof lifts the sets with indicator coordinates and applies Carath\'eodory's theorem); the exact potential identity under D1 (Proposition~\ref{prop:D1}); the combinatorial core of Theorem~\ref{thm:np} (with integer-scaled gains, the equilibrium conditions hold exactly on kernels); the lower-bound core of Proposition~\ref{prop:tight}; and the counting core of Proposition~\ref{prop:cycles}. The file covers the credal and aggregative-game results only; BRL, DIP, ARP and the computational results are not formalised. Lean checks these core statements, not the full theorems: the radius bounds of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}(b)--(c), the Hoeffding step, the NP-completeness statement itself (Mathlib has no framework for polynomial-time reductions) and the algorithmic results are not formalised.%======================================================================
\section{Data and calibration}\label{sec:data}

\paragraph{Road networks and zones} For each of nine cities (New Delhi, Cairo, Moscow, Buenos Aires, New York, Mumbai, London, Paris, Berlin) we use an OpenStreetMap extract \citep{Haklay2008} distributed by BBBike (replication timestamp 16 October 2026). The drivable major-road network (motorway to tertiary, with links) is kept as an undirected graph with class-based free-flow speeds and lane counts (OSM \texttt{lanes} tag where present, class defaults otherwise). Population comes from the GHS-POP R2023A grid, epoch 2025, at 30 arc-seconds \citep{GHSPOP2023}. Zones are blocks of $4\times4$ raster cells (about 3.7\,km) inside the extract's bounding box with at least one inhabitant; zone-to-zone free-flow times are shortest paths between population-weighted centroids plus a fixed access time. Points of interest from the same extracts give a depot score per zone used to place carrier depots. Table~\ref{tab:cities} (\ref{app:tables}) summarises the instances; $Z$ ranges from \Zmin{} to \Zmax.


\paragraph{Operating parameters} Per-city parameters were collected from public web sources on 23 October 2026 and graded A (official or primary statistic), B (industry report or country-level proxy) or C (rough estimate or proxy from elsewhere). Table~\ref{tab:params} in \ref{app:tables} lists the values used; the repository file \texttt{data/parameters/SOURCES.md} gives source, URL, grade and note for each number. Congestion levels are those of the TomTom Traffic Index 2025 \citep{TomTom2025}; volume-delay curves use the BPR form with $\alpha=0.15$, $\beta=4$ \citep{BPR1964}, linearised at the observed congestion level.

\paragraph{Weak numbers} Parcels per capita are country-level figures. Driver wages are minimum-wage floors or job-site averages, not courier payroll. Drops per vehicle and van capacities outside the United States are proxies. Delivery fees in Cairo, Moscow and Buenos Aires are consumer or reference rates, not carrier revenue per parcel, and the European fees are regulator revenue per parcel. Moscow is absent from the 2025 TomTom index and uses a 2020 value. Market shares are available only for Paris and Berlin; elsewhere carriers have equal shares. The sensitivity of the equilibria to the five weakest parameters is in Table~\ref{tab:sens}.

\paragraph{Calibration rules and simplifications} Each carrier serves 22 customer zones sampled around its depot with weight proportional to population times a gravity decay in travel time, so carriers overlap in some zones. A lot is 10--50\% of a vehicle's daily drops, proportional to the zone's parcel demand. Inverse demand has intercept $\alpha_z=1.5\times$ fee and slope $b_z=\alpha_z/(2Q_z^{\rm full})$. The congestion slope $c_z$ is the derivative of the BPR curve at the TomTom level, with capacity from lane-kilometres, scaled by a slice multiplicity $m_z$ (the ratio of the city's real delivery vehicle-hours to the modelled ones) so that the slice faces the congestion of the whole city. Travel-time multipliers of nine sectors follow a lognormal law matching the TomTom all-day and peak congestion; each carrier observes 30 days, from which DKW boxes at confidence $1-\alpha$ are built. The route loss \eqref{eq:loss} uses the vehicle-hour cost (wage plus fuel) per hour, twice that as fixed cost, three times that per overtime hour, and the shift length as deadline. The simplifications are:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(S\arabic*),leftmargin=*,itemsep=0pt]
\item all-or-nothing lots: a carrier serves a customer zone fully or not at all, which makes the Cournot game lumpy;
\item the integer routing step: the main game experiments use branch-and-price with a time limit per call; some earlier experiments use price-and-branch (Section~\ref{sec:results});
\item vehicle-hours are proportional to the lot, so within a zone a congestion charge on hours and a delivery charge are collinear;
\item linear congestion (D1);
\item oracle exactness at city scale is checked against an independent oracle, not proved.
\end{enumerate}
Further proxies are the sampled customer zones instead of whole-city demand, equal market shares in seven cities, the slice multiplicity, and a \emph{broad} social cost that adds the delay imposed on background traffic (population times vehicle ownership times one hour per day, valued at the wage, times the added slowdown, divided by $m_z$). The broad objective is reported but not used for conclusions.

\paragraph{Instances as a benchmark} The pipeline (extract download, zones, travel times, depots, parameters with grades, p-box construction, game instances with seeds) is in the repository and regenerates all instances from the raw extracts. The instances are small (22 customer zones per carrier, four to six carriers) so that exact oracles are possible; they are intended as a common test bed for algorithms on multi-carrier routing games, not as validated city models.

\section{Computational study}\label{sec:results}

\paragraph{Research questions} 
\begin{description}[itemsep=0pt]
\item[RQ1] Is BRL pricing exact, and does its completion-window dominance reduce the work compared with other labelling rules? Does branch-and-price give exact integer plans on the city instances? (Section~\ref{sec:res-brl})
\item[RQ2] How do equilibria computed with an exact oracle differ from those computed with price-and-branch, and how do the equilibrium engine's components compare? (Sections~\ref{sec:res-engine} and~\ref{sec:res-eq})
\item[RQ3] How do DIP and ARP compare with the grid in oracle calls and in the leader's gain, and what do they lose? (Section~\ref{sec:res-leader})
\item[RQ4] What do the equilibria, charges, price of anarchy and imprecision effects look like on the nine cities, and how stable are the findings over seeds? (Sections~\ref{sec:res-eq}--\ref{sec:res-poa})
\item[RQ5] Do the theoretical bounds hold on the city equilibria, and does vehicle-count congestion break pure equilibria in practice? (Section~\ref{sec:res-theory})
\end{description}

\paragraph{Protocol} All numbers are generated from the result files of the repository by \texttt{make\_tables.py}. Experiments use three seeds (0, 1, 2). A seed redraws the depots, the customer zones of each carrier, the travel-time sectors and the 30 observed days, hence the probability boxes; calibration inputs are the same in every seed. With three seeds we report the mean with the minimum and maximum, or the values per seed, as a rough indication of spread, not a confidence interval. Seeds do not cover the uncertainty in the calibration inputs, which is examined separately (Table~\ref{tab:sens}).

\paragraph{Oracle and coverage} The base equilibria, the grid and DIP comparisons, the price of anarchy and the imprecision sweeps use the exact branch-and-price oracle with a limit of 20 seconds per call and a 120-second retry. Of \ExCalls{} oracle calls, \ExOptimal{} (\ExProvenPct\%) were proven optimal; \ExUnproven{} remained unproven, with a remaining gap of at most \ExMaxGap{} USD on plans worth hundreds to tens of thousands of USD. Coverage is partial in places (Table~\ref{tab:coverage}, \ref{app:tables}):
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=0pt]
\item base equilibria: all \ExEoneN{} city-seeds;
\item grid: \ExEtwoN{} city-seeds, with the full 63-point exact grid only for \ExEtwoFullCities. For \ExEtwoRedCities{} only \ExEtwoRedMin{} to \ExEtwoRedMax{} grid points per seed were solved exactly, chosen by screening on the price-and-branch grid, so the grid's gains there are lower bounds (marked $\dagger$);
\item price of anarchy: \ExEthreeN{} city-seeds; in New York, Moscow and Berlin the social-optimum benchmark of the broad objective used price-and-branch;
\item imprecision: \ExEfourN{} city-seeds; all 12 widths for \ExEfourFullCities, and \ExEfourRedWidths{} widths for \ExEfourRedCities;
\item DIP: all \DipRuns{} city-seeds; for \DipPbCases{} the comparison grid is the price-and-branch grid;

\subsection{Equilibrium engine and exactness of the oracle}\label{sec:res-engine}

\paragraph{Effect of the inexact integer step} The game experiments were first computed with price-and-branch. Tested against a fresh exact oracle, those base equilibria were exact in \ExOldExactCities{} in every seed but not in \ExOldBadN{} of the \ExOldN{} city-seeds: \ExOldBadList. In those cases a carrier could gain at most \ExOldBadMax{} USD per day with a plan the pooled integer program had not generated. City-mean welfare of the base equilibria changes by at most \ExEoneWMeanMaxNotBA\% outside Buenos Aires, and by \ExEoneWMeanBA\% in Buenos Aires, where seed 0 moves to a different equilibrium (\ExEoneWChangeBA\%); single seeds elsewhere change by up to \ExEoneWChangeMaxNotBA\% with both signs. The exact oracle thus changes which equilibrium is reached rather than improving welfare systematically. It takes three to twelve times longer per equilibrium.

\paragraph{Engine components} On the city games (Table~\ref{tab:e5} in \ref{app:tables}, price-and-branch oracle) ZMC uses between \EfiveCallsZMCmin{} and \EfiveCallsZMCmax{} calls, about ten per carrier, and its potential gap coincides with those of Frank--Wolfe and column generation with eight rounds to three digits in most cities. Plain best response needs only \EfiveCallsBRmin{} to \EfiveCallsBRmax{} calls and is as good as ZMC (within $10^{-3}$) in \EfiveBRasgoodCities; it is worse in \EfiveBRworseCities. ZMC therefore does not beat best response on these games. Reasons we can see: only \EfiveDeffMin{} to \EfiveDeffMax{} coordinates are active, so the dimension that favours ZMC in the synthetic instances is absent; the games are mildly nonconvex; and carriers share few zones. ZMC's dimension independence is not tested at city scale.

\emph{Where the gain is large.} The gain is material only where the unpriced equilibrium under-delivers. \subsection{Price of anarchy and imprecision}\label{sec:res-poa}

\paragraph{Empirical price of anarchy and stability} The social optimum is approximated by column generation on the convexified welfare problem followed by derandomised rounding and coordinate polish; this gives a feasible pure profile $W_{\rm SO}$ and, from the relaxation, an upper bound. The equilibria considered are those found by the engine, by best response from the empty and the full-service profile and from three random price vectors: between \NEmin{} and \NEmax{} distinct equilibria per city and seed. The price of anarchy and stability in Table~\ref{tab:e3} are therefore \emph{empirical}: ratios of $W_{\rm SO}$ to the equilibria found, not bounds over all equilibria.

In \PoAlowCities{} the empirical price of anarchy of the engine's equilibrium is at most \PoAlowMax{} in every seed. It is larger in \PoAhighCities{} in every seed; the ordering London $>$ Buenos Aires $>$ New Delhi holds in each seed, and across seeds the values range from \PoALondonMin{} to \PoALondonMax{} in London, \PoABAMin{} to \PoABAMax{} in Buenos Aires and \PoADelhiMin{} to \PoADelhiMax{} in New Delhi. London's value is therefore not pinned down. Compared with the price-and-branch run, city means move by at most \ExPoAMeanChangeMax{} and the ordering of the cities is unchanged; the largest single change is Buenos Aires seed 0 (\ExBAPoAold{} to \ExBAPoAnew), where a different unpriced equilibrium is selected. In \PoSgapN{} of the \PoSgapTotal{} city-seed games the price of anarchy of the worst equilibrium found exceeds the price of stability by more than 0.05: \PoSgapCases. Equilibrium selection thus matters in some games. With the grid's best charges the equilibrium comes closer to the rounded optimum, not uniformly: the shortfall is at most 0.4\% in every seed in \PricedSmallCities, and reaches \PricedGapMax\% in \PricedGapMaxCase{} and \PricedGapDelhiMax\% in New Delhi seed \PricedGapDelhiSeed. The relaxation bound exceeds the rounded optimum by up to \SObound\% (\SOboundCity), so the true price of anarchy could be larger by that margin, and all values refer to the lumpy lots (S1).

\paragraph{Imprecision} Two kinds of imprecision are varied (Table~\ref{tab:e4}, Figure~\ref{fig:e4}). The first is the width of the travel-time probability boxes, through the DKW level, from $\varepsilon=0$ to $\varepsilon=\EfourEps$; it acts through the BRL route costs. At the widest box, over all cities and seeds, deliveries fall by between \EfourPbDelMin\% and \EfourPbDelMax\% and welfare by between \EfourPbWMin\% and \EfourPbWMax\%. The grouping of cities is the same in every seed: Cairo loses no deliveries, Moscow and New York a few per cent, Paris and Berlin somewhat more, Mumbai more, and New Delhi, Buenos Aires and London about half or more; the magnitudes in the low-service cities vary by seed (London: \EfourLondonPb\% in seeds 0, 1, 2). The second is the credal width about rivals' aggregates under $\Gamma$-maximin, through the surcharge of Lemma~\ref{lem:gamma} with $w$ equal to the unpriced equilibrium aggregate, from $\delta=0$ to $\delta=\EfourDelta$; deliveries fall by between \EfourGaDelMin\% and \EfourGaDelMax\% and welfare by between \EfourGaWMin\% and \EfourGaWMax\%. Relative to the price-and-branch run, the city means of these widest-width effects change by at most \ExEfourMeanChangeMax{} percentage points and single seeds by up to \ExEfourSeedChangeMax.

Deliveries are not always monotone in the width: of \EfourSeries{} (city, seed, sweep) series, \EfourNonMonoN{} contain at least one rise, by up to \EfourMaxRise{} deliveries, all in \EfourNonMonoCities. Proposition~\ref{prop:cs} does not apply: for $\Gamma$-maximin no monotonicity holds in general, and for the probability-box sweep the reported equilibrium is a single one that can switch as the width changes. In every case a pure equilibrium was found, every run converged, and the largest regret against the pooled exact oracle was \EfourRegretMax. Imprecision acts like a tax on activity: it reduces deliveries, vehicle-hours and congestion together at the cost of welfare, and because vehicle-hours are proportional to deliveries (S3), the model cannot separate a congestion effect from a delivery effect.

\paragraph{Credal absorption} Non-trivial mixed equilibria were constructed from pairs of distinct pure equilibria by solving the two-plan indifference conditions and checking with the oracle that every support plan is a best response over all plans. This succeeded in \ThGamesMixed{} of the \ThGamesTotal{} city-seed games; \ThNoMixedCities{} have none, because almost all demand is served. On all \ThProfiles{} tested profiles from the products of the supports, no statement of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} was violated: the empirical E-threshold never exceeded the anchored radius, the minimum anchored radius never exceeded the three-term bound, the existence threshold never exceeded the $N$-uniform bound, and the order of the thresholds held (\ThViol{} violations). The bound is loose: the largest empirical threshold is \ThRatioMin{} to \ThRatioMax{} times the anchored bound (median \ThRatioMed) and \ThRatioNMin{} to \ThRatioNMax{} times the $N$-uniform bound. The ratio shows no dependence on $N$ in this small sample and a mild increase with $d$; with \ThAnchors{} anchors this is exploratory. Without the best-response polish the rounded profile of the first engine phase is E-admissible at a finite width in only \ThZmcFinite{} of \ThZmcN{} games, because some carrier plays a strictly dominated plan. Limits: mixed equilibria were found only with two-plan supports, and thresholds are exact only up to the oracle gap.

\paragraph{$\Gamma$-maximin} In all \ThGamCases{} (city, seed, $\delta$) cases the equilibrium of the surcharge game differs from the precise equilibrium, and in \ThGamNotAdm{} of them the precise equilibrium is not $\Gamma$-admissible. The surcharge equilibrium is maximal at width $\delta$, with a maximality threshold below $\delta$ in every case with a finite threshold (at $\delta=1$ between \ThGamMthrMin{} and \ThGamMthrMax{} of $\delta$ in the city means; \ThGamInf{} Cairo cases are infinite because of oracle slack). Averaged over seeds, the share of demand served drops by \ThGamDropLoMin{} to \ThGamDropLoMax{} at $\delta=0.1$ and by \ThGamDropHiMin{} to \ThGamDropHiMax{} at $\delta=1$.

\paragraph{Vehicle-count congestion} The gadgets behave as proved: the example of Proposition~\ref{prop:tight} has no pure equilibrium and existence threshold exactly $1/w$ (\ThPropOK{} weights), and the kernel construction of Theorem~\ref{thm:np} reproduces the kernel sets for all four criteria on \ThKernOK{} digraphs. Among random bilinear D2 games with three players, \ThRandDtwo{} have no pure equilibrium, against \ThRandDone{} D1 controls. Small routing-integrated D2 games always had a pure equilibrium (no pure equilibrium in \ThTiny{} game-scale cases). On the city games of Cairo, Mumbai and Paris (three seeds each) with a vehicle-count externality scaled by factors \ThCityScales, all \ThCityRuns{} best-response runs converged (\ThCityCycles{} cycles). Vehicle-count congestion did not prevent pure equilibria on the city instances; non-existence required adversarial plan menus. The D2 calibration is an assumption introduced for this test.

\subsection{Verification}\label{sec:res-verif}

On \TinyN{} tiny games (two or three carriers, three or four customer zones) solved by exhaustive enumeration, the engine's profile is a pure equilibrium of the enumerated game in every case and reaches the global potential minimum in \TinyZMCglobal{} of \TinyN; best response reaches it in \TinyBRglobal, Frank--Wolfe with rounding in \TinyFWglobal{} and column generation in \TinyCGglobal{} (Table~\ref{tab:tiny}, \ref{app:tables}). These instances support correctness more than they separate the methods. At city scale, every base equilibrium, every verified grid best point (\VerBestN{} city-seeds) and every equilibrium of the price-of-anarchy experiment has regret at most \ExRegretFreshMax{} USD against an independent fresh exact oracle (elementary routes, empty column pool). Because \ExUnproven{} oracle calls were not proven optimal, the guaranteed regret bound (incumbent of the verifying call against its proven lower bound) is nonzero for \ExBoundN{} profiles, at most \ExBoundMax{} USD (a New York base case with welfare of about 20{,}000 USD per day). These checks rely on the same branch-and-price method as the oracle; the pricer itself was checked against complete enumeration (Section~\ref{sec:res-brl}). The clearing identity of Theorem~\ref{thm:clear}(a) held to \EsixResid{} in every round of every city in the price-and-branch run (Table~\ref{tab:e6}); this check was not repeated with the exact oracle.

\section{Managerial implications}\label{sec:implications}

The model is stylised, several parameters are weak (Section~\ref{sec:data}) and the effects below are qualitative. They indicate what to test with local data, not what to do in any particular city.

\paragraph{Where pricing matters} In the model, the efficiency loss from competition is concentrated where service is incomplete. Where carriers already serve almost all demand (Cairo, Moscow, New York, Paris, Berlin), the empirical price of anarchy is close to one and charges change little; kerb space and background traffic are the more relevant levers there. Where service is partial, the instrument with the largest effect is a per-delivery subsidy, and in every material case the best flat charge was a subsidy. The size of the gain depends strongly on the sampled slice (London: \EtwoLondonBothMin\% to \EtwoLondonBothMax\% of social cost across three seeds), so the direction is better supported than the size. A congestion charge on delivery vehicle-hours alone recovered little in all but one case, because the congestion carriers impose on each other is small against the under-provision from fixed route costs and market power; the exception arose from a worse unpriced equilibrium. A subsidy has a fiscal cost that the welfare criterion with transfers does not count, access stays unequal in the low-service cities even at the best charges, and an objective that counts delay to other road users can reverse the recommendation, which argues for measuring that delay.

\paragraph{How a city could compute charges} A city does not need to search a grid. With the carriers' routing oracle, DIP gives zone charges at a few per cent of the grid's calls, with a readable structure (a subsidy of roughly 37.5\% of the fee divided by the number of carriers in the zone, corrected for congestion). Without the oracle, ARP needs only the zone totals after each posted charge, and the marginal tariff needs billing on own deliveries and the zone total. The price of observation-only pricing is selection risk: the flat rule can cycle or settle in a worse local optimum, and the tariff stops at a unilateral social optimum. A pilot with a few posted charges is a cheap check of both. Zone-specific charges are harder to administer than a flat subsidy.

\paragraph{For carriers and for modelling} Wider travel-time boxes and wider beliefs about rivals reduce the volume a carrier finds worth serving. Under the dependence-robust criterion the planned cost is well above the expected cost under the simulated law, so a carrier that can estimate a law or knows the dependence between segments should use that information. For a modeller, the practical rule is that exposure-based congestion gives a potential game with guaranteed pure equilibria, whereas vehicle-count congestion can make existence NP-complete; on our instances that worst case did not occur.

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limits}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
\item \emph{Model.} Lots are all-or-nothing (S1), so part of the measured inefficiency may be an artifact of indivisibility. Congestion is linear and, within a zone, proportional to deliveries (S3, S4). Demand is sampled, market shares are equal in seven cities, and the congestion coupling is scaled by a slice multiplicity. Most operating parameters are country-level or rough; delivery fee, drops per vehicle and the number of carriers move welfare by tens of per cent over their plausible ranges (Table~\ref{tab:sens}), and the sign of the best per-delivery instrument depends on the fee-to-cost ratio.
\item \emph{Exactness and coverage.} \ExUnproven{} of \ExCalls{} oracle calls were not proven optimal; for \ExBoundN{} profiles the guaranteed regret bound is positive (at most \ExBoundMax{} USD). The grid is exact in full only for \ExEtwoFullCities, so the grid's gains in the other six cities are lower bounds; imprecision sweeps in \ExEfourRedCities{} use three exact widths per sweep; the engine comparison, ZMC checks, theory tests and tiny-game verification use price-and-branch, whose equilibria were not exact in \ExOldBadN{} of \ExOldN{} city-seeds. Branch-and-price was tested only on instances with 22 customer zones.
\item \emph{Leader methods.} DIP is no better than its social target, its selection is optimistic, its closed forms need D1 and one hours-per-delivery ratio, and the required subsidies under strong ambiguity aversion are unrealistic. ARP was run on four cities, can cycle, can settle in a worse local optimum, and has no guarantee for the pure game unless its fixed point is reached; Theorem~\ref{thm:pure}(b) is conditional on potential minimisation. Neither method has a worst-equilibrium guarantee. The comparison with the grid is on a two-parameter family against zone-specific charges.
\item \emph{Theory.} The credal results assume affine coupling. The absorption tests rely on two-plan mixed equilibria and thresholds exact up to the oracle gap; the full rounding algorithm was not run on real data; D2 was tested only with a calibration introduced for the test. The Lean formalisation covers the discrete core of the credal results only.
\item \emph{Evidence.} Three seeds give a rough indication of spread; they vary the sampled slice, not the calibration inputs. Several readings from an earlier single-seed, price-and-branch analysis did not survive the second and third seeds or the exact oracle (the role of the congestion charge in Mumbai, a priced equilibrium within 2.4\% of the optimum everywhere, a price of stability always within 0.05 of the price of anarchy, a congestion-only gain below 1.7\% everywhere). Price-of-anarchy values come from at most six equilibria per city and seed. The broad social cost is driven by a proxy and supports no conclusion. Novelty searches were web and index searches, partly at the level of abstracts; the closest neighbours (\citealp{Liu2023,Beauchene2016,Feik2026,Masiha2026,GhosalHoWiesemann2024}) should be compared in full text.
\end{itemize}
Open questions include the complexity of finding a pure E-admissible equilibrium above the guaranteed width (we conjecture PPAD-hardness), smoothness constants for the Cournot--congestion game, an absorption result for nonaffine congestion, worst-equilibrium guarantees for the leader, and carrier-level data that would replace our proxies.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

A city that prices competing delivery carriers faces NP-hard best responses under probability-box travel times, a congestion game among the carriers, and a bilevel search over charges. BRL prices routes exactly under the dependence-robust criterion, and with branch-and-price it proved optimal robust plans on all nine metro instances; the new element is the completion-window dominance, on top of a classical decomposition. DIP made its target an exact equilibrium in every run and, in the three cities with a full exact grid, reached \DipFullGainMin--\DipFullGainMax\% of the grid's gain with \DipFullCallsMin--\DipFullCallsMax\% of its calls, partly because its charges are zone-specific. ARP, which sees only zone totals, reached \ArpFullArpMin--\ArpFullArpMax\% there with \ArpFullCallsMin--\ArpFullCallsMax{} calls, contracts at a rate that does not depend on instance size in the convexified game, and in the pure game can cycle or settle in a worse local optimum. Pure equilibria exist under exposure-based congestion, deciding their existence is strongly NP-complete under vehicle-count congestion, and a belief width independent of the number of carriers turns a mixed equilibrium into an exact pure imprecise one. On the nine cities, the empirical price of anarchy is near one where service is complete and larger, with a wide spread over seeds, where it is partial; a per-delivery subsidy captures most of the grid leader's gain in most material cases. These findings come from stylised slices with weak parameters and three seeds, and the novelty of each component is limited as stated in Section~\ref{sec:intro}.

\subsection{Classical tools}

\begin{theorem}[Shapley--Folkman, constructive vertex form; \citealp{Starr1969}]\label{thm:sf}
Let $S_1,\dots,S_N\subset\R^d$ be finite and nonempty, $L=\spn\bigcup_i(S_i-S_i)$ and $d_L=\dim L$. Index the elements of $S_i$ by $\cP_i$ through an onto map $a_i$. For $\bar x\in\sum_i\conv S_i$, the polytope $\Lambda(\bar x)=\{\lambda\in\prod_i\Delta(\cP_i):\sum_i\sum_p\lambda_i(p)a_i(p)=\bar x\}$ is nonempty, and at every vertex $\lambda$ the set $J(\lambda)=\{i:|\supp\lambda_i|\ge2\}$ has $|J(\lambda)|\le\min\{N,d_L\}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Nonemptiness: $\bar x=\sum_iy_i$ with $y_i\in\conv S_i$, and each $y_i$ is a convex combination of points $a_i(p)$. $\Lambda(\bar x)$ is a bounded polyhedron in standard form $\{\lambda\ge0:H\lambda=h\}$ with columns $c_{i,p}=(a_i(p),e_i)\in\R^d\times\R^N$ and $h=(\bar x,\mathbf 1)$. A feasible $\lambda$ is a vertex if and only if the columns $\{c_{i,p}:\lambda_i(p)>0\}$ are linearly independent. Fix a vertex and pick $p_i^0\in\supp\lambda_i$ for each $i$. The family $\{c_{i,p_i^0}\}_i\cup\{c_{i,p}-c_{i,p_i^0}:p\in\supp\lambda_i\setminus\{p_i^0\}\}$ is obtained from the positive columns by an invertible (unitriangular) transformation, so it is linearly independent. Its second part consists of the vectors $(a_i(p)-a_i(p_i^0),0)\in L\times\{0\}$, which number $\sum_i(|\supp\lambda_i|-1)\ge|J(\lambda)|$. Linearly independent vectors in $L\times\{0\}$ number at most $d_L$. Hence $|J|\le d_L$, and trivially $|J|\le N$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:reduction}]
(a) $u_i(p;m,\xi)$ is affine in $(m,\xi)$ with fixed coefficients, and $P$ has finite first moments; linearity of the integral gives the identity. (b) Into: by definition. Onto: for $k=(m,\xi)\in K_i^\delta(\sigma)$, the Dirac law at $(m,\xi)$ is supported in $\R^d_{\ge0}\times\R^Z_{\ge0}$ because $m\ge0$ and $\xi\in\Xi\subset\R^Z_{\ge0}$; its means are $(m,\xi)$, so it belongs to $\cM_i^\delta(\sigma)$. (c) Each criterion depends on $P$ only through the numbers $\E_P[u(p)]$ and $\E_P[u(p')-u(p)]$, which are affine functions of the mean by (a). By (b), ranging over $P$ is the same as ranging over $k\in K_i^\delta(\sigma)$, a product of compact intervals; continuous functions attain their extrema on it.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:nesting}]
For a finite $\cP$ and the compact convex box $K$ with continuous affine payoffs: \emph{E $\subseteq$ M.} If $p$ is optimal at $k_0$, then for every $p'$, $\ub(p';k_0)-\ub(p;k_0)\le0$, so $\min_k[\ub(p')-\ub(p)]\le0$. \emph{M $\subseteq$ ID.} If $p$ is maximal, then for each $p'$ there is $k$ with $\ub(p;k)\ge\ub(p';k)$; hence $\max\ub(p;\cdot)\ge\ub(p;k)\ge\ub(p';k)\ge\min\ub(p';\cdot)$. \emph{$\Gamma\subseteq$ M.} If $\min_k[\ub(p';k)-\ub(p;k)]>0$, then $\min\ub(p')>\min\ub(p)$, so $p$ is not $\Gamma$-maximin. These inclusions hold carrier by carrier and give the inclusions between equilibrium sets. \emph{Monotonicity.} $K^\delta(c)\subseteq K^{\delta'}(c)$ for every $c$, and the realised centre $A_{-i}(\sigma)$ does not depend on $\delta$. E: a witness belief stays admissible. M: a dominance strict on the larger set is strict on the smaller one, so non-domination on the smaller set implies it on the larger one. ID: $\overline\E$ increases and every $\underline\E$ decreases as the set grows.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[$\Gamma$-equilibria are not nested]\label{ex:gamma}
Take one carrier with a fixed external aggregate $c=(1,2)$, $d=2$, $M=I$, $w=(2,1)$, no traffic term, and two plans with $\pi=0$, $\varphi(A)=(1,0)$, $\varphi(B)=(0,1)$. The lower values are $-(1+2\delta)$ for $A$ and $-(2+\delta)$ for $B$, so $\ICNE_\Gamma(0)=\{A\}$ and $\ICNE_\Gamma(2)=\{B\}$.
\end{example}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:gamma}]
A minimum of affine functions of the mixture $x$ is concave, and \[\min_k\textstyle\sum_px(p)\ub_i(p;k)\ge\sum_px(p)\min_k\ub_i(p;k)\] because the left side picks one $k$ for all $p$. With $\varphi,t\ge0$ and $M\ge0$, $\ub_i(p;m,\xi)$ is coordinatewise nonincreasing in $(m,\xi)$. The box $K^\delta(c)$ has the coordinatewise maximum $c+\delta w$, which lies in it (clipping only removes points below $0$), and $\Xi$ has the maximum $\overline\mu$. So $k^+=(c+\delta w,\overline\mu)$ minimises every $\ub_i(p;\cdot)$ and the lower expectation is linear in the mixture. Substituting $k^+$ with $c=A_{-i}(\sigma)$ gives \[\ub_i(p;k^+)=\pi^\Gamma_i(p)-\ip{\varphi_i(p)}{MA_{-i}(\sigma)},\] the payoff of $G^\Gamma_\delta$; $\Gamma$-maximin is then best response in $G^\Gamma_\delta$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Relaxed equilibria are mixed equilibria]\label{prop:relaxed}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:affine}, $x$ is a mixed Nash equilibrium of the finite game with payoffs $\ub_i(\sigma_i;A_{-i}(\sigma),\hat\xi)$ if and only if $\supp x_i\subseteq\arg\max_p\ub_i(p;\Abar_{-i}(x),\hat\xi)$ for all $i$. Such $x$ exist.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Under the product law $x_{-i}$, the expected payoff of a pure plan $p$ is $\E[\ub_i(p;A_{-i},\hat\xi)]=\ub_i(p;\E A_{-i},\hat\xi)$ because $\ub_i$ is affine in $A_{-i}$, and $\E A_{-i}=\Abar_{-i}(x)$ by linearity. A mixed profile is a Nash equilibrium if and only if each $x_i$ is supported on pure best responses to $x_{-i}$. Existence follows from \citet{Nash1951}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:D1}]
For a unilateral change $p\to p'$ of carrier $i$, put $a=a_i(p)$, $a'=a_i(p')$ and $R=A_{-i}$. Then $\Psi(p')-\Psi(p)=\pi_i(p')-\pi_i(p)+\tfrac12\|a'\|_M^2-\tfrac12\|a\|^2_M-\tfrac12\|R+a'\|_M^2+\tfrac12\|R+a\|_M^2-\gamma\ip{t_i(p')-t_i(p)}{\hat\xi}$, which equals $\pi_i(p')-\pi_i(p)-\ip{a'-a}{MR}-\gamma\ip{t_i(p')-t_i(p)}{\hat\xi}=u_i(p')-u_i(p)$, using $\varphi_i=a_i$. A maximiser $\sigma$ is a pure equilibrium \citep{Monderer1996}. The point $(A_{-i}(\sigma),\hat\xi)$ lies in $K_i^\delta(\sigma)$ for every $\delta$, so $\sigma\in\ICNE_E(\delta)$. For $\Gamma$, Lemma~\ref{lem:gamma} shows that $\ICNE_\Gamma(\delta)$ is the equilibrium set of $G^\Gamma_\delta$, which is again D1 with modified private profits and therefore has a potential and a pure equilibrium.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
\emph{(a)} Fix $i$ and $\delta\ge\hat\delta(p)$. The anchor $\Abar^*_{-i}$ is a sum of convex combinations of nonnegative vectors, hence nonnegative. By definition of $\hat\delta(p)$, $|\Abar^*_{-i,k}-A_{-i,k}(p)|\le\hat\delta(p)w_k\le\delta w_k$ for all $k$, so $(\Abar^*_{-i},\hat\xi)\in K_i^\delta(p)$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:reduction}(b) the Dirac law at this point belongs to $\cM_i^\delta(p)$; under it the expected payoff is $\ub_i(\cdot;\Abar^*_{-i},\hat\xi)$, which $p_i\in B_i$ maximises. Hence $p_i$ is E-admissible for every $i$, and the inclusions follow from Lemma~\ref{lem:nesting}.

\emph{(b), first term.} For any $p\in\prod_jB_j$, $A_{-i}(p)-\Abar^*_{-i}=\sum_{j\ne i}(a_j(p_j)-\abar_j)$ and $|a_{jk}(p_j)-\abar_{jk}|\le r_{jk}$.

\emph{(b), second term.} $\Abar^*\in\sum_j\conv a_j(B_j)$ because $\supp x_j^*\subseteq B_j$. Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:sf} with $\cP_j$ replaced by $B_j$: a vertex $\lambda$ of $\Lambda(\Abar^*)$ exists and can be found by linear programming. Let $J=J(\lambda)$, $|J|\le k^\sharp$, and $y_j=\sum_p\lambda_j(p)a_j(p)$. For $j\notin J$, $\lambda_j$ is a point mass at some $p_j\in B_j$ and $a_j(p_j)=y_j$; for $j\in J$ choose any $p_j\in\supp\lambda_j\subseteq B_j$. Since $\sum_jy_j=\Abar^*=\sum_j\abar_j$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:decomp}
A_{-i}(p)-\Abar^*_{-i}=\textstyle\sum_{j\in J}(a_j(p_j)-y_j)-(a_i(p_i)-\abar_i).
\end{equation}
For $j\in J$ both $a_{jk}(p_j)$ and $y_{jk}$ lie in $[\min_{B_j}a_{jk},\max_{B_j}a_{jk}]$, so the sum is at most $k^\sharp\bar\sigma w_k$ in coordinate $k$, and the last term is at most $\bar\rho w_k$.

\emph{(b), third term.} If $J=\emptyset$ or $\bar\sigma=0$, the sum vanishes. Otherwise draw $p_j\sim\lambda_j$ independently for $j\in J$. For each $k$ the variables $(a_{jk}(p_j)-y_{jk})/w_k$, $j\in J$, are independent, have mean zero and range in an interval of length at most $\bar\sigma$. By Hoeffding's inequality and a union bound over $k$, $\Pr(\|E\|_{w,\infty}\ge t)\le2d\exp(-2t^2/(k^\sharp\bar\sigma^2))$, where $E$ is the sum in \eqref{eq:decomp}. This is below one for $t>t_0=\bar\sigma\sqrt{k^\sharp\ln(2d)/2}$; outcomes are finitely many, so some outcome has $\|E\|_{w,\infty}\le t_0$, and $\hat\delta(p)\le t_0+\bar\rho$ for it.

\emph{(c)} Relaxed equilibria exist (Proposition~\ref{prop:relaxed}). Use $r_{jk}\le s_{jk}$, $\bar\rho\le\bar\sigma\le\sigma_{\max}$, $k^\sharp\le d$ and $\sum_{j\ne i}r_{jk}/w_k\le(N-1)\sigma_{\max}$; the order of thresholds follows from Lemma~\ref{lem:nesting}. \emph{(d)} This was shown in (b); linear programming finds a vertex in time polynomial in $\sum_j|B_j|$ and $d$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:eps}]
Let $m^*=\Abar^*_{-i}$ and $m=A_{-i}(p)$. For any $p'$, $\ub_i(p';m,\mu)-\ub_i(p_i;m,\mu)=[\ub_i(p';m^*,\hat\xi)-\ub_i(p_i;m^*,\hat\xi)]-\ip{\varphi_i(p')-\varphi_i(p_i)}{M(m-m^*)}-\gamma\ip{t_i(p')-t_i(p_i)}{\mu-\hat\xi}$. The bracket is nonpositive because $p_i\in B_i$; each term $|M_k(\varphi_{ik}(p')-\varphi_{ik}(p_i))(m_k-m_k^*)|$ is at most $w_kM_k|\Delta\varphi_{ik}|\hat\delta(p)$, and $|\mu_z-\hat\xi_z|\le\varpi_z$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:tight}]
For carrier 1, $\ub(A_1;n)-\ub(B_1;n)=6-3n$, so $A_1$ is optimal iff $n\le2$; for carrier 2, $\ub(Y;n)-\ub(X;n)=6-3n$, so $Y$ is optimal iff $n\le2$. With two plans and a convex belief set, E-admissibility and maximality coincide: some $n$ in the box makes the plan weakly better. In $(A_1,X)$ carrier 2 sees $n=1$ and needs a belief $\ge2$; in $(A_1,Y)$ carrier 1 sees $n=3$ and needs a belief $\le2$; in $(B_1,Y)$ carrier 2 sees $n=3$ and needs $\le2$; in $(B_1,X)$ carrier 1 sees $n=1$ and needs $\ge2$. Each requirement holds iff $\delta w\ge1$, and the other carrier best-responds at the realised count. Hence $\ICNE_E(\delta)=\ICNE_M(\delta)=\emptyset$ for $\delta<1/w$ and both equal the full profile set at $\delta=1/w$; there is no pure Nash equilibrium. The unique mixed equilibrium makes each carrier indifferent, so the rival's mean count is $2$, both mix $\tfrac12$--$\tfrac12$, $r_j=1$, $\bar\rho=1/w$ and $(N-1)\bar\rho=1/w$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:cycles}]
Take two carriers with plan sets $\{A_1,B_1\}^Z$ and $\{X,Y\}^Z$, the one-zone data of Proposition~\ref{prop:tight} in each zone and no cross-zone interaction, and $N-2$ carriers with a single null plan. Payoffs are sums over zones and plan sets are products, so a carrier's regret is the sum of its zone regrets. In each zone and each of the four zone profiles, exactly one of the two carriers has regret $3$ and the other $0$ (e.g.\ in $(A_1,X)$ carrier 2 gains $2-(-1)=3$ by switching to $Y$). So $\varepsilon_1+\varepsilon_2=3Z$ with both multiples of $3$, and $\max\ge3\lceil Z/2\rceil$. For the upper bound, the zone-wise mixed equilibrium has $\bar\rho=1$, every $p\in\prod B_j$ has $\hat\delta(p)\le1$, and $L_i(p)\le\sum_z1\cdot1\cdot|\Delta\tau_z|=3Z$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:np}]
\emph{Membership.} The witness is a profile $\sigma$. With $K$ the clipped box and $\Xi$ a point: E-admissibility of $\sigma_i$ is feasibility of the linear system $\{m\in K:\ub_i(\sigma_i;m)\ge\ub_i(p';m)\ \forall p'\}$; maximality requires $\max_{m\in K}[\ub_i(\sigma_i;m)-\ub_i(p';m)]\ge0$ for each $p'$, a linear function maximised over a box coordinatewise; ID and $\Gamma$ require maxima and minima of $\ub_i(p;\cdot)$ over the box, also coordinatewise.

\emph{Construction.} Let $V(D)=[n]$ and $\Delta^-=\max(1,\text{max in-degree})$. Create one zone per arc $e=(u,v)$, using only its vehicle coordinate, with $M_e=\gamma c_e=1$, $w_e=1$ and no deliveries. Set $\eta=1/(2\Delta^-+2)$. Carrier $i$ has the plan \emph{out} with all data $0$, and the plan \emph{in} with $\pi_i=1$ and, for each out-arc $e=(i,j)$, vehicles $n_{ie}=1$ and exposure $\tau_{ie}=1$; for each in-arc $e=(h,i)$, vehicles $n_{ie}=2$ and exposure $\tau_{ie}=\eta$; zero elsewhere. This is D2, since $\varphi=\tau\ne a=n$.

\emph{Payoff gap.} The gain of \emph{in} at belief $m$ is $D_i(m)=1-\sum_{e=(i,j)}m_e-\eta\sum_{e=(h,i)}m_e$. At the realised aggregate of $\sigma^S$, $m_e=2\cdot\mathbf 1[j\in S]$ for $e=(i,j)$ and $m_e=\mathbf 1[h\in S]$ for $e=(h,i)$, so $D_i^{\rm real}=1-2o_i-\eta h_i$ with $o_i=|N^+(i)\cap S|$ and $h_i=|N^-(i)\cap S|\le\Delta^-$. If $o_i=0$, $D_i^{\rm real}\ge1-\Delta^-/(2\Delta^-+2)>\tfrac12$; if $o_i\ge1$, $D_i^{\rm real}\le-1$.

\emph{Robustness of the sign.} Over $K^\delta$, $|D_i(m)-D_i^{\rm real}|\le\delta(\deg^+(i)+\eta\deg^-(i))<\delta(\Delta^++\tfrac12)$, which is below $\tfrac12$ for $\delta<1/(2\Delta^++1)$; clipping only shrinks the box. So $D_i$ has the sign of $D_i^{\rm real}$ on the whole box.

\emph{All criteria select the realised strict best response.} If $D_i>0$ on the box, \emph{in} is the unique optimum at every belief, hence the only E-admissible plan; \emph{out} is strictly dominated everywhere, so \emph{in} is the only maximal plan; and $\min_K\ub(\text{in})>0=\max_K\ub(\text{out})$, so \emph{out} is interval-dominated and \emph{in} is the unique $\Gamma$-maximin plan. The case $D_i<0$ is symmetric. So $\sigma$ is admissible under every criterion iff each carrier plays \emph{in} exactly when $o_i=0$.

\emph{Equilibria are kernels.} With $S=\{i:\sigma_i=\text{in}\}$, the condition reads $i\in S\Rightarrow N^+(i)\cap S=\emptyset$ (independence) and $i\notin S\Rightarrow N^+(i)\cap S\ne\emptyset$ (absorption). NP-hardness follows from \citet{Chvatal1973}; all numbers are polynomially bounded, so hardness is strong; with $\Delta^+\le2$ \citep{Fraenkel1981} the threshold is $1/5$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:inapprox}]
On the instances of Theorem~\ref{thm:np}, $\delta_0^E=0$ if $D$ has a kernel; otherwise $\ICNE_E(\delta)=\emptyset$ for all $\delta<1/(2\Delta^++1)$, so $\delta_0^E\ge1/(2\Delta^++1)$. A multiplicative approximation outputs $0$ in the first case and a positive number in the second; an additive one separates the cases when its error is below $1/(2\Delta^++1)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[The relaxation step is PPAD-hard under D2]\label{prop:ppad}
Every bimatrix game $(R,C)\in\mathbb Q^{m\times n}\times\mathbb Q^{m\times n}$ is, in polynomial time, the precise game of a D2 instance with $N=2$ and $d=m+n$. Hence computing a mixed equilibrium under D2 is PPAD-hard.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $M=I_{m+n}$, no traffic term, $\bar R=\max R$ and $\bar C=\max C$. Carrier 1 has plans $p\in[m]$ with $a_1(p)=e_p$, $\varphi_1(p)=\sum_q(\bar R-R_{pq})e_{m+q}\ge0$ and $\pi_1(p)=\bar R$; carrier 2 has plans $q\in[n]$ with $a_2(q)=e_{m+q}$, $\varphi_2(q)=\sum_p(\bar C-C_{pq})e_p\ge0$ and $\pi_2(q)=\bar C$. Then $u_1(p;a_2(q))=R_{pq}$ and $u_2=C_{pq}$. By Proposition~\ref{prop:relaxed}, relaxed equilibria are exactly the Nash equilibria of $(R,C)$, and computing these is PPAD-complete \citep{Chen2009,Daskalakis2009}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:transfer}]
Write $k^t=(A_{-i}(\sigma),\mu)\in K_i^\delta(\sigma)$ and $\Delta(p',k)=\ub_i(p';k)-\ub_i(\sigma_i;k)$, affine in $k$. For any $k$ in the (unclipped) box, $|\Delta(p',k)-\Delta(p',k^t)|\le\delta\sum_kw_kM_k|\Delta\varphi_{ik}|+\gamma\sum_z\varpi_z|\Delta t_{iz}|$. \emph{E:} take the witness $k$, at which $\Delta(p',k)\le0$. \emph{M:} for each $p'$, maximality and compactness give $k_{p'}$ with $\Delta(p',k_{p'})\le0$. \emph{ID:} $\ub_i(p';k^t)-\ub_i(\sigma_i;k^t)\le[\ub_i(p';k^t)-\min_K\ub_i(p')]+[\max_K\ub_i(\sigma_i)-\ub_i(\sigma_i;k^t)]$, since $\max_K\ub_i(\sigma_i)\ge\min_K\ub_i(p')$, and each bracket is at most $\delta H_i+\gamma\Theta_i$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:cs}]
The minimum over a growing finite set does not increase and the maximum does not decrease (Lemma~\ref{lem:nesting}). Each profile enters $\ICNE_C(\delta)$ at a single threshold and never leaves, so the sets change at most at $\prod_i|\cP_i|$ values. For $\Gamma$ see Example~\ref{ex:gamma}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Price of anarchy under smoothness; conditional]\label{prop:smooth}
Assume $W(\sigma)\ge\sum_iU_i(\sigma)$ for all $\sigma$, $W^*>0$, and that the precise game is $(\lambda,\mu)$-smooth: $\sum_iU_i(\sigma^*_i,\sigma_{-i})\ge\lambda W(\sigma^*)-\mu W(\sigma)$ for all $\sigma$ and an optimal $\sigma^*$. For $C\in\{E,M,ID\}$ let $\mathcal E(\delta)=\max_{\sigma\in\ICNE_C(\delta)}\sum_i\varepsilon_i(\sigma)$ from Lemma~\ref{lem:transfer}. If $\lambda W^*>\mathcal E(\delta)$, then $\mathrm{PoA}_C(\delta)\le(1+\mu)/(\lambda-\mathcal E(\delta)/W^*)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For $\sigma\in\ICNE_C(\delta)$: $W(\sigma)\ge\sum_iU_i(\sigma)\ge\sum_i[U_i(\sigma^*_i,\sigma_{-i})-\varepsilon_i]\ge\lambda W^*-\mu W(\sigma)-\mathcal E(\delta)$. Rearrange.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:pos}]
With $\pi_i$ as in Section~\ref{sec:model} and $x=\tau$, direct expansion gives $W-\Psi_{\hat\mu}=\sum_z[\tfrac{b_z}{2}\sum_iq_{iz}^2-\tfrac{\gamma c_z}{2}(T_z^2-\sum_i\tau_{iz}^2)]$; the $\alpha$, $\hat\mu$ and $C_i$ terms cancel. By Cauchy--Schwarz, $\sum_i\tau_{iz}^2\ge T_z^2/N$, and $\sum q^2\ge0$, so $W(\sigma)\ge\Psi_{\hat\mu}(\sigma)-\sum_z\tfrac{\gamma c_z}{2}(1-\tfrac1N)T_z(\sigma)^2$. Next, $\Psi_{\hat\mu}(\sigma)\ge\Psi_{\hat\mu}(\sigma^*)-\eta$, and $\Psi_{\hat\mu}(\sigma^*)\ge W^*-\sum_z\tfrac{b_z}{2}\sum_iq_{iz}^{*2}$ because $T^2\ge\sum\tau^2$ for $\tau\ge0$. Membership of a potential maximiser in every $\ICNE_E(\delta)$ is Proposition~\ref{prop:D1}, extended to M and ID by Lemma~\ref{lem:nesting}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Exact thresholds and regret brackets]\label{thm:cert}
Let $p$ be a pure profile. (a) The exact E-threshold of carrier $i$ is the optimal value of the linear program
$A_i^E(p)=\min\{\delta\ge0: m\ge0,\ \xi\in\Xi,\ |m_k-A_{-i,k}(p)|\le\delta w_k\ \forall k,\ \ub_i(p_i;m,\xi)\ge\ub_i(p';m,\xi)\ \forall p'\in\cP_i\}$ ($+\infty$ if infeasible), and $p\in\ICNE_E(\delta)$ iff $\delta\ge\max_iA^E_i(p)$. With an exact oracle and implicit $\cP_i$, Kelley's cutting-plane method on this program terminates finitely. (b) For anchors $m^i\ge0$, $\xi^i\in\Xi$ with $p_i\in\arg\max\ub_i(\cdot;m^i,\xi^i)$, $A_i^E(p)\le\|A_{-i}(p)-m^i\|_{w,\infty}$. (c) For a heuristic plan $h_i$ and a valid bound $\mathcal U_i\ge\max_{p'}\ub_i(p';m,\xi)$, the regret of $p_i$ lies in $[\ub_i(h_i)-\ub_i(p_i),\ \mathcal U_i-\ub_i(p_i)]$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
(a) By Lemma~\ref{lem:reduction}, $p_i$ is E-admissible at width $\delta$ iff the system with that $\delta$ is feasible; feasibility is upward closed in $\delta$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:nesting}) and the infimum of an LP is attained when finite. Kelley's method adds the oracle's best plan at the current $(m,\xi)$ as a violated constraint; a plan already present cannot be violated, so cuts are distinct and finitely many. (b) The anchor is feasible with $\delta=\|A_{-i}(p)-m^i\|_{w,\infty}$. (c) Immediate from the definitions.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Rounding guarantee under D2]\label{thm:D2}
Under D2, if the relaxation phase returns a mixed equilibrium $x$, the sparsify phase (a feasibility LP over $\Lambda(\Abar(x))$ with plan sets $\supp x_j$) and the round phase return $p\in\prod_jB_j(x)$ with $p\in\ICNE_E(\delta)$ for all $\delta\ge\hat\delta(p)$; $\hat\delta(p)$ satisfies the second bound of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}(b) for any choice in the round phase and the third for a successful sample. Sparsify and round run in time polynomial in $\sum_j|\supp x_j|$ and $d$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The sparsify LP's vertices have $|J|\le k^\sharp$ by Theorem~\ref{thm:sf}. The rest is Theorem~\ref{thm:main}(a), (b) and (d).
\end{proof}

The double-oracle relaxation \citep{McMahan2003} stops, with exact restricted solves and exact oracles, after at most $\sum_i|\cP_i|$ rounds at a relaxed equilibrium: at termination every support plan attains the restricted maximum and no plan exceeds it, and each non-terminal round adds a new plan.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:brl-main}]
Write $S=\sum_{a\in r}X_a$ (arcs counted with multiplicity). Fix $j$ and $\Theta=\Theta_j$.

\emph{Step 1 (pointwise inequality).} For reals $x_a$, $d_a$ with $\sum_ad_a\le\Theta$, $\pos{\sum_ax_a-\Theta}\le\pos{\sum_a(x_a-d_a)}\le\sum_a\pos{x_a-d_a}$, because $t\mapsto t^+$ is nondecreasing and subadditive.

\emph{Step 2 (upper bound).} Let $P\in\cM$ with marginals $F_a$. Taking expectations, $\E_P\pos{S-\Theta}\le\sum_a\E_{F_a}\pos{X_a-d_a}$. Since $F_a\ge\Fl_a$ pointwise, $F_a$ is first-order dominated by $\Fl_a$, and $x\mapsto\pos{x-d}$ is nondecreasing, so $\E_{F_a}\pos{X_a-d_a}\le\Pib_a(d_a)$; likewise $\E_{F_a}X_a\le\xb_a$. Hence $\E_P\phi(S)\le c_0+\gamma\sum_a\xb_a+\sum_j\rho_j\sum_a\Pib_a(d^j_a)$ for every $P$ and admissible $d^j$.

\emph{Step 3 (attainment).} Let $U$ be uniform on $(0,1)$, $\bar X_a=\Qb_a(U)$ and $\bar S=\Sigma(U)$ with $\Sigma(u)=\sum_a\Qb_a(u)$; then $\bar X_a\sim\Fl_a$ and the law of $(\bar X_a)$ belongs to $\cM$. Let $u^*=\sup\{u\in(0,1]:\Sigma(u)\le\Theta\}$ ($\sup\emptyset=0$). If $u^*=1$, put $d_a=\Qb_a(1)$; then $\sum_ad_a\le\Theta$ and both sides of Step 1 vanish almost surely. If $u^*<1$, put $\ell_a=\Qb_a(u^*)$ (or $\Qb_a(0^+)$ if $u^*=0$) and $h_a=\Qb_a(u^{*+})$; then $\sum_a\ell_a\le\Theta$ when $u^*>0$ and $\sum_ah_a\ge\Theta$. If $u^*=0$ choose $d_a\le\ell_a$ with $\sum_ad_a=\Theta$; otherwise choose $d_a\in[\ell_a,h_a]$ with $\sum_ad_a=\Theta$. On $\{U\le u^*\}$ all $\bar X_a\le d_a$ and both sides of Step 1 are $0$; on $\{U>u^*\}$ all $\bar X_a\ge d_a$ and $\sum_a\pos{\bar X_a-d_a}=\bar S-\Theta=\pos{\bar S-\Theta}$. So Step 1 holds with equality almost surely, and the comonotone law attains the bound of Step 2; the minima are attained.

\emph{Step 4.} $\E\phi(\bar S)=\int_0^1\phi(\Sigma(u))du$ because $\bar S=\Sigma(U)$.

\emph{Step 5 (integrality).} With integer quantiles and $\Theta$, the numbers $\ell_a$, $h_a$, $\Qb_a(1)$ are integers and integer vectors with the required properties exist in each case of Step 3.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:resource}]
Substitute the budget form of Theorem~\ref{thm:brl-main} into the objective and merge the two minimisations.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Concatenation is inf-convolution]\label{lem:infconv}
For a label $p$ and a completion $q$, $\Eu[\phi(T_{p\oplus q})]-c_0=f_{p\oplus q}(T_{\max})=\min_D\{f_p(D)+f_q(T_{\max}-D)\}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The stop-loss terms are separable over arcs. Split the budget vector into the arcs of $p$ and of $q$, put $D=\sum_{a\in p}d_a$, minimise first over the arcs of $p$ with budget $D$ and then over those of $q$ with budget $T_{\max}-D$, and apply Theorem~\ref{thm:brl-main} to each part. The mean terms add.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Completion window]\label{lem:window}
Let $C_{\min}(v)\le S_{q,1}$ for every completion $q$ of labels ending at $v$. For every completion $q$, the minimum in Lemma~\ref{lem:infconv} is attained at some $D^*\le\Dh(p)$; if $D^*<\Dl(p)$ then $D^*\le S_{p,1}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $\Phi_q(D)=f_p(D)+f_q(T_{\max}-D)$. The map $D\mapsto f_q(T_{\max}-D)$ is convex and nondecreasing with slope in $[0,\rho]$, equal to $\rho$ when $T_{\max}-D<S_{q,1}$; the slope of $f_p$ lies in $[-\rho,0]$ and is $0$ for $D>S_{p,L}$. (i) On $D>T_{\max}-S_{q,1}$, $\Phi_q$ has slope $\ge0$, so a minimiser exists with $D\le T_{\max}-S_{q,1}\le T_{\max}-C_{\min}(v)$. (ii) On $D>S_{p,L}$, $\Phi_q$ has slope $\ge0$, so decreasing $D$ to $S_{p,L}$ does not increase it. Applying (i) and then (ii) gives $D^*\le\Dh(p)$. The last claim is immediate from $\Dl(p)\le S_{p,1}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:fdom}]
\emph{Validity.} Let $q$ be a completion of $p_2$; since $\mathcal V(p_1)\subseteq\mathcal V(p_2)$ and $\ell(p_1)\le\ell(p_2)$, it is a completion of $p_1$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:window} there is a minimiser $D^*\le\Dh(p_2)$ for $p_2\oplus q$. Let $g$ be the difference in Definition~\ref{def:fdom}. If $D^*\in\mathcal W(p_2)$, $g(D^*)\le0$. Otherwise $D^*<\Dl(p_2)\le S_{p_2,1}$; on $(-\infty,S_{p_2,1}]$, $f_{p_2}$ has slope $-\rho$ and $f_{p_1}$ slope $\ge-\rho$, so $g$ is nondecreasing there and $g(D^*)\le g(\Dl(p_2))\le0$. In both cases, by Lemma~\ref{lem:infconv}, $f_{p_1\oplus q}(T_{\max})-y(p_1)\le f_{p_1}(D^*)+f_q(T_{\max}-D^*)-y(p_1)\le f_{p_2\oplus q}(T_{\max})-y(p_2)$.

\emph{Exactness.} Call a label processed if it is popped while not deleted. Let OPT be the optimum and $\Omega$ the set of pairs $(o,q)$ with $o$ a created label, $q$ a completion of $o$ and reduced cost of $o\oplus q$ equal to OPT. The pair (depot label, optimal route) is in $\Omega$ and the depot label is processed. Among pairs in $\Omega$ with $o$ processed choose one with the fewest arcs $|q|$. If $|q|=1$, closing $o$ records a route of value OPT. If $|q|\ge2$, let $(v,j)$ be the first arc of $q$, $q'$ the rest and $o_1=o\oplus(v,j)$, which is created when $o$ is processed; $(o_1,q')\in\Omega$. If $o_1$ is processed, $|q'|<|q|$ contradicts minimality. Otherwise $o_1$ was discarded or deleted because a live label $o_2$ functionally dominates it; by validity $(o_2,q')\in\Omega$, and if $o_2$ is not processed it was deleted later by some $o_3$, with $(o_3,q')\in\Omega$ by transitivity. The moments at which these labels cease to be live strictly increase and labels are finitely many, so the chain ends at a processed label with $(o_k,q')\in\Omega$ and $|q'|<|q|$, a contradiction.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Comparison with vector dominance]\label{prop:compare}
Assume $\mathcal V(p_1)\subseteq\mathcal V(p_2)$, $\ell(p_1)\le\ell(p_2)$ and $y(p_1)\ge y(p_2)$. (a) If $S_{p_1}\le S_{p_2}$ componentwise, $p_1$ functionally dominates $p_2$. (b) In the sector model $X_a=\sum_k\tau_{a,k}\xi_k$ with probability boxes on the multipliers, $\tau_{p_1}\le\tau_{p_2}$ componentwise implies $S_{p_1}\le S_{p_2}$. (c) Neither converse holds.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
(a) $f$ is nondecreasing in each $S_l$. (b) The upper quantiles of the multipliers are nonnegative. (c) With $L=2$, $\omega=(\tfrac12,\tfrac12)$, $\gamma=0$, $\rho=1$ and equal duals: $S_{p_1}=(1,1)$ and $S_{p_2}=(0,2.2)$ give $f_{p_1}\le f_{p_2}$ on $\R$ (values $(1,1.1)$, $(0,0.6)$, $(0,0)$ at $D=0,1,2.2$, constant difference $-0.1$ below $0$) although $S_{p_1,1}>S_{p_2,1}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[A single risk level is not exact]\label{prop:fixedu}
For every $u$, $\min_{\rm plan}\sum_r\Eu[\phi(T_r)]\ge\max_u\min_{\rm plan}\sum_r[c_0+\gamma\xb_r+\rho(\mathcal H_r(u)-(1-u)T_{\max})]$, where $\mathcal H_r(u)=\int_u^1\sum_{a\in r}\Qb_a(v)\,dv$; the inequality can be strict and the best fixed-$u$ plan need not be optimal.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The maximum over $u$ inside each route is at least its value at a common $u$, and $\min\max\ge\max\min$. Strictness is shown by exact computations by enumeration and set partitioning (Section~\ref{sec:res-brl}); structurally, the optimal level of a route is its worst-case on-time level, which differs between routes of the same plan.
\end{proof}

\section{Zone-market clearing (ZMC)}\label{app:zmc}

ZMC is the first phase of the equilibrium engine (Section~\ref{sec:engine}). It is stated here with its guarantees and proofs; Remark~\ref{rem:zmc} gives the negative empirical result.

ZMC targets the D1 game in cost form. Let $c_i(p)=-\pi_i(p)+\gamma\ip{t_i(p)}{\hat\xi}$ (charges included), $\tilde c_i=c_i-\tfrac12\|a_i\|_M^2$, and assume $M_k>0$ on the active coordinates. Then $\Phi(\sigma)=\sum_i\tilde c_i(\sigma_i)+\tfrac12\|A(\sigma)\|_M^2=-\Psi_{\hat\xi}(\sigma)$ is an exact potential (Proposition~\ref{prop:D1}). For a common price $\lambda\in\R^d$, the pricing oracle returns $O_i(\lambda)=\arg\min_p\{\tilde c_i(p)+\ip{\lambda}{a_i(p)}\}$, which is the oracle of Assumption~\ref{ass:oracle} with $Q=M$. Let $V_i(\lambda)$ be its value, $\mathcal D(\lambda)=\sum_iV_i(\lambda)-\tfrac12\|\lambda\|^2_{M^{-1}}$ the price dual of the convexified potential, $\lambda^*$ its unique maximiser, and $\bar\Phi^*=\max\mathcal D\le\min\Phi$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:dual}).

\begin{theorem}[Clearing identity]\label{thm:clear}
Let $\sigma_i\in O_i(\lambda)$ for all $i$ and let $e=M^{-1}\lambda-A(\sigma)$ be the clearing error (supply minus demand at price $\lambda$). Then
(a) $\Phi(\sigma)-\mathcal D(\lambda)=\tfrac12\|e\|_M^2$, so $\Phi(\sigma)-\min\Phi\le\tfrac12\|e\|_M^2$;
(b) carrier $i$'s Nash regret is at most $\tfrac12\sum_{k\in\mathcal Z_i}M_ke_k^2$, where $\mathcal Z_i$ is the set of coordinates in which its plans differ;
(c) $\sigma_i$ is an exact best response to the belief $m_i=M^{-1}\lambda-a_i(\sigma_i)=A_{-i}(\sigma)+e$, hence E-admissible at every width $\delta\ge\max_{k\in\mathcal Z_i}|e_k|/w_k$ (if $m_{ik}\ge0$ on $\mathcal Z_i$);
(d) $e=0$ implies that $\sigma$ minimises $\Phi$ and is a pure Nash equilibrium;
(e) conversely, exact clearing is attainable if and only if the convexification of the potential is tight at a pure profile.
\end{theorem}

Part (a) is Fenchel--Young equality and a standard duality-gap fact. The game-specific content is in (b) and (c): the own-contribution term of the absorption bound cancels, because under D1 the own quadratic term turns a pricing-optimal plan into a best response to the anchor $m_i$. Finding a good pure equilibrium thus becomes a price search: find one $\lambda$ at which the carriers' pricing answers nearly clear every zone. Because $\mathcal D$ is concave, the aggregate response is antitone in $\lambda$, and along each coordinate the sign of the residual $s_k=A_k(\lambda)-\lambda_k/M_k$ localises $\lambda^*_k$ up to $M_k\chi_k$, where $\chi_k$ is the cross-zone variation: how far other coordinates' prices can move the response in coordinate $k$ at a fixed own price.

\begin{theorem}[Bracket invariant and dimension-free rate]\label{thm:bracket}
Start from brackets $[0,M_k\Amax_k]$ and, after each round, move the lower (upper) end of bracket $k$ to $\lambda_k$ when $s_k>0$ ($s_k<0$). Then $\operatorname{dist}(\lambda^*_k,[l_k,u_k])\le M_k\chi_k$ after every round. If the bracket midpoint is queried at least every other round, after $T$ rounds ($NT$ oracle calls, independent of $d$) every point of the bracket satisfies $|\lambda_k-\lambda^*_k|\le M_k\Amax_k2^{-\lceil T/2\rceil}+M_k\chi_k$ for all $k$. If plan sets are separable across coordinates, $\chi=0$.
\end{theorem}

The floor $M_k\chi_k$ is genuine when zones are complementary (Example~\ref{ex:kappa}). Algorithm~\ref{alg:zmc} alternates midpoint steps (for the rate) with secant steps on isotonic fits of each coordinate's response (for speed), then rounds over the collected plans and finishes with best-response polish.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{ZMC: zone-market clearing for pure equilibria of the D1 game}\label{alg:zmc}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require pricing and best-response oracles; rounds $T$
\State $l\gets0$, $u\gets M\Amax$, $\lambda\gets(l+u)/2$; plan pools $\mathcal Q_i\gets\emptyset$
\For{$t=0,\dots,T-1$}
 \State for all $i$ in parallel: $\sigma^t_i\gets O_i(\lambda)$; $\mathcal Q_i\gets\mathcal Q_i\cup\{\sigma^t_i\}$ \Comment{$N$ routing solves}
 \State $s\gets A(\sigma^t)-M^{-1}\lambda$; update every bracket by the sign of $s_k$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:bracket})
 \State \textbf{if} $t$ odd: $\lambda\gets(l+u)/2$; \textbf{else} for each $k$: secant step on an isotonic fit of $(\lambda_k,A_k)$, clipped to $[l_k,u_k]$
\EndFor
\State $\sigma^{\rm price}\gets$ the pricing profile with the smallest $\tfrac12\|e^t\|_M^2$
\State $\sigma^{\rm round}\gets$ restricted master over the pools and derandomised rounding (no oracle calls)
\State $\sigma\gets$ the better of the two; sequential best responses until no carrier improves
\State \Return $\sigma$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{theorem}[Guarantee of ZMC]\label{thm:zmc}
With exact oracles, ZMC uses $NT$ pricing calls before the polish; the polish stops after finitely many best-response calls at a pure Nash equilibrium $\sigma$ with $\Phi(\sigma)\le\bar\Phi^*+\tfrac12\min_t\|e^t\|_M^2$; and $\sigma\in\ICNE_C(\delta)$ for all $\delta\ge0$ and $C\in\{E,M,ID\}$. Run on the surcharged game of Lemma~\ref{lem:gamma}, it returns an exact $\Gamma$-maximin equilibrium at width $\delta$.
\end{theorem}

Charges translate the supply line: under charges $t$ the equilibrium gross price is 1-Lipschitz in $t$ and convexified aggregates respond monotonically (Proposition~\ref{prop:charges}), so brackets and plan pools warm-start the next evaluation when a city leader searches over charges.

\paragraph{Prior art and limits} Price decomposition with a pricing oracle is classical (Dantzig--Wolfe, column generation and bundle methods; \citealp{Briant2008}); learning separable piecewise-linear concave approximations from sampled slopes is SPAR \citep{Powell2004}; Walrasian prices with indivisible goods are computed from demand oracles by \citet{PaesLeme2020}; and \citet{Duguet2025} relate the regret of price-allocation pairs to duality and integrality gaps in combinatorial markets, which is the closest conceptual neighbour of Theorem~\ref{thm:clear}(a)--(b). We claim novelty only for the own-term cancellation with credal anchors (Theorem~\ref{thm:clear}(b),(c),(e)), the sign-bracket invariant with the cross-variation floor (Theorem~\ref{thm:bracket}) and the method they define. ZMC has no D2 version, does not bound the clearing error of pure answers a priori, and spends $NT$ calls even on easy games.

\begin{lemma}[Price duality]\label{lem:dual}
Let $\bar\Phi(x)=\sum_i\sum_px_i(p)\tilde c_i(p)+\tfrac12\|\Abar(x)\|_M^2$ on $\prod_i\Delta(\cP_i)$, $\bar\Phi^*=\min\bar\Phi$ and $\mathcal B=\prod_k[0,M_k\Amax_k]$ with $\Amax_k=\sum_i\max_pa_{ik}(p)$. (a) $\mathcal D$ is strongly concave with a unique maximiser $\lambda^*$, and $\max\mathcal D=\bar\Phi^*\le\min_\sigma\Phi(\sigma)$. (b) All minimisers of $\bar\Phi$ share the aggregate $\Abar^*$, and $\lambda^*=M\Abar^*\in\mathcal B$. (c) $M^{-1}\lambda^*\in R(\lambda^*)$, where $R(\lambda)=\sd\sum_iV_i(\lambda)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
(a) $V=\sum_iV_i$ is concave and finite and $\tfrac12\|\lambda\|^2_{M^{-1}}$ strongly convex, so $\mathcal D$ is strongly concave, continuous and coercive, with a unique maximiser. For $L(x,\lambda)=\sum_i\ip{x_i}{\tilde c_i}+\ip{\lambda}{\Abar(x)}-\tfrac12\|\lambda\|^2_{M^{-1}}$, affine in $x$ on a compact convex set and concave in $\lambda$, Sion's theorem gives $\min_x\sup_\lambda L=\sup_\lambda\min_xL$. For fixed $x$ the supremum is at $\lambda=M\Abar(x)$ with value $\bar\Phi(x)$; for fixed $\lambda$, $\min_xL=\mathcal D(\lambda)$ because a linear function on a product of simplices is minimised at a vertex carrier by carrier. Pure profiles are vertices, where $\bar\Phi=\Phi$. (b) Two minimisers with different aggregates would contradict strict convexity of $\tfrac12\|\cdot\|_M^2$. A minimiser $x^*$ and $\lambda^*$ form a saddle point, and the unique maximiser of $L(x^*,\cdot)$ is $M\Abar(x^*)$. Bounds on $\Abar^*$ follow from nonnegativity. (c) This is $0\in\sd\mathcal D(\lambda^*)=R(\lambda^*)-M^{-1}\lambda^*$, by the sum rule for concave functions \citep{Rockafellar1970}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:clear}]
For any pure $\sigma$ and $\lambda$, with $A=A(\sigma)$ and $e=M^{-1}\lambda-A$,
\begin{align*}
\textstyle\sum_i[\tilde c_i(\sigma_i)+\ip{\lambda}{a_i(\sigma_i)}]-\tfrac12\|\lambda\|^2_{M^{-1}}
&=\textstyle\sum_i\tilde c_i(\sigma_i)+\ip{M(A+e)}{A}-\tfrac12\|A+e\|_M^2\\
&=\Phi(\sigma)-\tfrac12\|e\|_M^2.\tag{$\ast$}
\end{align*}
(a) If $\sigma_i\in O_i(\lambda)$ the left side is $\mathcal D(\lambda)\le\bar\Phi^*\le\min\Phi$. (b) Fix $i$, $a=a_i(\sigma_i)$, $a'=a_i(p)$, $\Delta=a'-a$ (zero outside $\mathcal Z_i$). Since $MA_{-i}=\lambda-M(a+e)$, the change in carrier $i$'s cost $c_i+\ip{a_i}{MA_{-i}}$ equals $[\tilde c_i(p)+\ip{\lambda}{a'}]-[\tilde c_i(\sigma_i)+\ip{\lambda}{a}]+\tfrac12\|\Delta\|^2_M-\ip{\Delta}{Me}\ge-\tfrac12\sum_{k\in\mathcal Z_i}M_ke_k^2$, minimising coordinatewise at $\Delta_k=e_k$. (c) For any $p$, $c_i(p)+\ip{a'}{Mm_i}=[\tilde c_i(p)+\ip{\lambda}{a'}]+\tfrac12\|a'-a\|^2_M-\tfrac12\|a\|^2_M$, minimised by $\sigma_i$. Coordinates outside $\mathcal Z_i$ shift all plans' costs equally, so the belief can be replaced by one equal to $A_{-i}$ there; within $\mathcal Z_i$ it deviates from $A_{-i}$ by $|e_k|\le\delta w_k$, and under D1 the Dirac law at it lies in the credal set (Lemma~\ref{lem:reduction}). (d) If $e=0$, $\Phi(\sigma)=\mathcal D(\lambda)\le\bar\Phi^*\le\min\Phi\le\Phi(\sigma)$; a global minimiser of an exact potential is a pure equilibrium, and uniqueness gives $\lambda=\lambda^*$. (e) If $\Phi(\sigma^\circ)=\bar\Phi^*$, Lemma~\ref{lem:dual}(b) gives $\lambda^*=MA(\sigma^\circ)$, so $e=0$, and $(\ast)$ shows that $\sum_i\{\tilde c_i(\sigma^\circ_i)+\ip{\lambda^*}{a_i(\sigma^\circ_i)}-V_i(\lambda^*)\}=0$, a sum of nonnegative terms.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:bracket}]
Let $\chi_k=\sup\{\sup_{r\in R(\lambda)}\inf_{r'\in R(\lambda')}|r_k-r'_k|:\lambda,\lambda'\in\mathcal B,\ \lambda_k=\lambda'_k\}$. \emph{Key inequality.} Let a query $\lambda\in\mathcal B$ have $\lambda_k=\mu$ and response $r\in R(\lambda)$. Suppose $s_k>0$ but $\mu-\lambda^*_k>M_k\chi_k$. Let $\lambda'$ equal $\lambda^*$ except $\lambda'_k=\mu$; then $\lambda'\in\mathcal B$. With $r^*=M^{-1}\lambda^*\in R(\lambda^*)$, antitonicity of $R$ gives $r'_k\le\lambda^*_k/M_k$ for every $r'\in R(\lambda')$, and the definition of $\chi_k$ gives $r'\in R(\lambda')$ with $r_k\le r'_k+\chi_k+\eta$ for any $\eta>0$. Hence $s_k=r_k-\mu/M_k\le-(\mu-\lambda^*_k-M_k\chi_k)/M_k+\eta<0$ for small $\eta$, a contradiction. The case $s_k<0$ is symmetric, and $s_k=0$ combines both. \emph{Induction.} Initially $\lambda^*_k\in[0,M_k\Amax_k]$. If $s_k>0$ the new bracket is $[\mu,u_k]$; if $\lambda^*_k\ge\mu$ the distance is unchanged, and otherwise it is $\mu-\lambda^*_k\le M_k\chi_k$. The other cases are symmetric. \emph{Rate.} Midpoint rounds halve every bracket and other rounds never enlarge it, so after $T$ rounds with at least $\lceil T/2\rceil$ midpoints the width is at most $M_k\Amax_k2^{-\lceil T/2\rceil}$. If plan sets are separable, $V_i$ is separable and the projection of $R(\lambda)$ on coordinate $k$ depends on $\lambda_k$ only, so $\chi_k=0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[The floor is genuine]\label{ex:kappa}
Let $d=2$, $M=I$ and one carrier with plans $0$ ($\tilde c=0$, $a=(0,0)$) and $b$ ($\tilde c=-1.5$, $a=(1,1)$). Then $\lambda^*=(0.75,0.75)$, $\chi_1=\chi_2=1$, and the queries $(0.7,0.9)$, $(0.6,1)$, $(0.5,1)$ all return plan $0$ (tie at the last resolved towards $0$), so the bracket update sets $u_1=0.5$ and excludes $\lambda^*_1$ by $0.25$, within the floor but positive.
\end{example}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:zmc}]
Each round makes one pricing call per carrier. The polish is sequential best-response dynamics in a finite exact-potential game; every accepted change strictly decreases $\Phi$, which takes finitely many values, so it stops at a pure Nash equilibrium. The polish does not increase $\Phi$, and the start has $\Phi\le\Phi(\sigma^{\rm price})=\mathcal D(\lambda^{t^\star})+\tfrac12\|e^{t^\star}\|_M^2\le\bar\Phi^*+\tfrac12\min_t\|e^t\|_M^2$ by Theorem~\ref{thm:clear}(a) and Lemma~\ref{lem:dual}(a). An exact pure equilibrium makes each plan optimal at the centre belief, which lies in every credal box; the $\Gamma$ case is Lemma~\ref{lem:gamma}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Lipschitz dependence on charges]\label{prop:charges}
With charges $t$ on the contributions, let $\mu^*(t)=\lambda^*(t)+t$. Then $\|\mu^*(t)-\mu^*(t')\|_{M^{-1}}\le\|t-t'\|_{M^{-1}}$ and $\ip{\Abar^*(t)-\Abar^*(t')}{t-t'}\le0$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
With charges, $V^t(\lambda)=V(\lambda+t)$, so the equilibrium condition reads $r=M^{-1}(\mu-t)\in R(\mu)$. For $r'=M^{-1}(\mu'-t')\in R(\mu')$, antitonicity gives $\ip{r-r'}{\mu-\mu'}\le0$, i.e.\ $\|\mu-\mu'\|^2_{M^{-1}}\le\ip{M^{-1}(t-t')}{\mu-\mu'}\le\|t-t'\|_{M^{-1}}\|\mu-\mu'\|_{M^{-1}}$. For the second claim, $\ip{r-r'}{t-t'}=\ip{r-r'}{(\mu-\mu')-M(r-r')}\le-\|r-r'\|^2_M\le0$.
\end{proof}

\section{Proofs for dual implementation pricing}\label{app:dip}

Notation as in Section~\ref{sec:dip}; $m^S_z=b_z+2\kappa_z$ is the social slope, and $q_{iz}^2=d_{iz}q_{iz}$ is used throughout. The proofs are taken from the theory notes of the repository, file\\ \texttt{theory/new\_algorithm\_C/theory.tex}.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:instr}]
(a) $\tau_{iz}=rq_{iz}$. (b) $s+rt_z=u_z$ and $t_z\ge0$ because $s\le u_z$; zones without lots carry no plan. (c) Substitution of $s_z\equiv s$ and $t_z=\vartheta g_zX^{\rm NE}_z$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:identity}]
With $q_{iz}^2=d_{iz}q_{iz}$, $X_z=rQ_z$ and $m^S_z-m_z=\kappa_z$, \[\Phi_u-\mathrm{SC}=\sum_z\big[\tfrac{m_z}{2}\textstyle\sum_id_{iz}q_{iz}-\tfrac{\kappa_z}{2}Q_z^2+u_zQ_z\big].\] Insert $u_z=\kappa_zQ^\circ_z-\tfrac{m_z}{2}\bar d_z+v_z$ and complete the square: $-\tfrac{\kappa_z}{2}Q_z^2+\kappa_zQ^\circ_zQ_z=-\tfrac{\kappa_z}{2}(Q_z-Q^\circ_z)^2+\tfrac{\kappa_z}{2}(Q^\circ_z)^2$, and $\tfrac{m_z}{2}\sum_id_{iz}q_{iz}-\tfrac{m_z}{2}\bar d_zQ_z=\sum_i\tfrac{m_z}{2}(d_{iz}-\bar d_z)q_{iz}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Carrier-specific charges; classical]\label{prop:pers}
Let $\sigma^*$ be a pure profile and $U_{iz}=\kappa_zQ^*_{-i,z}-\tfrac{b_z}{2}d_{iz}$. Under carrier-specific per-delivery charges $U_i$, for all $i$ and $p,p'$: $J^U_i(p;\sigma^*_{-i})-J^U_i(p';\sigma^*_{-i})=\mathrm{SC}(p,\sigma^*_{-i})-\mathrm{SC}(p',\sigma^*_{-i})$. Hence every profile that no single carrier can improve socially is a pure equilibrium.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$\mathrm{SC}(p,\sigma^*_{-i})=R_i(p)+\sum_zq_{iz}(-\alpha_z+m^S_zQ^*_{-i,z}+\tfrac{m^S_z}{2}d_{iz})+\text{const}$ and $J^U_i(p)=R_i(p)+\sum_zq_{iz}(-\alpha_z+m_zd_{iz}+m_zQ^*_{-i,z}+U_{iz})$; the brackets coincide because $m_z+\kappa_z=m^S_z$ and $m_z-\tfrac{b_z}{2}=\tfrac{m^S_z}{2}$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Convexified game} With mixtures $x_i\in\Delta(\mathcal P_i)$, $\bar q_i=\E_{x_i}q_i$ and $\bar Q=\sum_i\bar q_i$, the convexified potential and social cost are
$\overline\Phi_u(x)=\sum_i\E_{x_i}[R_i+\sum_zq_{iz}(-\alpha_z+u_z+\tfrac{m_z}{2}d_{iz})]+\sum_z\tfrac{m_z}{2}\bar Q_z^2$ and
$\overline{\mathrm{SC}}(x)=\sum_i\E_{x_i}[R_i-\sum_z\alpha_zq_{iz}]+\sum_z\tfrac{m^S_z}{2}\bar Q_z^2$. Both are convex, agree with $\Phi_u$ and $\mathrm{SC}$ at pure profiles, and the identity of Lemma~\ref{lem:identity} holds for them with $Q,q$ replaced by $\bar Q,\bar q$.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:asym}]
$\overline{\mathrm{SC}}$ is linear in $x$ plus $\sum_z\tfrac{m^S_z}{2}\bar Q_z^2$ with $\bar Q$ linear in $x$, so $\overline{\mathrm{SC}}(x)-\overline{\mathrm{SC}}(x^*)=\ip{\nabla\overline{\mathrm{SC}}(x^*)}{x-x^*}+\sum_z\tfrac{m^S_z}{2}(\bar Q_z-\bar Q^*_z)^2$, and the inner product is non-negative by optimality of $x^*$. Hence $\Delta:=\overline{\mathrm{SC}}(x^u)-\overline{\mathrm{SC}}(x^*)\ge\sum_z\tfrac{m^S_z}{2}(\Delta\bar Q_z)^2$. By the identity with $Q^\circ=\bar Q^*$, $v=0$: $\overline\Phi_u(x)=\overline{\mathrm{SC}}(x)-\sum_z\tfrac{\kappa_z}{2}(\bar Q_z-\bar Q^*_z)^2+L(x)+\text{const}$. Since $x^u$ minimises $\overline\Phi_u$, $\Delta\le\sum_z\tfrac{\kappa_z}{2}(\Delta\bar Q_z)^2+L(x^*)-L(x^u)$. Combining, $\sum_z\tfrac{b_z+\kappa_z}{2}(\Delta\bar Q_z)^2\le L(x^*)-L(x^u)$; then $\sum_z\tfrac{\kappa_z}{2}(\Delta\bar Q_z)^2\le\varrho[L(x^*)-L(x^u)]$ and $\Delta\le(1+\varrho)[L(x^*)-L(x^u)]$. Finally $|L(x^*)-L(x^u)|\le\sum_{i,z}\tfrac{m_z}{2}|d_{iz}-\bar d_z|\,|\bar q^*_{iz}-\bar q^u_{iz}|\le E$ because $\bar q_{iz}\in[0,d_{iz}]$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:convex}]
With $\bar d=d$, $L$ vanishes on the feasible set, so $E=0$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:asym} and every minimiser of $\overline\Phi_u$ has aggregate $\bar Q^*$ and social cost $\overline{\mathrm{SC}}(x^*)$. Moreover $\overline\Phi_u=\overline{\mathrm{SC}}-\sum_z\tfrac{\kappa_z}{2}(\bar Q_z-\bar Q^*_z)^2+\text{const}$; the subtracted term has zero gradient at $x^*$, so $x^*$ satisfies the first-order optimality condition of the convex function $\overline\Phi_u$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:cut}]
(a) $J^u_i(p)=J^0_i(p)+\ip{u}{q_i(p)}$, so constraint $(i,p)$ reads $J^u_i(p)\ge J^u_i(\sigma^*_i)$; all of carrier $i$'s constraints hold iff $\min_pJ^u_i(p)\ge J^u_i(\sigma^*_i)$, and the violation $J^u_i(\sigma^*_i)-J^u_i(p)$ is maximised by a best response. (b) If a round adds a cut $(i,b_i)$, the current $u$ violates it and satisfies every earlier cut (it is LP-feasible), so the cut is new; there are $\sum_i(|\mathcal P_i|-1)$ non-trivial constraints. Every cut is a valid constraint of $P(\sigma^*)$, so an infeasible LP shows that $P(\sigma^*)$ is empty.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:opt}]
$\sigma$ is an equilibrium under $u$ iff $u\in P(\sigma)$, so both descriptions of $V^\star$ range over the same pairs $(u,\sigma)$. The inequality is an inclusion of feasible sets. If the loop ends with $u\in P(\sigma^*)$, the leader attains $\mathrm{SC}(\sigma^*)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:fallback}]
Best-response dynamics strictly decreases $\Phi_u$ on a finite set, so it ends at a pure equilibrium with $\Phi_u(\sigma^u)\le\Phi_u(\sigma^*)$. Substitute the identity with $Q^\circ=Q^*$ on both sides; the constants cancel and the congestion term vanishes at $\sigma^*$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:calls}]
Count the calls of the three phases; Theorem~\ref{thm:cut} bounds the rounds, and each improvement of the fallback dynamics costs at most $N$ calls.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:credal}]
By Lemma~\ref{lem:gamma}, carrier $i$'s $\Gamma$-maximin payoff is its precise payoff minus $\delta\ip{a_i(p)}{Mw}$ (traffic already enters the route loss at its upper expectation). With $a_i=(q_i,rq_i)$, $\delta\ip{a_i(p)}{Mw}=\ip{\hat s(\delta)}{q_i(p)}$, so the $\Gamma$-maximin cost under $u$ is the precise cost under $u+\hat s(\delta)$, and $u$ implements $\sigma^*$ iff $u+\hat s(\delta)\in P(\sigma^*)$. The social cost does not contain the surcharge. The last claim is Lemma~\ref{lem:nesting}: an equilibrium of the precise game lies in $\ICNE_C(\delta)$ for $C\in\{E,M,ID\}$ and every $\delta$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:uniform}]
The first claim is Theorem~\ref{thm:convex} with a uniform tangent charge. For the second, $Q^{\rm full}_z=n_zd_z$ with equal lots, and without congestion ($m_z\approx b_z$ when $\kappa_z\ll b_z$) $u_z=-\tfrac{b_z}{2}d_z=-\tfrac{\alpha_z}{4n_z}=-0.375\,\mathrm{fee}/n_z$. The last claim is Proposition~\ref{prop:instr}.
\end{proof}

\section{Proofs for aggregate-response pricing}\label{app:arp}

Notation as in Section~\ref{sec:dip}: $q_{iz}^2=d_{iz}q_{iz}$, $\kappa_z=g_zr^2$, $m_z=b_z+\kappa_z$, $m^S_z=b_z+2\kappa_z$, and $\Phi_u$ the exact potential of the game under the per-delivery-equivalent charge $u$. For a reference $\bar d\in\R^Z$, $L_{\bar d}(\sigma)=\sum_{i,z}\tfrac{m_z}{2}(d_{iz}-\bar d_z)q_{iz}$ and $E_{\bar d}=\sum_{i,z}\tfrac{m_z}{2}|d_{iz}-\bar d_z|d_{iz}$. The convexified potential $\overline\Phi_u$ and social cost $\overline{\mathrm{SC}}$ are those of \ref{app:dip}. The proofs are taken from \texttt{theory/new\_algorithm\_D/theory.tex}.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:F}
$G$ is convex and piecewise linear on $\cQ$; $F$ is proper, closed and $1$-strongly convex in $\|\cdot\|_m$, i.e.\ $F-\tfrac12\|\cdot\|_m^2$ is convex. Moreover $\min_{x\in\cX}\overline\Phi_u(x)=\min_Q\{F(Q)+\ip uQ\}$ and $\min_{x\in\cX}[\overline{\mathrm{SC}}(x)+L_{\bar d}(x)]=\min_Q\{F(Q)+g(Q)\}$ with $g(Q)=\sum_z[\tfrac{\kappa_z}{2}Q_z^2-\tfrac{m_z}{2}\bar d_zQ_z]$, and in both cases $x$ is a minimiser on the left if and only if $\bar Q(x)$ is a minimiser on the right and $x$ attains the minimum defining $G$ at $Q=\bar Q(x)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
$G$ is the optimal value of a linear program in $x$ whose right-hand side $Q$ enters through $\bar Q(x)=Q$; such a value function is convex and piecewise linear on the set of right-hand sides for which the program is feasible, which is $\cQ$. The quadratic part of $F$ is $\tfrac12\|Q\|_m^2$ and the rest is convex, so $F-\tfrac12\|\cdot\|_m^2$ is convex; $F$ is closed because $G$ is continuous on the closed set $\cQ$. For the identities, write $\tfrac{m_z}{2}d_{iz}=\tfrac{m_z}{2}(d_{iz}-\bar d_z)+\tfrac{m_z}{2}\bar d_z$ in $\overline\Phi_u$ and $\tfrac{m^S_z}{2}=\tfrac{m_z}{2}+\tfrac{\kappa_z}{2}$ in $\overline{\mathrm{SC}}+L_{\bar d}$; in both the objective depends on $x$ only through $\bar Q(x)$ plus the quantity minimised in the definition of $G$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:resolvent}]
By Lemma~\ref{lem:F}, $F+\ip u\cdot$ is strongly convex on the compact set $\cQ$, so it has a unique minimiser $Q(u)$, and every convexified equilibrium has $\bar Q(x)=Q(u)$. For closed proper strongly convex $F$ the conjugate $F^*$ is finite (the domain is bounded) and differentiable with $\nabla F^*(y)=\arg\max_Q\{\ip yQ-F(Q)\}$ \citep[Theorem 26.3]{Rockafellar1970}; with $y=-u$ this is $Q(u)$. The optimality conditions read $-u_k\in\partial F(Q_k)$; strong convexity gives $\ip{(-u_1)-(-u_2)}{Q_1-Q_2}\ge\|Q_1-Q_2\|_m^2$, and by Cauchy--Schwarz in the $m$-inner product $\ip{u_2-u_1}{Q_1-Q_2}\le\|u_2-u_1\|_{m^{-1}}\|Q_1-Q_2\|_m$, which gives the Lipschitz bound.

Let $D(u)=F^*(-u)+g^*(u)$ with $g^*(u)=\sum_z(u_z+\tfrac{m_z}{2}\bar d_z)^2/(2\kappa_z)$ (maximise $\ip uQ-g(Q)$ coordinatewise). $D$ is differentiable with $\nabla D(u)=-Q(u)+\kappa^{-1}\odot(u+\tfrac m2\bar d)$, which is observable, and strongly convex because $g^*$ has Hessian $\mathrm{diag}(1/\kappa)$. Fenchel duality \citep[Corollary 31.2.1]{Rockafellar1970}, with attainment because $g$ is finite and continuous, gives $\min_Q\{F+g\}=-\min_uD(u)$. The minimiser $u^*$ is unique and characterised by $\nabla D(u^*)=0$, i.e.\ $u^*=\kappa\odot Q(u^*)-\tfrac m2\odot\bar d=:T(u^*)$; any fixed point of $T$ is such a zero. At the saddle point the primal minimiser is $Q(u^*)$, which minimises $F+g$, i.e.\ $\overline{\mathrm{SC}}+L_{\bar d}$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:F}. For welfare, let $x^*$ minimise $\overline{\mathrm{SC}}$ and $x^\infty$ be a convexified equilibrium under $u^*$; then $\overline{\mathrm{SC}}(x^\infty)-\overline{\mathrm{SC}}(x^*)\le L_{\bar d}(x^*)-L_{\bar d}(x^\infty)\le\sum_{i,z}\tfrac{m_z}{2}|d_{iz}-\bar d_z||\bar q^*_{iz}-\bar q^\infty_{iz}|\le E_{\bar d}$, because $\bar q_{iz}\in[0,d_{iz}]$; with symmetric lots $L_{\bar d}\equiv0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:rate}]
Let $K=\mathrm{diag}(\kappa)$ and equip $\R^Z$ with $\ip\cdot\cdot_{\kappa^{-1}}$. The gradient of $D$ in this geometry is $K\nabla D(u)=u+\tfrac m2\bar d-\kappa\odot Q(u)$, so the rule~\eqref{eq:rule} is $u^{t+1}=u^t-\theta K\nabla D(u^t)$, gradient descent with step $\theta$. Since $\kappa_z\le\varrho m_z$ and $1/m_z\le\varrho/\kappa_z$ on active zones, the Lipschitz bound of Theorem~\ref{thm:resolvent} gives $\|Q(u_1)-Q(u_2)\|_\kappa\le\sqrt\varrho\|Q(u_1)-Q(u_2)\|_m\le\sqrt\varrho\|u_1-u_2\|_{m^{-1}}\le\varrho\|u_1-u_2\|_{\kappa^{-1}}$. Hence $u\mapsto\kappa\odot Q(u)$ is $\varrho$-Lipschitz from $\|\cdot\|_{\kappa^{-1}}$ to itself (note $\|\kappa\odot v\|_{\kappa^{-1}}=\|v\|_\kappa$), $K\nabla D$ is $(1+\varrho)$-Lipschitz, and $D$ is strongly convex with modulus $1$ in this geometry. (a) $T$ is $\varrho$-Lipschitz and $u^*=T(u^*)$. (b) For a $\mu$-strongly convex function with $L$-Lipschitz gradient, gradient descent with step $\theta\le2/(\mu+L)$ satisfies $\|u^{t+1}-u^*\|^2\le(1-\tfrac{2\theta\mu L}{\mu+L})\|u^t-u^*\|^2$ \citep[Theorem 2.1.15]{Nesterov2004}; the proof uses only an inequality valid in any inner-product space. With $\mu=1$, $L=1+\varrho$ and $\theta=2/(2+\varrho)$ the factor is $1-4(1+\varrho)/(2+\varrho)^2=\varrho^2/(2+\varrho)^2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:pure}]
(a) Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:identity} with $Q^\circ=Q(\sigma)$. Since $\sigma$ is an equilibrium, $\Phi_u(\sigma)\le\Phi_u(\sigma')$ \citep{Monderer1996}. The term $\sum_z\tfrac{\kappa_z}{2}(Q_z-Q^\circ_z)^2$ vanishes at $\sigma$ and equals $\sum_z\tfrac{\kappa_z}{2}\Delta Q_z^2$ at $\sigma'$; rearranging the identity for $\sigma$ and $\sigma'$ gives the inequality. The last bound uses $|q_{iz}(p)-q_{iz}(\sigma_i)|\le d_{iz}$.

(b) $u^{t+1}=\kappa\odot Q^t-\tfrac m2\bar d=T(u^t)+\kappa\odot e^t$ and $u^*=T(u^*)$, so the first inequality follows from Theorem~\ref{thm:rate}(a); iterate for the limsup. For the gap bound let $h=F+\ip{u^t}\cdot$, which is $1$-strongly convex in $\|\cdot\|_m$ and minimised at $Q(u^t)$, so $h(Q)\ge h(Q(u^t))+\tfrac12\|Q-Q(u^t)\|_m^2$. A pure profile is a point of $\cX$ with $\bar Q=Q^t$, so $\overline\Phi_{u^t}(\sigma^t)\ge h(Q^t)$ while $\min\overline\Phi_{u^t}=h(Q(u^t))$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:F}); with $\Phi_{u^t}(\sigma^t)=\overline\Phi_{u^t}(\sigma^t)$ at a pure profile, $\eta_t\ge\tfrac12\|e^t\|_m^2$ and $\|e^t\|_\kappa\le\sqrt\varrho\|e^t\|_m\le\sqrt{2\varrho\eta_t}$.

(c) By Theorem~\ref{thm:resolvent}, $Q=Q(u)$ iff $-u\in\partial F(Q)$. $F$ is polyhedral plus a quadratic on the polytope $\cQ$, so $\partial F(Q)\ne\emptyset$ on $\cQ$ and the graph of the response map is the graph of $-\partial F$ on $\cQ$. A closed proper convex function is determined by its subdifferential up to an additive constant \citep[Theorem 24.9]{Rockafellar1970}. The convexified optimum minimises $F+g$ with $g$ known, and $u^*$ is the unique zero of $\nabla D$; both are unchanged by a constant shift of $F$. Example~\ref{ex:ident} shows that the pure optimum is not determined.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}\label{ex:ident}
One zone, $\kappa=0$, slope $b=m$, intercept $\alpha$, reference $\bar d=d$. Instance A: two carriers, each with lot $d$ and plans ``stay out'' ($R=0$) and ``serve'' ($R=r$). Instance B: one carrier with lot $2d$ and plans ``stay out'' ($R=0$) and ``serve'' ($R=2r-md^2$), with $r>md^2/2$. In both, $\cQ=[0,2d]$ and $G$ is linear with $G_A(Q)=G_B(Q)=\tfrac rdQ$, so $F_A=F_B$ and the response maps coincide. The pure social costs are $\mathrm{SC}_A(Q)=\tfrac rdQ-\alpha Q+\tfrac m2Q^2$ on $Q\in\{0,d,2d\}$ and $\mathrm{SC}_B(Q)=(2r-md^2)\mathbf 1[Q=2d]-\alpha Q+\tfrac m2Q^2$ on $\{0,2d\}$. With $\alpha=\tfrac rd+md$, $Q=d$ is the unique pure optimum of A with value $-\tfrac m2d^2$, while $Q=2d$ is the unique pure optimum of B with value $-md^2$. The optima differ in aggregate and value although nothing observable in the convexified game differs.
\end{example}

\begin{lemma}[Lot estimates]\label{lem:lots}
(a) If lots are symmetric within zones, $Q_z/n_z=\bar d_z$ in every served zone. (b) If between two observations $n_z$ changes by exactly one, $|Q^t_z-Q^{t-1}_z|$ is the lot of the carrier that entered or left; if $n_z=1$, $Q_z$ is the lot of the serving carrier. (c) For a zone never served the observation carries no information about its lots; the bound of Theorem~\ref{thm:resolvent} holds for any reference vector, with $E_{\bar d}$ for the vector actually used.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
(a) $Q_z=n_z\bar d_z$. (b) The other lots in $z$ are unchanged, so the difference of the sums is the changed term. (c) Two instances that differ only in the lots of a never-served zone are indistinguishable; the bound was shown for an arbitrary reference vector.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:arpcredal}]
By Lemma~\ref{lem:gamma}, the $\Gamma$-maximin cost gains the surcharge
\[\delta\ip{a_i(p)}{Mw}=\ip{\hat s(\delta)}{q_i(p)},\]
with $\hat s_z(\delta)=\delta\hat w_z$, $\hat w_z=b_zw_{z,Q}+g_zr\,w_{z,X}$, linear in deliveries; so the response to $u$ is the precise response to $u+\hat s$, the map $u\mapsto T(u+\hat s)$ is $\varrho$-Lipschitz and the rule contracts as before, with a fixed point at which $u+\hat s$ is a zero of the dual gradient of $\min_QF+g+\ip{\hat s}{Q}$. For the loss, let $Q_0$ and $Q_\delta$ minimise $H=F+g$ and $H+\ip{\hat s}\cdot$, both $1$-strongly convex in $\|\cdot\|_m$. Strong convexity at $Q_\delta$ gives $H(Q_\delta)-H(Q_0)\le\ip{\hat s}{Q_0-Q_\delta}-\tfrac12\|Q_0-Q_\delta\|_m^2\le\|\hat s\|_{m^{-1}}\|Q_0-Q_\delta\|_m-\tfrac12\|Q_0-Q_\delta\|_m^2\le\tfrac12\|\hat s\|_{m^{-1}}^2$.

For E-admissibility, write $\Delta_z=u_z-u^*_z$ for a posted charge $u$ and a pure equilibrium $\sigma^*$ under $u^*$ with precise beliefs. Carrier $i$'s cost of plan $p$ at charge $u$ and belief $(\mu_Q,\mu_X)$ about rivals is $R_i(p)+\sum_zq_{iz}(p)(-\alpha_z+m_zd_{iz}+b_z\mu_{Q,z}+g_zr\mu_{X,z}+u_z)$. Choose $\mu_{Q,z}=Q_{-i,z}-\Delta_zw_{z,Q}/\hat w_z$ and $\mu_{X,z}=X_{-i,z}-\Delta_zw_{z,X}/\hat w_z$. Then $b_z\mu_{Q,z}+g_zr\mu_{X,z}+u_z=b_zQ_{-i,z}+g_zrX_{-i,z}+u^*_z$, so every plan's cost at this belief and charge $u$ equals its cost at the realised aggregate and charge $u^*$, where $\sigma^*_i$ is optimal. The belief lies in the box iff $|\Delta_z|\le\delta\hat w_z=\hat s_z(\delta)$, and it is nonnegative when $\Delta_z\le0$, or when $Q_{-i,z}\ge\Delta_zw_{z,Q}/\hat w_z$ and $rQ_{-i,z}\ge\Delta_zw_{z,X}/\hat w_z$ for $\Delta_z>0$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:reduction}, $\sigma^*_i$ is E-admissible. The round count follows from Theorem~\ref{thm:rate}(a) and $|u_z-u^*_z|\le\sqrt{\kappa_z}\|u-u^*\|_{\kappa^{-1}}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:mct}]
(a) $J^T_i(p;\sigma_{-i})=R_i(p)+\sum_zq_{iz}(-\alpha_z+m_zd_{iz}+m_zQ_{-i,z}+\kappa_zQ_{-i,z}-\tfrac{b_z}{2}d_{iz})=R_i(p)+\sum_zq_{iz}(-\alpha_z+\tfrac{m^S_z}{2}d_{iz}+m^S_zQ_{-i,z})$, using $q_{iz}^2=d_{iz}q_{iz}$, $m_z-\tfrac{b_z}{2}=\tfrac{m^S_z}{2}$ and $m_z+\kappa_z=m^S_z$. On the other side $\mathrm{SC}(p,\sigma_{-i})-\mathrm{SC}(\emptyset_i,\sigma_{-i})=R_i(p)+\sum_z[-\alpha_zq_{iz}+\tfrac{m^S_z}{2}((q_{iz}+Q_{-i,z})^2-Q_{-i,z}^2)]$, which is the same expression. The right side differs from $\mathrm{SC}(p,\sigma_{-i})$ by a term independent of $p$, which is the definition of an exact potential. (b) Monderer--Shapley for the potential $\mathrm{SC}$ on a finite profile set. (c) Immediate from the first expression in (a).
\end{proof}
