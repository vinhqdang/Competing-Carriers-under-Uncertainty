## 1. Errors and omissions found first (fix before anything else)

1. **Wrong citation key for best-response cuts.** `Duguet2025` in paper/references.bib is "Minimal regret Walras equilibria for combinatorial markets" (arXiv 2511.09021, Duguet-Harks-Schmidt-Schwarz). main.tex line 105 cites it together with `Dragotto2023` for "best-response cuts are used for equilibria of integer programming games". That sentence needs the branch-and-cut paper (arXiv 2506.02520, Duguet-Harks-Schmidt-Schwarz), whose abstract says: "For NEPs, we show that suitable cuts always exist via best-response inequalities and prove that our B&C method terminates in finite time whenever the set of best-response sets is finite." Line 1001 (ZMC) uses `Duguet2025` correctly for the Walras paper. The author list "Duguet-Carvalho-Dragotto-Ngueveu 2025" exists as a different paper: "Computing approximate Nash equilibria for integer programming games", Optimization Letters 20(1):231-255 (Crossref; arXiv 2402.04250). Its abstract is about piecewise-linear approximation of nonlinear payoffs, not best-response cuts. Add `DuguetHarks2025BC`; optionally `Duguet2025IPG`, `Carvalho2025FTO`.
2. **Missing the closest neighbour of the credal-set construction.** Lanzetti et al. 2025 (arXiv 2507.15325), Definition 1: agent i's strategy must maximise the *minimum* over an ambiguity ball B^eps_i(p-bar_{-i}) *centred at the equilibrium play of the others* ("endogenously-generated uncertainty"). Our assumption A4 (credal set centred on the realised aggregate) is the same device. Feik et al. 2026 is the aggregative specialisation of this paper. Not in either .bib file. Add `Lanzetti2025`.
3. **Misattribution in the ARP prior-art sentence.** main.tex lines 105 and 381 and the "not new" paragraph say Maheshwari2022 and Maheshwari2026 treat "atomic Cournot games with continuous strategies". Atomic Cournot competition is Section 4.2 of the CDC paper (arXiv 2204.05507, Prop. 4.2, convergence if lambda > delta). The TAC paper (arXiv 2405.16716 v3) treats networked atomic aggregative games and nonatomic routing; the word Cournot occurs once. Say so.
4. **Masiha et al. is nonatomic.** Line 105 says the method covers "freight carriers with hard tour problems". True only as an Appendix C example: followers are a nonatomic population with load vectors y(z) in [0,1]^n and a Wardrop equilibrium; there is no atomic carrier game and no pure-equilibrium issue. Add "nonatomic".

## 2. Paper-by-paper comparison

### (1) Liu-Oudjane-Wan, MOR 48(3) 2023, arXiv 2011.12604
Model: cost f_i(x_i, x_-i) = theta_i(x_i, (1/n) sum_j A_j x_j), x_i in compact, possibly nonconvex X_i, Assumption 1 (l.s.c. in x_i, (H, gamma)-Holder in the aggregate). The aggregate includes player i's own term. Results: **Prop. 2.3** (the convexified game has a PNE); **Lemma 5.5/5.6** (every point of a generator is a best reply to the convexified rivals); **Prop. 2.4** ("the non-convex game admits an eps-PNE, where eps = 2H(((sqrt q + 1) M Delta)/n)^gamma"); **Thm 2.7** (construction by x* in argmin ||sum A_i x~_i - sum A_i x_i||^2 over generators, eps~ = eps + eta + same term); **Prop. 2.8 / Lemma 5.7** (independent randomisation over the generator, E||sum A_i x~_i - sum A_i X_i|| <= sqrt(n) M Delta, giving an eps-mixed equilibrium); **Thm 3.3** (O(n^3) iterations for an O(1/n)-PNE in congestion games). Nothing on beliefs, ambiguity or exact equilibria under a relaxation.

Relation to our claims:
- Cor. 6.2 and Thm 6.1(b) terms two and three, (d) the LP-vertex and sampling constructions: **contained** in Prop. 2.4, Thm 2.7, Prop. 2.8 specialised to affine coupling. The own-term "+1" is LOW's "(sqrt q + 1)". Two cosmetic differences, neither a result: our Hoeffding/union-bound step gives an l-infinity, high-probability statement with a ln(2d) factor, where LOW give an l2 expectation bound; and our "d+1" deterministic term is weaker than LOW's sqrt(q)+1 in their norm (the norms differ, so this is not a clean comparison).
- Thm 6.1(a) (absorption, exact): **not in LOW**, but it follows in two lines from their Lemma 5.6 plus the definition of E-admissibility with a box containing the anchor.
- Thm 6.1(b) first term max_i max_k sum_{j != i} r_jk / w_k: a trivial support-selection bound; no counterpart in LOW because LOW target large n.
- Prop. 7.1 and 7.2 (tightness): LOW prove no lower bounds, so these are not contained. But see risk 3 in Section 6: what is attained is the N-dependent term at N = 2.
- Narrowest defensible statement: *LOW23's N-independent rounding, specialised to affine coupling, converts an approximate pure equilibrium into an exact pure equilibrium for imprecise-probability criteria, with the width equal to the rounding error; the threshold has an explicit form and the support-selection term is attained at N = 2.*

### (2) Feik-Lanzetti-Bolognani-Dörfler-Paccagnan, arXiv 2604.23669 (and its parent, 2507.15325)
Feik et al.: convex aggregative game (Assumption 1: X^i compact convex, J^i convex in x^i, continuous), sigma(x) any continuous aggregate. **Definition 1**: x_W is a pure strategically robust Wardrop equilibrium if x^i in argmin_{x^i} sup_{mu in B_eps(delta_sigma(x_W))} E[J^i(x^i, sigma)], B_eps a Wasserstein ball around the Dirac at the realised aggregate. **Theorem 1** (existence for every eps >= 0, via Berge and Kakutani, "under the very same assumptions required for a standard Wardrop equilibrium"). Proposition 1 gives a finite-dimensional convex reformulation. Section III is an EV-charging experiment with a price-of-anarchy plot (a "coordination via robustification" effect). The text states no theorem about price of anarchy, nonconvex or discrete sets, or E-admissibility.
Lanzetti et al. 2025: Definition 1 above for mixed strategies; **Thm 1** (existence under finite actions, Assumption 1.A, with hemicontinuous ambiguity sets), **Thm 3** (pure equilibria exist for concave games), experiments on bimatrix and congestion games. Their equilibria in finite games are *mixed*; no pure existence result for nonconvex or finite sets.
Relation: **merely related.** Same centring device; different criterion (worst case, a convex operation, versus E-admissibility); different regime (convex or mixed, versus finite nonconvex plan sets with pure answers). Our D1 result (Prop. D1, existence at every width) parallels Feik Thm 1 in spirit only. Our Lemma 3.6 and Remark "Gamma-maximin" show that the worst-case criterion cannot give existence under D2, which is consistent with their need for convexity. We should say so rather than only "convex aggregative games with an optimal-transport ambiguity ball".
Narrowest claim: *to our knowledge, no published equilibrium notion with a belief set centred on the realised aggregate gives pure equilibria in finite, nonconvex aggregative games; existing results (Lanzetti 2025, Feik 2026) are for convex games or mixed strategies and use worst-case criteria.* Say "to our knowledge", and note that I read Feik et al. in full but Lanzetti et al. only for definitions and the existence theorems.

### (3) Beauchêne, Theory and Decision 80(2):245-269 (2016), doi 10.1007/s11238-015-9502-3 (Crossref/RePEc verified)
Abstract only. It "examines three equilibrium solutions: Nash equilibrium ...; Crawford equilibrium in beliefs; and pure equilibrium in beliefs, where equilibrium strategies are mixtures of best responses restricted to pure actions", with "necessary and sufficient conditions for existence and equivalence", tested against the principle of indifference and the reduction principle. Reference list (Crossref) includes Crawford 1990, Dow-Werlang 1994, Eichberger-Kelsey 2000, Gilboa-Schmeidler 1989, Dekel-Safra-Segal 1991. Nothing visible about aggregation, width bounds or Shapley-Folkman. **Conclusion I can support:** it is the nearest *conceptual* ancestor of "pure equilibria become possible under ambiguity", for two-player normal-form games with non-expected-utility preferences. I cannot say what its existence conditions are. The paper must not call Thm 6.1 "unprecedented" relative to it. The current wording ("as pure equilibrium in beliefs with ambiguous payoffs") is safe. The statement that needs full text is whether Beauchêne's existence condition can be read as a width threshold. Obtain via interlibrary loan before submission.

### (4) Ghosal-Ho-Wiesemann (OR 72(2):425-443, 2024) and Ghosal-Wiesemann (OR 68(3):716-732, 2020)
GHW24 (preprint read): CVRP with uncertain **demand**; travel times are assumed known (Section 1: "make the simplifying assumption that the travel times are known"). The ambiguity set (7) is rectangular conditional on a scenario w. **Theorem 4** (credited there to Long et al. 2020): for convex f and x >= 0, sup over P of E[f(x^T q)] is attained by a W(2n+1)-point distribution that does not depend on f or x. The proof moves mass to joins and meets of unordered pairs, using supermodularity of q -> f(x^T q), until the support is a chain; that is exactly the comonotone worst case under unknown dependence. Branch-and-cut over a set-function demand estimator (Theorem 1, Props 3-5). Ghosal-Wiesemann 2020: demand under moment ambiguity, chance constraint, branch-and-cut; I read only abstracts.
Relation to BRL: Thm 2.1 (comonotone upper prevision and budget decomposition) is **contained** in Meilijson-Nádas plus the stop-loss identity for comonotone sums; our text already says so. The *dependence-robust comonotone reduction for route cost* is also contained in GHW24 Thm 4 / Long-Qi-Zhang 2024 / Tchen 1980 (supermodular functions and given marginals). Our current sentence "a comonotone reduction for ambiguous demand is known in routing" is accurate; add "and the supermodular argument is general (Tchen 1980; Long et al. 2024)". The difference that remains is that BRL's quantity is the *arc travel time* summed along a path inside a pricing subproblem. Theorem 2.5 is, mechanically, the exchange of two minimisations (the stop-loss form is a minimum, and so is the pricing problem), which a referee will call immediate.

### (5) Masiha-Elahi-Kiyavash-Thiran, arXiv 2602.23277
Nonatomic population on combinatorial strategies; Wardrop equilibrium as minimiser of a convex potential f(theta, y) over C (Assumptions 2.1, 2.4, 2.5); leader objective Phi(theta) = F(theta, y*(theta)) nonsmooth. ZO-Stackelberg: inner Frank-Wolfe with an exact or subsampled LMO, outer two-point zeroth-order update of theta. **Thm 5.4** (O(1/(kappa_m T)) Frank-Wolfe rate with subsampling), **Thm 5.5** (convergence to generalised Goldstein stationary points). Appendix C gives a Steiner-cycle freight example. No atomic game, no pure-equilibrium existence issue, no uncertainty, no target implementation. It needs an equilibrium (approximate) solve per outer step; our main.tex sentence says so correctly. Relation to ARP: **related**. Both avoid differentiating through equilibria; ARP uses observed aggregates only and a closed-form fixed-point map, with rates that do not depend on the instance; Masiha's rates depend on constants of Assumptions 2.4-2.5.

### (7) Maheshwari-Kulkarni-Wu-Sastry, arXiv 2204.05507 (CDC 2022) and 2405.16716 (TAC 2026)
Mechanism: incentive update p_{k+1} = (1 - beta_k) p_k + beta_k e(x_k), where the externality e_i is the difference between player i's marginal cost and the society's; per-player incentives p_i; strategies in an interval of R; two-timescale stochastic approximation (vanishing steps). **Prop. 3.1**: the fixed-point set is non-empty and every fixed point is socially optimal (Brouwer). **Prop. 3.2**: uniqueness conditions. **Thm 3.3** (CDC): convergence under (C1) or (C2), a monotonicity or Lyapunov condition. **Prop. 4.2**: atomic Cournot, p-dagger = e(x*(p-dagger)) is the unique optimal incentive, convergence if lambda > delta. In the TAC version the corresponding results are Prop. 3.1 and 3.3 and Section 4 treats networked aggregative games and nonatomic routing. The planner needs gradients (atomic) or values of costs at the current profile.
Relation to our claims:
- ARP **fixed point = tangent charge** (Thm 3(prox) / Fixed point, and the marginal-tariff theorem): the statement "a fixed point of the externality update implements a social optimum" is **contained** in their Prop. 3.1 for continuous strategies. Ours is the lumpy, finite-plan (convexified) analogue with the lot-asymmetry error E.
- ARP **contraction rate** (theory D Thm 6, paper thm:rate, rate varrho = max kappa/m with 1-step equilibrium): their results are asymptotic with vanishing steps. A geometric rate with a one-timescale iteration where each step best-responds to the posted charge is a different, quantitative statement. A referee may note that for a strongly convex F, gradient descent on the Fenchel dual with condition number bounded by kappa/m is a textbook rate; the contribution is the identification (Thm 3: equilibrium response is a proximal map of the convexified value function), and the instance-free constant. State it that way.
- Flat zone charges versus per-player incentives and aggregate-only observation versus gradients: genuine differences, but modest ones.

### (8) Yang-Meng-Lee 2004 and the trial-and-error toll literature
Yang-Meng-Lee (TR-B 38(6):477-493): "A set of link tolls is tried, the resulting aggregate link flows are observed, and new tolls are computed for the next trial", successive averages, convergence proof, nonatomic user equilibrium, demand function unknown (abstract). Han-Yang 2009 (TR-E 45(1):159-171): step sizes need not be square summable. Yang-Xu-He-Meng 2010 (TR-C 18(2):157-175): unknown demand and cost. Wang-Yang 2012 (TR-B 46(9):1085-1096): bisection variant and a repair of a non-convergence of an earlier bisection. Ye-Yang-Tan 2015 (TR-B 81:794-807): trial-and-error under day-to-day flow dynamics, flows out of equilibrium. All checked at abstract level only.
Relation: ARP's *procedure* (post a charge, observe aggregates, update) is **contained** in this literature; so is the marginal-cost tariff (their fixed point is the marginal-cost toll). What these papers do not have, as far as the abstracts show, is an atomic lumpy game, NP-hard best responses, a rate with no dependence on instance size, and a statement of what the aggregate observations identify (our Thm "What aggregate observations identify"). Only YMLee 2004 is cited now; add Han-Yang 2009 and Ye-Yang-Tan 2015 at least.

### (9) Bhaskar-Ligett-Schulman-Swamy (GEB 118:533-569 (2019), arXiv 1408.1429)
**Thm 3.2**: "Given a target acyclic multicommodity flow f* and query access to Gamma*, we can compute tolls that enforce f* or determine that no such tolls exist, in polytime using a polynomial number of queries" (ellipsoid, nonatomic). **Thm 3.7**: the same for atomic splittable games under uniqueness of the equilibrium. Lower bounds in Section 5 (Omega(|E|) queries, Thm 5.1). Relation to DIP: DIP's goal (implement a *target* with tolls, using only oracle access) is the same; BLSS's oracle is an equilibrium-flow oracle, DIP's is a *best-response* oracle at the fixed target (one NP-hard call per carrier). Both are cutting-plane/ellipsoid in toll space with separation from the oracle. DIP Thm 7(b) (finite termination in at most 1 + sum_i(|P_i| - 1) rounds) is **subsumed in spirit** by this template and by the equilibrium-inequality idea of ZERO Regrets (below). Their lower bound makes our "N oracle calls per round, no equilibrium solve" worth stating against their poly-queries in an equilibrium oracle.

### (11) Zero Regrets and best-response cuts
Dragotto-Scatamacchia (IJOC 35(5), arXiv 2111.06382): **Prop. 1** equilibrium inequality "u_i(x~_i, x_-i) <= u_i(x_i, x_-i)" for each best response x~_i; **Thm 1** the closure from best-response inequalities describes the PNE set; a cutting-plane algorithm with finite termination, and selection of equilibria by an objective. Duguet-Harks-Schmidt-Schwarz (arXiv 2506.02520): best-response inequalities for NEPs, finite termination if the set of best-response sets is finite. DIP's constraints (carrier i weakly prefers sigma*_i to every plan, linear in the leader's charge) are the *same inequalities read in the parameter space of the leader*, and the separation is the same best-response oracle. DIP Thm 7 is therefore a **re-reading**, not a new technique. What is DIP-specific: the target is a social optimum chosen first, the implementation polytope P(sigma*) is in charge space, and the fallback loss bound (Thm 9 of theory C). Optimising a leader objective over PNEs of an IPG (the leader picks which PNE) is exactly the ZERO Regrets "selection" task; our single-level form (Prop. "Single-level form") is that with a charge variable added.

## 4. Hostile-referee edits to paper/main.tex (do not edit paper/ from here; these are for the author)

1. **Intro (iv), line ~89.** Current: "a credal absorption theorem, which gives a belief width bounded independently of the number of carriers above which a mixed equilibrium rounds to an exact pure imprecise equilibrium, and shows that the bound is attained". Problems: (a) the paper's own Remark "Novelty and regime" says that on the city instances N <= 6 and d = 2Z >= 84, so the operative bound is the N-dependent support-selection term (N-1) rho-bar; independence of N holds only for the terms that bind when N > d. (b) Prop. "support-selection bound attained" is for N = 2 and the N-dependent term; the N-uniform term is not shown attained; Prop. "Linear dependence on the number of zones" is a statement about eps, not delta_0. Replace by: "...a belief width of at most sigma_max min{N-1, d+1, 1+sqrt(d ln(2d)/2)} above which a mixed equilibrium rounds to an exact pure imprecise equilibrium (the N-free terms bind only when the number of carriers exceeds the number of coupling dimensions, which does not happen on our city instances); the term N-1 is attained for N = 2 (closely related to Liu et al., who obtain an approximate equilibrium of the precise game)."
2. **Intro (iv), same item, "bounded independently of the number of carriers" and the abstract/highlights, if repeated.** Grep for "independent" or "uniform in N" in frontmatter.tex and highlights.txt and apply the same restriction.

## Addendum: full-text reading by the author (2026-10-27)

**Beauchêne (2016), Theory and Decision 80(2):245-269**, main text pp. 245-258 read (appendix proofs and examples not read). Finite games with ambiguous state-dependent payoffs and non-expected-utility players (representation V_i over acts). Three solution concepts: Nash equilibrium (strategies are best responses), Crawford equilibrium in beliefs (mixtures of best responses over mixed strategies) and pure equilibrium in beliefs (mixtures of pure best responses). Results: CEB and PEB exist in every finite game (Kakutani, Prop. 1); NE exists for all games iff players are ambiguity averse (Azrieli-Teper); NE and CEB coincide iff ambiguity averse, PEB and CEB coincide iff ambiguity seeking (Prop. 2); the principle of indifference always holds for PEB (Prop. 3); the reduction principle holds iff players are expected-utility maximisers (Prop. 4). Beliefs about the opponents' play are accurate. Nothing on aggregative games, belief widths, rounding or complexity. Relation to Thm 6.1: the nearest concept is PEB (a mixture of pure best responses); our result gives an explicit width at which a pure profile built from the support of a mixed equilibrium is an exact equilibrium under imprecise beliefs about the rivals' aggregates. The two ambiguity notions differ (payoffs versus strategic aggregates).

**Liu-Ma-Ma (2026), EJOR, doi 10.1016/j.ejor.2026.09.028**, pp. 1-13 of 16 read. DFL-DRO-DVRP: decision-focused learning (SPO+) for a fleet of m vehicles in dynamic e-commerce routing; "samplewise adversarial" robustification inside a Wasserstein ball, explicitly not an exact Wasserstein-DRO reformulation (no equivalence or coverage guarantee claimed); multi-agent coordination as a non-cooperative game among agents (vehicles) with conflict penalties; Nash existence by Nash's theorem for finite games in mixed strategies (Thm 3.2); computed by an iterative best-response heuristic with no convergence theorem (Alg. 2); Proposition 3.4 is a conditional error accounting; evaluation on Solomon-derived instances, a Shanghai e-commerce dataset (15,847 orders), distribution shifts and 3-5 agent scenarios against ten baselines (differences from the strongest baseline not significant on the nominal e-commerce benchmark). The agents are vehicles of one setting, not competing carriers; no price or traffic coupling, no pricing leader, no imprecise probabilities, no exact routing, no pure-equilibrium theory beyond finiteness. Verdict: a neighbour in the combination of multi-agent routing, distributional robustness and learning; not a competitor to any theorem or algorithm in this paper. Cited in Related work.

### A. Ghosal-Ho-Wiesemann (GHW24)

**Model.** VRP(C): intra-route feasibility set C given by phi(1_R) <= B with phi monotone (assumptions (D) downward closed, (P) permutation invariant). Uncertainty: customer **demands** q (random, nonnegative); **travel times are known** (Section 1). Risk attached to the route load x^T q, not to cost. Ambiguity set (6): scenario w with probability s_w (known, or in a 1-norm, inf-norm, ellipsoid or entropy set); conditional on w, per-customer support [q_w, qbar_w], mean mu_w, mean absolute deviation nu_w, **dependence unrestricted** (rectangular, eq. (7)). Wasserstein-inf, KL and TV sets arise as special cases (Example 3).

**Results (numbering of the preprint read).**
- Prop 1: under (D),(P), d_low <= dbar^1 <= dbar^m (demand estimators). Thm 1: VRP(C) is equivalent to the two-index flow model 2VF(d) for every d in [d_low, dbar^m]. Prop 2: boundary cases. Thm 2: (D),(P) are tight (counterexamples, Lemmas 1-2).
- Prop 3: form (4) satisfies (P), and (D) iff phi monotone. Prop 4: packing estimator d^P equals dbar^1 for binary assignment. Prop 5: if phi convex, d^P(S) = min{I : phi(1_S/I) <= B}. Thm 3: (i) phi subadditive gives d^S <= d^P, equal if positively homogeneous; (ii) additive phi reduces to a deterministic CVRP; (iii) without subadditivity d^S <= dbar^m can fail.
- **Thm 4 (credited to Long et al. 2020)**: for convex f and x >= 0, sup over P of E f(x^T q) is attained by a W(2n+1)-point distribution that does not depend on f or x. Proof (p. 20 and Appendix A): q -> f(x^T q) is supermodular (Simchi-Levi et al. Thm 2.2.6(a)); under rectangularity, mass on unordered pairs moves to join and meet without changing marginals and with weakly larger value, until the support is a **chain** (that is, comonotone); Algorithm 1 (Appendix B, from Long et al.) builds it in O(n) steps per scenario.
- Thms 5-7 and Lemmas 4-10: worst-case expected disutility, essential riskiness index, expectile, entropic risk, requirements violation index, CVaR, service fulfilment index, underperformance index are computed from P* by bisection or trisection (O(n^2 W + nW log nW)). Prop 6, Cor 1, Prop 7, Cor 2 (ambiguous scenario probabilities, 1-norm, inf-norm, entropy, ellipsoid). Obs 1-2, Example 4, Props 8-9, Thm 8, Prop 10: chance-constrained case via a modified VaR (VaR is neither convex nor subadditive).

**What is solved and how.** Branch-and-cut on the two-index vehicle-flow formulation with capacity (rounded-capacity) inequalities whose right-hand side is a demand estimator d^S or d^P computed from the worst-case risk; RCI separation by the tabu heuristic of Augerat et al.; CPLEX 20.1.0, C++. **No pricing problem, no column generation, no labels, no dominance.** The introduction remarks that the findings "can be employed in branch-and-cut-and-price schemes" and cites robust resource-constrained shortest paths under budgeted uncertainty (Pessoa et al. 2015; Di Puglia Pugliese et al. 2019) as the pricing problems that are known to be solvable; nothing of this is done in the paper.

**Experiments.** Diaz benchmark (sets A, B, E, F, M, P, up to n = 150, Euclidean data only); four quadrant scenarios with equal probability, demands 110/100/90% of nominal by quadrant, support +-10% of nominal, MAD from a 90% Normal; capacities +20%; 12 h limit, one core. Experiment 6.1: deterministic, relaxed deterministic, stochastic and ambiguous (1-norm ball, theta = 0.1) under 90%-CVaR; roughly 75-87% of instances solved within 12 h (read off Fig. 2), with runtimes between those of the two deterministic variants; Table 1 lists every instance (several unsolved or infeasible, for example A-n80-k10 ambiguous, M-n151-k12). Experiment 6.2: A-n32-k5 under exponential disutility, vehicles 5 to 11 as risk aversion rises.

### B. Masiha-Elahi-Kiyavash-Thiran (arXiv 2602.23277)

**Model.** Nonatomic population of total mass 1 over combinatorial strategies S in a family S subset 2^[n]; load polytope C = conv{1_S}; separable strictly increasing resource costs c_i(y_i; theta); Wardrop equilibrium = minimiser y*(theta) of the convex Beckmann potential f(theta, y) over C. Leader chooses theta in a closed convex set and minimises Phi(theta) = F(theta, y*(theta)), generally nonsmooth because the active set changes.

**Results.** Prop 2.3 (Wardrop equilibrium iff potential minimiser; proof E.2). Lemma 2.6 (equilibrium map and Phi are globally Lipschitz under local quadratic growth, Assumption 2.4, and Lipschitz regularity, Assumption 2.5; constant L_{f,2}/alpha). Example 2.7 and E.1 (kinks, Omega(|S|) of them). Remarks 5.1, 5.3 (Kerdreux et al. subsampling as a special case). **Thm 5.4**: Frank-Wolfe with a subsampled linear minimisation oracle and exact line search has E[f(y_T)] - f(y*) <= 2 L D^2 / (kappa_m (T - T0 + 1)), with kappa_m the probability that the sample contains an exact minimiser (Assumption 5.2), and a mean-square distance bound under quadratic growth. **Thm 5.5**: the outer two-point zeroth-order projected iteration reaches an (epsilon, rho, eta)-generalised Goldstein stationary point of Phi, with an error term linear in the inner mean-square error eps_y; total oracle cost O(rho^-3 epsilon^-6) in the dominant regime.

**What is computed.** Inner: Frank-Wolfe (T = 3000 steps) with LMO = shortest path (Dijkstra), exact min-cost over a zero-suppressed decision diagram (ZDD), or best-of-m sampled strategies (uniform, uniform-length, harmonic-length). Outer: random-direction two-point estimator, B = 4, rho = 0.05, eta = 0.05, 500 iterations, 2B inner solves per iteration. The leader needs the follower cost functions to run the inner solver; the outer loop itself needs only values F(theta, y_T(theta)).

**Experiments.** TNTP networks: Winnipeg (49 nodes, 82 edges, 3.12e7 s-t paths, ZDD 1.36e4 nodes), Chicago-Sketch (Hamiltonian s-t paths, 2.53e6), Philadelphia (Steiner cycles through fixed terminals, 3.81e14 strategies, ZDD 1.08e6 nodes, build 302 s). Cost family c_i = d_i (1 + C y_i/(theta_i + 1)) with theta in {theta >= 0, sum theta = n}, that is, a **capacity-type allocation, not tolls**; leader objective total travel cost. Against the differentiable baseline of Sakaue-Nakamura: 20 to 1000 times faster per outer iteration, 0.3 to 1.7 GiB against 10 to 194 GiB; uniform sampling stalls in Scenario 3, length-stratified sampling matches exact ZDD at m = 1000. Appendix C lists urban tolls, multicast and "freight logistics" (Steiner cycles with a per-segment fee) as motivating templates; there is no freight experiment with tolls, no carriers with private objectives, no lots, no uncertainty.

### C. Feik-Lanzetti-Bolognani-Dorfler-Paccagnan (arXiv 2604.23669)

**Model.** N players, action sets X^i compact convex in R^n, cost J^i(x^i, sigma(x)) convex in x^i, sigma continuous aggregate (Assumption 1), sigma(X) contained in a compact set X_sigma (Assumption 2). **Definition 1**: x_W is a pure strategically robust Wardrop equilibrium at level epsilon if x^i_W minimises sup over mu in B_eps(delta_{sigma(x_W)}) of E_mu J^i(x^i, sigma_hat); B_eps is the Wasserstein-p ball (any norm) of radius epsilon around the **Dirac at the realised aggregate**; players do not move the centre when deviating (Wardrop), and epsilon = 0 gives the Wardrop equilibrium, epsilon -> infinity security strategies.

**Results.** **Thm 1** (existence of a pure SR Wardrop equilibrium for every epsilon, via Berge, Kakutani, upper hemicontinuity and convex best-response sets; the same assumptions as a standard Wardrop equilibrium). **Prop 1** (equivalent convex aggregative game with extra variables lambda^i in [0, M^i]; proof "analogous to Lanzetti et al. Prop 4.3"). **Cor 1** (explicit convex program when J^i is concave in sigma and X_sigma = {f_k(sigma) <= 0}, via DRO duality). **Example 1** (affine prices, Euclidean norm). Section II-D: proximal-best-response iteration, **no convergence analysis** ("left for future research"). Section III: EV-charging game, N = 100, n = 24; Figs 1-2 (demand profile flattens as epsilon grows), Figs 3-4 (robustness to a 1, 2, 4 kW aggregate shock), **Fig 5: price of anarchy against epsilon, about 1.33 at epsilon = 0, equal to 1 near epsilon = 1.2**, a "coordination via robustification" effect, numerical only. There is **no uniqueness result, no price-of-anarchy theorem, no finite or nonconvex strategy set, no E-admissibility, no complexity result.** Finite games enter only through mixed extensions (strategy sets are simplices), so equilibria are mixed.

### E. Verdicts on our novelty statements

1. **BRL comonotone reduction and budget decomposition (Thm 2.1).** Contained in prior work (Meilijson-Nadas, Tchen, Dhaene et al.; GHW24 Thm 4 for the supermodular argument). We claim nothing there, and the paper says so. Hostile reading: GHW24 Thm 4 applies as stated with q the arc-time vector and x the route incidence vector. The honest remaining novelty is the use of the decomposition inside pricing.
2. **BRL budget-resource reformulation (Thm 2.5) and completion-window dominance (Thm "Validity and exactness").** Not in GHW24, which has no pricing, resources or labels. Thm 2.5 is the exchange of two minimisations after Thm 2.1; a referee will see it as immediate, and the claim should stay at "H resources independent of L". The window is the substantive element; it stays "unverified against resource-extension functions and function-valued labels (Irnich 2008, Dabia et al. 2013), not read". Budgeted-uncertainty robust RCSP pricing (Pessoa et al., Pugliese et al.) is cited by GHW as the known tractable case and is the most likely neighbour for a reviewer; not read.
3. **DIP.** Masiha et al. do not threaten it: no implementation problem, no best-response separation, no finite termination. The earlier verdict (re-reading of equilibrium inequalities in charge space) stands.
4. **ARP.** Masiha et al. do not contain the prox identification or the contraction. Their Lemma 2.6 contains generic Lipschitz stability. The claim "leader pricing without equilibrium search" is accurate against Masiha for DIP; for ARP, what is saved is leader computation, not market settlement (one settling per update, as in trial-and-error pricing), which the text already states.
5. **Credal absorption (Thm 6.1).** Not contained in Feik/Lanzetti: those papers have no finite plan sets, no E-admissibility, no rounding. Liu et al. remains the source of the quantitative content (earlier verdict unchanged). Feik/Lanzetti are the source of the **centring device**; the contribution list now says so.
6. **Gamma-maximin lemma and remark.** The lemma is an instance of the Feik/Lanzetti worst-case reformulation for affine payoffs; the paper now says so. The remark (no absorption, possibly empty under D2) is consistent with their convexity requirement and is not contradicted by their existence theorem, which needs convex strategy sets or mixed strategies.

**Threats to a theorem.** None found. No statement of ours is contradicted by any of the three papers. The one result that is weaker than the paper implied is the Gamma-maximin lemma (an instance, not a new reformulation).
